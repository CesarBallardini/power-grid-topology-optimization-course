    Chapter 24 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 24. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: genome_mutation.py (the genotype of the worked example), line_switching.py (IEEE 30, the fitness and the brute force), switching_ga.py (the genetic algorithm, the seed table, penalty against repair) and annealing.py (hill climbing and simulated annealing). python line_switching.py prints the numbers of Lab steps 1 and 2 and of Exercise 3, python switching_ga.py those of Lab steps 3 and 4 and of Exercise 4, and python annealing.py those of Exercises 1 and 2. Every random number generator is seeded, so the values below are reproducible; the times are those of one laptop CPU and will differ on yours.

    Lab¶

    1. Fitness evaluator. pandapower ships MATPOWER's case30; to_ppc returns the MATPOWER matrices, whose branch column 5 (rateA) holds the ratings (16 to 130 MVA, read as MW in the DC model). The unscaled grid is N-1 secure: \(E(\emptyset) = 0.000\) MW (one line sits exactly on its limit). Scaling every load and generator by 1.2 gives \(E(\emptyset) = 16.972\) MW, by 1.4 gives \(145.351\) MW with 51 post-contingency overloads and a worst loading of 140.0 %; the lab uses 1.4. For a set \(D\) the evaluator removes the lines, checks connectivity with scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components, rebuilds the PTDF \(H\) of the reduced grid and the whole LODF matrix at once, \(L_{\ell k} = (H A^\top)_{\ell k} / (1 - (H A^\top)_{kk})\) with \(L_{kk} = -1\), and sums \(\max(0, |f_\ell + L_{\ell k} f_k| - \bar f_\ell)\) over the non-bridge outages \(k\) (a bridge has \((H A^\top)_{kk} = 1\); the unswitched grid has 3: the radial lines L12-13, L9-11 and L25-26). The LODF flows agree with a fresh DC power flow of the grid without the outaged branch to \(10^{-13}\) MW (dc_power_flow_check, three cases checked). Each evaluation takes about 0.25 ms.
    2. Brute force. \(|D| \le 2\): \(1 + 41 + \binom{41}{2} = 862\) sets in 0.2 s, 146 of them islanding; the optimum is \(E = 27.343\) MW at \(\{\)L24-25, L6-28\(\}\). \(|D| \le 3\): 11 522 sets in 2.9 s, 3 302 islanding; the optimum is \(E = 14.391\) MW at \(\{\)L22-24, L24-25, L6-28\(\}\). \(|D| \le 4\) has \(112\,792\) sets, so at the measured rate it would take about 29 s, and \(|D| \le 5\) (862 190 sets) about 4 minutes: on IEEE 30 the enumeration is still affordable, which is what makes it a usable baseline. On a 1 000-branch grid \(|D| \le 3\) alone is \(1.7 \times 10^8\) sets.
    3. Genetic algorithm. Population 32, tournament of size 3, 2 elites copied unchanged, crossover with probability 0.7 followed by the mutation of the worked example, a budget of \(1\,152\) evaluations (10 % of the \(|D| \le 3\) enumeration). Every call of the fitness is counted, including those that return \(-\infty\). The crossover of two parents samples \(k\) unique indices from the union of their lines (fewer when the union is smaller), so a child never has a duplicate index, and normalize keeps equal sets equal.

    line_switching.py
    """IEEE 30 line switching: the N-1 overload energy of a switched topology and its brute-force optimum (Chapter 24).

The grid is MATPOWER's ``case30`` as shipped with pandapower (41 branches with MVA ratings). Loads and generation
are scaled by a common factor so that the unswitched grid has N-1 overloads; the slack bus absorbs the imbalance.
The fitness of a set D of switched-off lines is the negated N-1 overload energy of the reduced grid, computed from
its PTDF and LODF matrices, or -inf when removing D islands the grid.
"""

import itertools
import time
from dataclasses import dataclass

import numpy as np
import pandapower.networks as pn
from pandapower.converter.pypower import to_ppc
from scipy.sparse import coo_matrix
from scipy.sparse.csgraph import connected_components

SCALE = 1.4  # common factor on loads and generation; the unswitched grid then has 51 N-1 overloads


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Case:
    """A DC grid: branch ends (0-based), reactances (p.u.), MW limits, net injections (MW) and branch names."""

    frm: np.ndarray
    to: np.ndarray
    x: np.ndarray
    limit: np.ndarray
    p: np.ndarray
    names: tuple[str, ...]

    @property
    def n_bus(self) -> int:
        """Number of buses."""
        return len(self.p)

    @property
    def n_branch(self) -> int:
        """Number of branches."""
        return len(self.frm)


def load_case30(scale: float = SCALE) -> Case:
    """MATPOWER case30 through pandapower, with every load and generator scaled by ``scale``."""
    ppc = to_ppc(pn.case30(), init='flat')
    branch, bus, gen = ppc['branch'], ppc['bus'], ppc['gen']
    frm, to = branch[:, 0].astype(int), branch[:, 1].astype(int)
    p = np.zeros(len(bus))
    np.add.at(p, gen[:, 0].astype(int), gen[:, 1])
    p -= bus[:, 2]
    names = tuple(f'L{f + 1}-{t + 1}' for f, t in zip(frm, to, strict=True))
    return Case(frm, to, branch[:, 3].copy(), branch[:, 5].copy(), scale * p, names)


def kept_branches(case: Case, switched) -> np.ndarray:
    """Indices of the branches that stay in service when the lines in ``switched`` are opened."""
    mask = np.ones(case.n_branch, dtype=bool)
    mask[list(switched)] = False
    return np.flatnonzero(mask)


def is_connected(case: Case, switched) -> bool:
    """True when the grid without the lines in ``switched`` is a single connected component."""
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    adjacency = coo_matrix((np.ones(len(keep)), (case.frm[keep], case.to[keep])), shape=(case.n_bus, case.n_bus))
    return connected_components(adjacency, directed=False)[0] == 1


def ptdf_matrix(case: Case, keep: np.ndarray, slack: int = 0) -> np.ndarray:
    """PTDF (kept branches x buses) of the reduced grid, bus ``slack`` as reference; the grid must be connected."""
    m = len(keep)
    a = np.zeros((m, case.n_bus))
    a[np.arange(m), case.frm[keep]] = 1.0
    a[np.arange(m), case.to[keep]] = -1.0
    bf = a / case.x[keep][:, None]
    bbus = a.T @ bf
    other = np.arange(case.n_bus) != slack
    h = np.zeros_like(a)
    h[:, other] = np.linalg.solve(bbus[np.ix_(other, other)], bf[:, other].T).T
    return h


def n1_flows(case: Case, switched=()) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray] | None:
    """Base flows, N-1 flow matrix (branch, outage) and the bridge mask of the reduced grid; None if islanded.

    Column k of the N-1 matrix holds the flows after the outage of kept branch k, from the LODF update
    f + LODF[:, k] f_k. Outages of bridges would island the grid; their columns are left equal to the base flows
    and flagged in the returned mask so that the caller can skip them.
    """
    if not is_connected(case, switched):
        return None
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    h = ptdf_matrix(case, keep)
    f0 = h @ case.p
    m = len(keep)
    a = np.zeros((m, case.n_bus))
    a[np.arange(m), case.frm[keep]] = 1.0
    a[np.arange(m), case.to[keep]] = -1.0
    c = h @ a.T  # c[l, k]: flow on l per MW injected at the ends of k
    denominator = 1.0 - np.diag(c)
    bridge = np.abs(denominator) < 1e-9
    lodf = c / np.where(bridge, 1.0, denominator)[None, :]
    np.fill_diagonal(lodf, -1.0)
    lodf[:, bridge] = 0.0
    return f0, f0[:, None] + lodf * f0[None, :], bridge


def overload_energy(case: Case, switched=()) -> float:
    """N-1 overload energy E(D) in MW: sum over non-bridge outages and monitored branches of the MW above the limit.

    Returns +inf when the switched grid is disconnected.
    """
    result = n1_flows(case, switched)
    if result is None:
        return np.inf
    _, f1, bridge = result
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    over = np.maximum(0.0, np.abs(f1) - case.limit[keep][:, None])
    over[:, bridge] = 0.0
    return float(over.sum())


def fitness(case: Case, switched=()) -> float:
    """Negated overload energy, -inf for an islanding set: the quantity the heuristics maximize."""
    return -overload_energy(case, switched)


def worst_loading(case: Case, switched=()) -> float:
    """Largest N-1 loading in percent of the limit over the non-bridge outages (inf if islanded)."""
    result = n1_flows(case, switched)
    if result is None:
        return np.inf
    _, f1, bridge = result
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    loading = 100.0 * np.abs(f1) / case.limit[keep][:, None]
    loading[:, bridge] = 0.0
    return float(loading.max())


def dc_power_flow_check(case: Case, switched, outage: int) -> float:
    """Largest difference (MW) between the LODF post-contingency flows and a fresh DC power flow without ``outage``.

    ``outage`` is a branch index of the original grid, not in ``switched``.
    """
    _, f1, _ = n1_flows(case, switched)
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    column = int(np.flatnonzero(keep == outage)[0])
    full = kept_branches(case, (*switched, outage))
    direct = ptdf_matrix(case, full) @ case.p
    lodf_flows = np.delete(f1[:, column], column)
    return float(np.abs(lodf_flows - direct).max())


@dataclass
class BruteForce:
    """Result of an exhaustive enumeration of all sets with at most ``k_max`` switched lines."""

    k_max: int
    values: dict[tuple[int, ...], float]
    seconds: float

    @property
    def n_evaluations(self) -> int:
        """Number of fitness evaluations, one per set."""
        return len(self.values)

    @property
    def best(self) -> tuple[tuple[int, ...], float]:
        """The optimal set and its fitness."""
        return max(self.values.items(), key=lambda item: item[1])

    @property
    def n_islanding(self) -> int:
        """Number of enumerated sets that disconnect the grid."""
        return sum(1 for v in self.values.values() if v == -np.inf)


def brute_force(case: Case, k_max: int) -> BruteForce:
    """Fitness of every set of at most ``k_max`` lines, enumerated lazily with ``itertools.combinations``."""
    start = time.perf_counter()
    sets = itertools.chain.from_iterable(itertools.combinations(range(case.n_branch), k) for k in range(k_max + 1))
    values = {d: fitness(case, d) for d in sets}
    return BruteForce(k_max, values, time.perf_counter() - start)


def count_sets(n: int, k_max: int) -> int:
    """Number of subsets of at most ``k_max`` out of ``n`` lines."""
    from math import comb

    return sum(comb(n, k) for k in range(k_max + 1))


def interaction(case: Case, a: int, b: int) -> tuple[float, float, float, float]:
    """E(empty), E({a}), E({b}) and E({a, b}); the benefits are additive when the last equals the sum of gains."""
    return (
        overload_energy(case),
        overload_energy(case, (a,)),
        overload_energy(case, (b,)),
        overload_energy(case, (a, b)),
    )


def per_line_energy(case: Case, switched=()) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Contribution of each overloaded branch to E(D), summed over the outages, by branch name (largest first)."""
    _, f1, bridge = n1_flows(case, switched)
    keep = kept_branches(case, switched)
    over = np.maximum(0.0, np.abs(f1) - case.limit[keep][:, None])
    over[:, bridge] = 0.0
    totals = over.sum(axis=1)
    order = np.argsort(-totals)
    return {case.names[keep[i]]: float(totals[i]) for i in order if totals[i] > 0}


def greedy(case: Case, k_max: int) -> tuple[list[int], list[float]]:
    """Add the single line that lowers E most at each step (no backtracking); the path of sets and energies."""
    chosen: list[int] = []
    energies = [overload_energy(case)]
    for _ in range(k_max):
        candidates = [
            (overload_energy(case, (*chosen, line)), line) for line in range(case.n_branch) if line not in chosen
        ]
        best_energy, best_line = min(candidates)
        if best_energy >= energies[-1]:
            break
        chosen.append(best_line)
        energies.append(best_energy)
    return chosen, energies


def describe(case: Case, switched) -> str:
    """Branch names of a set, for printing."""
    return '{' + ', '.join(case.names[i] for i in sorted(switched)) + '}' if len(switched) else '{}'


def main() -> None:
    """Lab steps 1 and 2 and Exercise 3: evaluator, brute force up to three lines, non-additive pair, greedy path."""
    for scale in (1.0, 1.2, 1.4):
        print(f'scale {scale}: E(empty) = {overload_energy(load_case30(scale)):.3f} MW')
    case = load_case30()
    _, f1, bridge = n1_flows(case)
    over = (np.abs(f1) - case.limit[:, None] > 0) & ~bridge[None, :]
    print(
        f'unswitched grid: {int(over.sum())} N-1 overloads, worst loading {worst_loading(case):.1f} %, '
        f'bridges {[case.names[i] for i in np.flatnonzero(bridge)]}'
    )
    for switched, outage in (((), 0), ((32, 40), 5), ((34,), 10)):
        print(
            f'LODF vs DC power flow, D={describe(case, switched)} outage {case.names[outage]}: '
            f'{dc_power_flow_check(case, switched, outage):.2e} MW'
        )
    a, b = 32, 40  # L24-25 and L6-28, the best pair
    for k_max in (2, 3):
        bf = brute_force(case, k_max)
        best, value = bf.best
        print(
            f'|D| <= {k_max}: {bf.n_evaluations} evaluations in {bf.seconds:.1f} s, {bf.n_islanding} islanding sets, '
            f'optimum E = {-value:.3f} MW at {describe(case, best)}'
        )
    per_eval = bf.seconds / bf.n_evaluations
    for k_max in (4, 5):
        n_sets = count_sets(case.n_branch, k_max)
        print(f'|D| <= {k_max}: {n_sets} sets, predicted {per_eval * n_sets:.0f} s')
    good = [d for d, v in bf.values.items() if -v < 30]
    with_pair = sum(1 for d in good if a in d and b in d)
    print(f'{len(good)} sets below 30 MW, {with_pair} of them contain both lines of the best pair')
    e0, ea, eb, eab = interaction(case, a, b)
    print(
        f'E(empty)={e0:.3f} E({case.names[a]})={ea:.3f} E({case.names[b]})={eb:.3f} E(both)={eab:.3f}; '
        f'additive prediction {ea + eb - e0:.3f}'
    )
    for switched in ((), (a,), (b,), (a, b)):
        top = list(per_line_energy(case, switched).items())[:3]
        print(f'  D={describe(case, switched)}: ' + ', '.join(f'{name} {e:.2f}' for name, e in top))
    path, energies = greedy(case, 3)
    print('greedy path', [case.names[i] for i in path], [round(e, 3) for e in energies])


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
    switching_ga.py
    """A genetic algorithm for "switch off at most k lines" on IEEE 30, checked against brute force (Chapter 24).

The genotype and the mutation are those of ``genome_mutation.py``: k sorted slots padded with the sentinel n.
Crossover samples k unique indices from the union of two parents. Selection is a tournament, the best genomes
survive unchanged (elitism), and the run stops after a fixed number of fitness evaluations. Islanding candidates
either get fitness -inf (penalty) or are repaired by dropping lines until the grid is connected.
"""

import time
from dataclasses import dataclass, field

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from genome_mutation import mutate, normalize
from line_switching import Case, fitness, is_connected, load_case30

K_MAX = 3  # at most three switched lines
BUDGET = 1152  # 10 % of the 11 522 sets with at most three lines


class Evaluator:
    """Counts fitness evaluations and records the best value seen after each one, with the elapsed time."""

    def __init__(self, case: Case):
        """Wrap the fitness of ``case``."""
        self.case = case
        self.n_evaluations = 0
        self.n_islanding = 0
        self.best = -np.inf
        self.best_genome: np.ndarray | None = None
        self.history: list[tuple[int, float, float]] = []  # (evaluations, best so far, seconds)
        self.start = time.perf_counter()

    def __call__(self, genome: np.ndarray) -> float:
        """Fitness of the set ``genome[genome < n]``, counted once per call."""
        value = fitness(self.case, genome[genome < self.case.n_branch])
        self.n_evaluations += 1
        if value == -np.inf:
            self.n_islanding += 1
        if value > self.best:
            self.best, self.best_genome = value, genome.copy()
        self.history.append((self.n_evaluations, self.best, time.perf_counter() - self.start))
        return value


def random_genome(k: int, n: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> np.ndarray:
    """A normalized genome whose slots are random lines or the sentinel, each with equal weight."""
    return normalize(rng.integers(n + 1, size=k), n)


def crossover(a: np.ndarray, b: np.ndarray, n: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> np.ndarray:
    """Sample up to k unique indices from the union of the two parents' lines."""
    union = np.union1d(a[a < n], b[b < n])
    child = rng.choice(union, size=min(len(a), len(union)), replace=False) if len(union) else np.array([], dtype=int)
    return normalize(np.concatenate([child, np.full(len(a) - len(child), n)]).astype(int), n)


def tournament(values: np.ndarray, size: int, rng: np.random.Generator) -> int:
    """Index of the best of ``size`` genomes drawn at random."""
    contenders = rng.choice(len(values), size=size, replace=False)
    return int(contenders[np.argmax(values[contenders])])


def repair(case: Case, genome: np.ndarray, rng: np.random.Generator) -> np.ndarray:
    """Drop lines from the genome in random order until the grid is connected (no fitness evaluation needed)."""
    n = case.n_branch
    lines = genome[genome < n]
    order = rng.permutation(len(lines))
    while len(lines) and not is_connected(case, lines):
        lines = np.delete(lines, order[0] % len(lines))
        order = order[1:]
    return normalize(np.concatenate([lines, np.full(len(genome) - len(lines), n)]).astype(int), n)


@dataclass
class Run:
    """One seeded run: the evaluation counter with its history, and the configuration."""

    seed: int
    evaluator: Evaluator
    repaired: bool
    config: dict = field(default_factory=dict)

    @property
    def best(self) -> float:
        """Best fitness found."""
        return self.evaluator.best

    @property
    def seconds(self) -> float:
        """Wall-clock time of the run."""
        return self.evaluator.history[-1][2]

    def evaluations_to_reach(self, target: float, tol: float = 1e-9) -> int | None:
        """Number of evaluations after which the best value first reached ``target``; None if never."""
        for n_eval, best, _ in self.evaluator.history:
            if best >= target - tol:
                return n_eval
        return None

    def curve(self) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
        """Best-so-far fitness against evaluations."""
        h = np.array(self.evaluator.history)
        return h[:, 0], h[:, 1]


def genetic_algorithm(  # noqa: PLR0913
    case: Case,
    seed: int,
    k: int = K_MAX,
    budget: int = BUDGET,
    pop_size: int = 32,
    n_elite: int = 2,
    tournament_size: int = 3,
    p_crossover: float = 0.7,
    repaired: bool = False,
) -> Run:
    """Run the GA until ``budget`` fitness evaluations have been spent; the run keeps the whole history."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    n = case.n_branch
    evaluator = Evaluator(case)
    fix = (lambda g: repair(case, g, rng)) if repaired else (lambda g: g)
    population = [fix(random_genome(k, n, rng)) for _ in range(pop_size)]
    values = np.array([evaluator(g) for g in population])
    while evaluator.n_evaluations < budget:
        elite = [population[i] for i in np.argsort(values)[::-1][:n_elite]]
        children = []
        while len(children) < pop_size - n_elite and evaluator.n_evaluations + len(children) < budget:
            parent = population[tournament(values, tournament_size, rng)]
            if rng.random() < p_crossover:
                parent = crossover(parent, population[tournament(values, tournament_size, rng)], n, rng)
            children.append(fix(mutate(parent, n, rng)))
        child_values = np.array([evaluator(g) for g in children])
        population = elite + children
        values = np.concatenate([[values[i] for i in np.argsort(values)[::-1][:n_elite]], child_values])
    config = {'k': k, 'budget': budget, 'pop_size': pop_size, 'n_elite': n_elite, 'p_crossover': p_crossover}
    return Run(seed, evaluator, repaired, config)


def seed_table(runs: list[Run], target: float, case: Case) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """One row per run: best overload energy, evaluations to first reach ``target``, time and wasted evaluations."""
    rows = []
    for run in runs:
        reached = run.evaluations_to_reach(target)
        genome = run.evaluator.best_genome
        rows.append(
            {
                'seed': run.seed,
                'best E [MW]': round(-run.best, 3),
                'lines': ', '.join(case.names[i] for i in genome[genome < case.n_branch]),
                'evals to optimum': 'not reached' if reached is None else reached,
                'seconds': round(run.seconds, 2),
                'islanding evals': run.evaluator.n_islanding,
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index('seed')


def plot_convergence(runs: list[Run], target: float | None = None, ax=None) -> None:
    """Best-so-far overload energy against evaluations, one line per seed."""
    ax = ax or plt.gca()
    for run in runs:
        evals, best = run.curve()
        ax.plot(evals, -best, lw=1, label=f'seed {run.seed}')
    if target is not None:
        ax.axhline(-target, color='k', ls='--', lw=1, label='brute-force optimum')
    ax.set_xlabel('fitness evaluations')
    ax.set_ylabel('best N-1 overload energy [MW]')
    ax.set_yscale('log')
    ax.legend(fontsize=7, ncol=2)

    Exercise 1¶

    A genome has \(k\) slots; "change one slot" overwrites one slot with one of the \(n\) line indices or the sentinel, so there are \(k(n + 1)\) raw moves, \(3 \times 42 = 126\) for the lab. After normalization several moves give the same set, so count sets instead. If the genome holds \(j \le k\) distinct lines, a real slot can be swapped for one of the \(n - j\) absent lines (\(j(n - j)\) neighbors), overwritten with the sentinel or with a line already present (\(j\) neighbors, each dropping one line), and, if \(j < k\), a sentinel slot can take one of the \(n - j\) absent lines (\(n - j\) neighbors):

    \[ N(j) = j(n - j) + j + [\,j < k\,](n - j). \]

    With \(n = 41\), \(k = 3\): a full genome has \(3 \times 38 + 3 = 117\) neighbors, a two-line genome \(2 \times 39 + 2 + 39 = 119\) and the empty genome 41. annealing.neighbors enumerates the sets and finds the same counts. The two-line toy example (toy_fitness): four lines, \(k = 2\), fitness

    set \(\{0, 1\}\) \(\{2, 3\}\) any mixed pair any single line \(\emptyset\)
    fitness 10 20 0 5 0

    From \(\{0, 1\}\) every neighbor (\(\{0, 2\}\), \(\{0, 3\}\), \(\{1, 2\}\), \(\{1, 3\}\), \(\{0\}\), \(\{1\}\)) scores at most 5, so hill climbing evaluates the 7 candidates and stops with 10. Simulated annealing at temperature \(T\) accepts the step down to a mixed pair with probability \(e^{-10/T}\), and from \(\{0, 2\}\) one more slot change reaches \(\{2, 3\}\); with \(T_0 = 10\), \(\alpha = 0.97\) and 200 steps, 18 of 20 seeded runs end at 20.

    annealing.py
    """Hill climbing and simulated annealing on the k-subset neighborhood (Chapter 24, Exercises 1 and 2).

The neighborhood of a genome is "change one slot": overwrite one of the k slots with another line or with the
sentinel, then normalize. Hill climbing only accepts improvements and stops at a local optimum. Simulated
annealing accepts a worse neighbor with probability exp(delta / T), T following a geometric cooling schedule.
"""

import itertools
import math
from typing import TYPE_CHECKING

import numpy as np
from genome_mutation import mutate, normalize
from line_switching import Case, load_case30
from switching_ga import BUDGET, K_MAX, Evaluator, Run, random_genome

if TYPE_CHECKING:
    from collections.abc import Callable


def neighbors(genome: np.ndarray, n: int) -> set[tuple[int, ...]]:
    """Every distinct genome reached by overwriting one slot with any line or the sentinel."""
    out = set()
    for slot in range(len(genome)):
        for value in range(n + 1):
            g = genome.copy()
            g[slot] = value
            out.add(tuple(normalize(g, n)))
    out.discard(tuple(genome))
    return out


def count_neighbors(n: int, k: int, j: int) -> int:
    """Distinct neighbors of a genome with j lines in k slots: j (n - j) swaps, j removals, n - j additions (j < k)."""
    return j * (n - j) + j + (n - j if j < k else 0)


TOY_N, TOY_K = 4, 2  # four lines, at most two switched off


def toy_fitness(genome: np.ndarray) -> float:
    """A deceptive two-line landscape: {0, 1} is a local optimum, {2, 3} the global one, every mixed pair is poor."""
    lines = frozenset(genome[genome < TOY_N].tolist())
    table = {frozenset({0, 1}): 10.0, frozenset({2, 3}): 20.0}
    return table.get(lines, 5.0 if len(lines) == 1 else 0.0)


def hill_climb(objective: Callable[[np.ndarray], float], start: np.ndarray, n: int) -> tuple[np.ndarray, int]:
    """Steepest ascent over the whole neighborhood; returns the local optimum and the number of evaluations."""
    current, value, evaluations = start, objective(start), 1
    while True:
        best = None
        for g in neighbors(current, n):
            candidate = np.array(g)
            v = objective(candidate)
            evaluations += 1
            if v > value and (best is None or v > best[1]):
                best = (candidate, v)
        if best is None:
            return current, evaluations
        current, value = best




def simulated_annealing(  # noqa: PLR0913
    objective: Callable[[np.ndarray], float],
    start: np.ndarray,
    n: int,
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    budget: int,
    t0: float,
    alpha: float,
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, float]:
    """Geometric cooling T_i = t0 alpha^i, one mutated neighbor per step; returns the best genome and value."""
    current, value = start, objective(start)
    best, best_value = current, value
    temperature = t0
    for _ in range(budget - 1):
        candidate = mutate(current, n, rng)
        v = objective(candidate)
        delta = v - value
        if delta >= 0 or (math.isfinite(delta) and rng.random() < math.exp(delta / temperature)):
            current, value = candidate, v
            if v > best_value:
                best, best_value = candidate, v
        temperature *= alpha
    return best, best_value


def toy_runs(seed: int = 0, n_runs: int = 20) -> tuple[float, int]:
    """Hill climbing from {0, 1} stays there; the fraction of seeded annealing runs that reach {2, 3}."""
    start = normalize(np.array([0, 1]), TOY_N)
    stuck, _ = hill_climb(toy_fitness, start, TOY_N)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    escaped = sum(
        simulated_annealing(toy_fitness, start, TOY_N, rng, budget=200, t0=10.0, alpha=0.97)[1] == 20.0
        for _ in range(n_runs)
    )
    return toy_fitness(stuck), escaped


def anneal_case(case: Case, seed: int, k: int = K_MAX, budget: int = BUDGET, t0: float = 20.0, alpha=0.995) -> Run:
    """Simulated annealing on the line-switching fitness with the GA's evaluation budget, as a ``Run``."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    evaluator = Evaluator(case)
    simulated_annealing(evaluator, random_genome(k, case.n_branch, rng), case.n_branch, rng, budget, t0, alpha)
    return Run(seed, evaluator, False, {'k': k, 'budget': budget, 't0': t0, 'alpha': alpha})

    Exercise 3¶

    Take $a = $ L24-25 and $b = $ L6-28, the best pair of the enumeration: \(E(\emptyset) = 145.351\), \(E(\{a\}) = 94.869\), \(E(\{b\}) = 138.910\) and \(E(\{a, b\}) = 27.343\) MW. The single gains are \(50.5\) and \(6.4\) MW, so additivity would predict \(E(\{a, b\}) = 88.427\) MW; the pair removes \(118.0\) MW, more than twice the sum. Two mechanisms, both visible in the LODFs (per_line_energy prints the contributions per branch):

    • The LODFs of the reduced grid are not those of the original. After removing \(a\), the PTDF changes, and with it the LODF column of every outage, including the one that overloads L6-8 (32 MW limit), the branch that carries 102.07 of the 145.35 MW of the base energy. Removing \(b\) alone shifts flow onto L25-27 and L24-25 and creates new overloads there (13.47 and 11.43 MW); removing \(a\) as well deletes one of those branches and removes the other overload, so the sum of two single effects counts damage that the pair never does.
    • The energy is a sum of hinges \(\max(0, \cdot)\). Each switch alone lowers L6-8's post-contingency flows but leaves them above 32 MW in many outages (its contribution drops to 57.53 MW with \(a\), to 65.79 MW with \(b\)); together they push the flows below the limit and the contribution falls to zero. A reduction that stays above the limit is credited in full, one that crosses it is credited only down to the limit, so gains superadd near the limit and subadd far from it.

    Greedy selection adds the single best line at each step: greedy picks L25-27 (\(69.449\) MW), then L6-28 (\(33.283\)), then L29-30 (\(28.883\)), and stops there. It never looks at L24-25, whose single gain is smaller than L25-27's, so it ends at twice the true \(|D| \le 3\) optimum of \(14.391\) MW (\(\{\)L22-24, L24-25, L6-28\(\}\)), and it cannot backtrack. A heuristic that mutates sets and keeps a population can hold L24-25 and L6-28 together even when neither is the best single move, which is the whole argument for population-based search on this problem.

    The same ten seeds with repaired=True, where a child that islands the grid loses lines in random order until it is connected (a connectivity check, not a fitness evaluation), against the penalty of Lab step 4:

Dropping lines biases the children towards smaller sets, and the optimum needs all three slots; the repaired children also take about 0.8 s per run instead of 0.5 s because of the connectivity checks. On a grid with many radial lines (3 302 of the 11 522 sets island IEEE 30) repair is the better default, but it has to be combined with an operator that adds lines back, which is what ToOp's add_disconnection_prob does. The honest conclusion from ten runs is "repair wastes fewer evaluations, and the final quality is not distinguishable"; Chapter 26 gives the test for the second half.

    Exercise 5¶

    The No Free Lunch theorems average over all fitness functions on a finite search space, including every permutation of the values: over that set, knowing the fitness of some sets says nothing about the fitness of others, so no algorithm can exploit anything and all do equally well. Line switching is not drawn from that set. Its fitness has structure: the flows are a smooth function of the admittance matrix, the LODF of a set changes little when one far-away line is added, sets sharing two lines of a good triple tend to be good (14 of the 11 522 sets are below 30 MW and 13 of them contain L24-25 and L6-28), and islanding sets can be recognized without a power flow. Operators that respect this structure, a mutation that changes one line, a crossover that keeps the common lines of two good parents, repair that keeps the grid connected, deduplication that avoids paying twice for the same set, exploit the correlation between neighboring sets that NFL assumes away. The theorem is a warning against claiming a universally best optimizer, not a license to ignore the problem: the choice of operators is exactly the place where knowledge about the problem class enters, and it is why ToOp's mutation acts on substations and disconnections rather than on random bit strings.
