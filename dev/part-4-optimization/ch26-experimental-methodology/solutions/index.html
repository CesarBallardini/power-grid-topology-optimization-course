    Chapter 26 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 26. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: compare_runs.py (the rank-sum test, \(\hat A_{12}\) and ECDFs of the worked example), ga_experiment.py (Lab steps 1 to 5 on the GA of Chapter 24), toop_anytime.py with toop_seeds.csv (Lab step 6), rank_tests.py (Exercises 2 and 5), performance_profile.py and its Octave twin perf_profile.m (Exercise 3). Every random number generator is seeded, so every value below is reproducible except the wall-clock times, which are those of one laptop CPU; the ToOp runs used ToOp ba1874e on CPU. python ga_experiment.py, python rank_tests.py, python performance_profile.py and python toop_anytime.py print the numbers quoted here.

    Lab¶

    1. Protocol first. Written before any run and printed with the results (ga_experiment.protocol):

      element choice
      instance IEEE 30 (MATPOWER case30), loads and generation × 1.4, switch off at most 3 lines (Chapter 24)
      configurations the Chapter 24 GA with crossover (probability 0.7) and without (0.0); everything else equal
      budget 1 152 fitness evaluations per run, islanding candidates included
      measure best N-1 overload energy at the end of the budget, in MW, lower is better
      repetitions seeds 0 to 9 for each configuration
      test two-sided Mann–Whitney U on the ten final values per configuration, \(\alpha = 0.05\)
      effect size Vargha–Delaney \(\hat A_{12}\) and a bootstrap 95 % confidence interval of the difference in medians

      Nothing in this list may change after the runs. In particular the measure is the value at the end of the budget; the evaluations needed to reach the optimum are not in the protocol, so they cannot become the headline when the final values disagree with the hypothesis.

    2. Run. run_all returns one pandas row per (configuration, seed, evaluation) with the best-so-far value and the elapsed seconds, \(2 \times 10 \times 1\,152 = 23\,040\) rows; switching_ga.Evaluator records that history on every call. Before the first finite evaluation of a run (every candidate islanded so far) the best value is \(+\infty\); the curves start at the first finite value. The runs took 0.42 to 0.80 s each on this machine.

    3. Anytime curves. anytime interpolates every run onto a common grid of 60 points and takes the 25, 50 and 75 % quantiles over the ten seeds; plot_anytime draws the median line and the quantile band per configuration, against evaluations and, with x='seconds', against time. Against evaluations the two bands overlap almost entirely: at 313 evaluations the medians are 28.96 MW (crossover) and 28.85 MW (no crossover), with quartiles 26.55–32.05 and 16.53–32.91. The wider band without crossover says that its seeds are more spread out, not that it is better. Against seconds the picture depends on the machine: the time per evaluation is the same for both configurations (the fitness dominates), so the curves against seconds are the curves against evaluations stretched by each run's speed, and the bands widen by the timing noise.

    4. ECDFs and tests. Final values (MW), seeds 0 to 9:

      configuration final \(E\) per seed median
      crossover 14.391 ×9, 22.943 (seed 4) 14.391
      no crossover 14.391 ×7, 22.943 (seeds 7 and 9), 22.943 14.391

      Both ECDFs are two-step functions: a jump at 14.391 (to 0.9 and 0.7) and the rest at 22.943. Mann–Whitney \(U = 45\), \(p = 0.58\); \(\hat A_{12} = 0.55\) (a crossover run beats a run without crossover 55 % of the time, ties counted half); bootstrap 95 % interval of the difference in medians \((-4.28, 0.00)\) MW, which contains 0. Conclusion, in two sentences: With 1 152 evaluations on this instance, the data do not show a difference between the GA with and without crossover (ten seeds each, \(p = 0.58\), \(\hat A_{12} = 0.55\), 9 against 7 runs at the optimum). A difference of one or two seeds reaching the optimum is within what chance produces, and nothing larger was observed. Not "crossover helps" and not "crossover does not help": the experiment has no power to see a small effect (Exercise 5).

    5. Wall-clock trap. The runs are too short (half a second) to share the machine with a real burner process in a controlled way, so wall_clock_budget simulates it: the recorded times of the even seeds are multiplied by 3, the budget is half the median run time (0.24 s here) and each run's value is its best-so-far at that moment. On a quiet machine the budget favours the configuration that happens to run faster on this machine: medians 14.391 against 26.664 MW, \(p = 0.12\), \(\hat A_{12} = 0.69\), a difference that the evaluation budget did not show, produced by the clock. With half the runs slowed the medians are 26.775 against 27.343 MW, \(p = 0.59\), \(\hat A_{12} = 0.57\): the slowed runs completed a median of 194 evaluations in the budget against 559 for the others, so the result now measures which seeds were slowed, not which configuration is better. Every number in this step changes from one machine, or one run, to the next, which is the point: a wall-clock budget confounds the optimizer with the hardware and the load, and only an evaluation budget (or a per-evaluation cost model) makes two runs comparable. This is exactly ToOp's situation, whose budget is runtime_seconds.

    6. Optional, ToOp. Five runs of the small-grid example with random_seed 1 to 5 and runtime_seconds = 30 (small_grid_toop.py of Chapter 25, which writes a res_<epoch>.json per epoch), collected by toop_anytime.collect and committed, compacted, as toop_seeds.csv:

      seed epochs in 30 s total_branch_combis final max_fitness first epoch at the maximum
      1 1 307 1 045 608 −42.345 0
      2 909 727 208 −42.345 0
      3 1 143 914 408 −42.345 0
      4 1 022 817 608 −42.345 0
      5 1 210 968 008 −42.345 0

      The same 30 s bought between 909 and 1 307 epochs (a ratio of 1.44) on one idle laptop: the number of epochs, not the seconds, is the budget a run actually received, and total_branch_combis (808 topologies per epoch here) is the comparable count. The anytime curves against epochs or against evaluated topologies are flat lines of different lengths at \(-42.345\): the small grid has ten topologies and the first epoch evaluates them all, so every seed is at the optimum from epoch 0. truncate cuts every run at the smallest common epoch (909 epochs, 720 808 topologies): the five runs are then compared under an equal budget, and here they are identical. On a real grid the curves climb for many epochs, and the truncation decides which run looks best.

    Solutions: main() of ga_experiment.py (steps 1 to 5) and of toop_anytime.py (step 6); the notebook lab.ipynb has the steps with empty cells.

    ga_experiment.py
    """Two GA configurations, ten seeds, one honest conclusion (Chapter 26, Lab steps 1 to 5).

The instance is the IEEE 30 line-switching problem of Chapter 24 and the optimizer its genetic algorithm (that
chapter's ``src`` folder is put on the import path). The two configurations differ in one component, crossover
(probability 0.7 against 0.0), the budget is a fixed number of fitness evaluations, and the performance measure is
the best N-1 overload energy at the end of the budget. Every run logs its best-so-far value after each evaluation
with the elapsed time, one row per (configuration, seed, evaluation).
"""

import sys
from pathlib import Path

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from compare_runs import compare

sys.path.insert(0, str(Path(__file__).resolve().parents[2] / 'ch24-evolutionary-algorithms' / 'src'))

from line_switching import load_case30  # noqa: E402
from switching_ga import BUDGET, genetic_algorithm  # noqa: E402

CONFIGURATIONS = {'crossover': {'p_crossover': 0.7}, 'no crossover': {'p_crossover': 0.0}}
SEEDS = tuple(range(10))
ALPHA = 0.05


def protocol() -> dict:
    """The pre-registered protocol: written down before any run, printed with the results."""
    return {
        'instance': 'IEEE 30 (MATPOWER case30), loads and generation x1.4, switch off at most 3 lines',
        'configurations': CONFIGURATIONS,
        'budget': f'{BUDGET} fitness evaluations per run (islanding candidates count)',
        'measure': 'best N-1 overload energy [MW] at the end of the budget (lower is better)',
        'seeds': list(SEEDS),
        'test': 'two-sided Mann-Whitney U on the 10 final values per configuration',
        'effect size': 'Vargha-Delaney A12 and a bootstrap 95 % confidence interval of the difference in medians',
        'significance level': ALPHA,
    }


def run_all(seeds=SEEDS, budget: int = BUDGET) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """One row per (configuration, seed, evaluation): best overload energy so far and elapsed seconds."""
    case = load_case30()
    frames = []
    for name, kwargs in CONFIGURATIONS.items():
        for seed in seeds:
            run = genetic_algorithm(case, seed, budget=budget, **kwargs)
            h = np.array(run.evaluator.history)
            frames.append(
                pd.DataFrame(
                    {
                        'configuration': name,
                        'seed': seed,
                        'evaluation': h[:, 0].astype(int),
                        'best': -h[:, 1],
                        'seconds': h[:, 2],
                    }
                )
            )
    return pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)


def final_values(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
    """Best value at the end of the budget, one array of seeds per configuration."""
    last = df.sort_values('evaluation').groupby(['configuration', 'seed'])['best'].last()
    return {name: last[name].to_numpy() for name in CONFIGURATIONS}


def anytime(df: pd.DataFrame, x: str = 'evaluation', n_points: int = 60) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Median and 25-75 % quantiles of the best-so-far value against ``x`` (``evaluation`` or ``seconds``).

    Before a run's first finite value (every candidate islanded so far) it has no value; grid points where no run
    has a value yet are NaN.
    """
    grid = np.linspace(df[x].min(), df[x].max(), n_points)
    rows = []
    for name in CONFIGURATIONS:
        curves = []
        for _, run in df[df['configuration'] == name].groupby('seed'):
            finite = run[np.isfinite(run['best'])]
            curves.append(np.interp(grid, finite[x], finite['best'], left=np.nan))
        stacked = np.array(curves)
        q = np.full((3, n_points), np.nan)
        for j in range(n_points):
            column = stacked[:, j][np.isfinite(stacked[:, j])]
            if len(column):
                q[:, j] = np.percentile(column, [25, 50, 75])
        rows.append(pd.DataFrame({'configuration': name, x: grid, 'q25': q[0], 'median': q[1], 'q75': q[2]}))
    return pd.concat(rows, ignore_index=True)


def plot_anytime(df: pd.DataFrame, x: str = 'evaluation', ax=None) -> None:
    """Median line and quantile band per configuration."""
    ax = ax or plt.gca()
    for name, curve in anytime(df, x).groupby('configuration'):
        ax.plot(curve[x], curve['median'], label=name)
        ax.fill_between(curve[x], curve['q25'], curve['q75'], alpha=0.25)
    ax.set_xlabel(x)
    ax.set_ylabel('best N-1 overload energy [MW]')
    ax.set_yscale('log')
    ax.legend()


def bootstrap_median_difference(
    a: np.ndarray, b: np.ndarray, n_boot: int = 10_000, seed: int = 0
) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Percentile 95 % confidence interval of median(a) - median(b) from paired resampling of each sample."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    ra = rng.choice(a, size=(n_boot, len(a)), replace=True)
    rb = rng.choice(b, size=(n_boot, len(b)), replace=True)
    diff = np.median(ra, axis=1) - np.median(rb, axis=1)
    low, high = np.percentile(diff, [2.5, 97.5])
    return float(low), float(high)


def analysis(a: np.ndarray, b: np.ndarray) -> dict:
    """U, p, A12 (probability that a run of ``a`` beats one of ``b``) and the bootstrap interval of the medians."""
    u, p, a12 = compare(a, b)
    low, high = bootstrap_median_difference(a, b)
    return {
        'U': u,
        'p': p,
        'A12': a12,
        'median a': float(np.median(a)),
        'median b': float(np.median(b)),
        'CI': (low, high),
    }


def wall_clock_budget(df: pd.DataFrame, seconds: float, slow_seeds=(), slowdown: float = 3.0) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
    """Best value reached within ``seconds`` of wall-clock time per run.

    The runs of ``slow_seeds`` are taken to have shared the machine with a heavy process: their recorded times are
    multiplied by ``slowdown`` before the budget is applied (a simulation of the trap, reproducible; running a real
    CPU burner during half of the runs gives the same effect and a different number each time).
    """
    out = {}
    for name in CONFIGURATIONS:
        values = []
        for seed, run in df[df['configuration'] == name].groupby('seed'):
            factor = slowdown if seed in slow_seeds else 1.0
            within = run[run['seconds'] * factor <= seconds]
            values.append(within['best'].iloc[-1] if len(within) else np.nan)
        out[name] = np.array(values)
    return out


def evaluations_within(df: pd.DataFrame, seconds: float, slow_seeds=(), slowdown: float = 3.0) -> pd.Series:
    """Evaluations completed within the wall-clock budget, per (configuration, seed)."""
    rows = {}
    for (name, seed), run in df.groupby(['configuration', 'seed']):
        factor = slowdown if seed in slow_seeds else 1.0
        rows[(name, seed)] = int((run['seconds'] * factor <= seconds).sum())
    return pd.Series(rows)


def main() -> None:
    """Protocol, runs, final values, test, effect size, interval, and the wall-clock trap."""
    for key, value in protocol().items():
        print(f'{key}: {value}')
    df = run_all()
    finals = final_values(df)
    for name, values in finals.items():
        print(f'{name:13s} final E [MW]: {np.round(values, 3).tolist()}  median {np.median(values):.3f}')
    a, b = finals['crossover'], finals['no crossover']
    result = analysis(a, b)
    print(
        f'U = {result["U"]:.1f}, p = {result["p"]:.4f}, A12 = {result["A12"]:.2f}, '
        f'95 % CI of median difference ({result["CI"][0]:.3f}, {result["CI"][1]:.3f}) MW'
    )
    curve = anytime(df)
    for name, c in curve.groupby('configuration'):
        at = c.iloc[np.searchsorted(c['evaluation'], 300)]
        print(
            f'{name:13s} at {at["evaluation"]:.0f} evaluations: '
            f'median {at["median"]:.2f}, q25 {at["q25"]:.2f}, q75 {at["q75"]:.2f}'
        )
    seconds = df.groupby(['configuration', 'seed'])['seconds'].max()
    print(f'run time per {BUDGET} evaluations: {seconds.min():.2f} to {seconds.max():.2f} s (machine dependent)')
    budget = float(seconds.median() / 2)
    slow = tuple(SEEDS[::2])
    fair = wall_clock_budget(df, budget)
    trapped = wall_clock_budget(df, budget, slow_seeds=slow)
    for label, values in (
        ('wall-clock budget, quiet machine', fair),
        ('wall-clock budget, half the runs slowed x3', trapped),
    ):
        r = analysis(values['crossover'], values['no crossover'])
        print(
            f'{label} ({budget:.2f} s): medians {r["median a"]:.3f} vs {r["median b"]:.3f}, '
            f'p = {r["p"]:.4f}, A12 = {r["A12"]:.2f}'
        )
    n_eval = evaluations_within(df, budget, slow_seeds=slow)
    slowed = n_eval[[s in slow for _, s in n_eval.index]]
    others = n_eval[[s not in slow for _, s in n_eval.index]]
    print(f'evaluations within the budget: slowed runs {slowed.median():.0f}, others {others.median():.0f}')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
    toop_anytime.py
    """Anytime curves of repeated ToOp runs from their ``res_<epoch>.json`` files (Chapter 26, Lab step 6).

ToOp writes one ``res_<epoch>.json`` per epoch into ``stats_dir``; each holds ``max_fitness``, ``iteration`` and
the cumulative counter ``total_branch_combis``. ``collect`` reads a folder with one run per seed (as written by
Chapter 25's ``small_grid_toop.py OUT seed<k> --seed <k>``), ``compact`` keeps only the epochs where something
changes so that the table can be committed (``toop_seeds.csv``), ``truncate`` cuts every run at the smallest
common epoch, and ``plot_anytime`` draws best fitness against epochs or against evaluated topologies.
"""

import json
import sys
from pathlib import Path

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import pandas as pd

HERE = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
EXAMPLE = HERE / 'toop_seeds.csv'


def collect(folder: Path) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """One row per (seed, epoch) from ``folder/seed<k>/results/*/res_<epoch>.json``."""
    rows = []
    for run in sorted(folder.glob('seed*')):
        seed = int(run.name.removeprefix('seed'))
        for path in run.glob('results/*/res_*.json'):
            res = json.loads(path.read_text(encoding='utf-8'))
            rows.append(
                {
                    'seed': seed,
                    'epoch': res['iteration'],
                    'max_fitness': res['max_fitness'],
                    'total_branch_combis': res['total_branch_combis'],
                    'runtime_seconds': res['args']['ga_config']['runtime_seconds'],
                }
            )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(['seed', 'epoch']).reset_index(drop=True)


def compact(df: pd.DataFrame, every: int = 100) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Keep the first epoch, every epoch where ``max_fitness`` changes, every ``every``-th epoch and the last one."""
    keep = []
    for _, run in df.groupby('seed'):
        changed = run['max_fitness'].diff().fillna(1.0) != 0
        periodic = run['epoch'] % every == 0
        last = run['epoch'] == run['epoch'].max()
        keep.append(run[changed | periodic | last])
    return pd.concat(keep).reset_index(drop=True)


def per_seed(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Epochs completed, topologies evaluated, final best fitness and the first epoch at which it was reached."""
    rows = []
    for seed, run in df.groupby('seed'):
        final = run['max_fitness'].max()
        rows.append(
            {
                'seed': seed,
                'epochs': int(run['epoch'].max()) + 1,
                'total_branch_combis': int(run['total_branch_combis'].max()),
                'max_fitness': final,
                'first_epoch_at_max': int(run.loc[run['max_fitness'] >= final - 1e-9, 'epoch'].min()),
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index('seed')


def truncate(df: pd.DataFrame) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, int]:
    """Rows up to the smallest number of epochs any seed completed: an equal budget for every run."""
    common = int(df.groupby('seed')['epoch'].max().min())
    return df[df['epoch'] <= common].reset_index(drop=True), common


def plot_anytime(df: pd.DataFrame, x: str = 'epoch', ax=None) -> None:
    """Best fitness so far against ``x`` (``epoch`` or ``total_branch_combis``), one step line per seed."""
    ax = ax or plt.gca()
    for seed, run in df.groupby('seed'):
        ax.step(run[x], run['max_fitness'], where='post', label=f'seed {seed}')
    ax.set_xlabel(x.replace('_', ' '))
    ax.set_ylabel('best fitness')
    ax.legend()


def main() -> None:
    """Collect and compact a folder of runs into ``toop_seeds.csv`` (one argument), then summarize that table."""
    if len(sys.argv) == 2:
        compact(collect(Path(sys.argv[1]))).to_csv(EXAMPLE, index=False)
        print(f'wrote {EXAMPLE}')
    df = pd.read_csv(EXAMPLE)
    print(
        f'{df["seed"].nunique()} seeds, runtime {df["runtime_seconds"].iloc[0]:.0f} s each, {len(df)} compacted rows'
    )
    table = per_seed(df)
    print(table.to_string())
    spread = table['epochs'].max() / table['epochs'].min()
    print(f'epochs: min {table["epochs"].min()}, max {table["epochs"].max()} (ratio {spread:.2f})')
    cut, common = truncate(df)
    by_seed = cut.groupby('seed')
    print(
        f'equal budget: {common + 1} epochs, {int(by_seed["total_branch_combis"].max().min())} topologies; '
        f'best fitness per seed {by_seed["max_fitness"].max().round(3).tolist()}'
    )


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

    Exercise 1¶

    Three things wrong:

    1. The best of \(n\) is not a performance measure. The maximum of 20 draws grows with 20 and with the variance, so a method with a wider spread wins this comparison even when its median is worse; the baseline may have found a nearly identical topology in 19 of 20 runs and lost on the extreme value. The best run is one sample of the random process, and the paper reports neither the distribution it came from nor how the other 19 runs ended.
    2. No test, no effect size, no uncertainty. "1 of 20 against 0 of 20" is a difference of one event; the two-sided Fisher test on that table gives \(p = 1\), and no confidence interval is given. Nothing in the report lets a reader tell luck from an effect.
    3. The conditions are not stated. Which budget (evaluations or seconds, and on what hardware), which seeds, which instance(s), whether the 20 runs were all the runs made or the 20 kept, and whether "best topology" was defined before the runs. Without the configuration and the seeds the result cannot be reproduced; without the budget it cannot be compared; a wall-clock budget on two machines (Lab step 5) can produce the reported sentence on its own.

    A better table reports the whole distribution and the design, for example

    method runs budget median final \(E\) [MW] 25–75 % best worst runs at target \(\hat A_{12}\) against baseline \(p\) (rank-sum)
    baseline 20 1 152 evaluations, seeds 0–19 … … … … 0/20 0.5 –
    new method 20 same … … … … 1/20 … …

    with the anytime median curves and the ECDFs of the final values as figures, the instance, the configuration files, the seeds and the hardware listed, and the sentence about the best run, if kept at all, placed after the median. Where the instances are many, one line per instance and the Friedman test of Exercise 2.

    Exercise 2¶

    rank_tests.synthetic_instances builds the table: eight instances with a random difficulty between 20 and 60 MW, three configurations with noise \(\sigma = 3\) MW, five seeds each and the median per instance, configuration C shifted by \(-1\) MW (better). The observations are paired by instance, so the Friedman test ranks the three configurations within each instance and tests whether the mean ranks differ:

    A B C
    mean rank 2.12 2.12 1.75

    Friedman \(\chi^2 = 0.75\), \(p = 0.69\): no evidence of any difference. The post-hoc comparisons would normally stop here; running them anyway shows the correction at work. Wilcoxon signed-rank \(p\)-values of the three pairs, Holm step-down (sorted \(p_i\) compared with \(\alpha / (m - i + 1)\), adjusted \(p\) the running maximum of \((m - i + 1) p_i\)):

    pair \(p\) threshold Holm-adjusted \(p\) reject
    A vs C 0.1484 0.0167 0.4453 no
    B vs C 0.1953 0.0250 0.4453 no
    A vs B 0.9453 0.0500 0.9453 no

    A 1 MW advantage with 3 MW of noise, eight instances and five seeds per instance is invisible to this design (with 0.3 MW it is \(p = 0.88\)). The rank tests are honest about that: with eight paired observations the smallest possible two-sided signed-rank \(p\)-value is \(2 / 2^8 = 0.0078\), so after Holm's \(0.05 / 3\) a pair can only be rejected when C wins on every instance. More instances, not more seeds per instance, is what raises the power here, because the test counts instances.

    rank_tests.py
    """Friedman test with Holm-corrected post-hoc comparisons, and the power of the rank-sum test (Chapter 26).

Exercise 2: three configurations on eight instances (the median of five seeds per instance is the paired
observation), Friedman over the three columns, then Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the three pairs with the Holm
correction. Exercise 5: Monte Carlo estimate of the probability that the Mann-Whitney U test rejects at 5 % for
true median differences of 0, 1, 2 and 4 MW with a standard deviation of 3 MW and 5, 10 or 30 runs per
configuration.
"""

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from scipy import stats

CONFIGS = ('A', 'B', 'C')


def synthetic_instances(seed: int = 0, n_instances: int = 8, n_seeds: int = 5, advantage: float = 1.0) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Final overload energies per instance (rows) and configuration (columns), median over ``n_seeds`` runs.

    Instances differ in difficulty (a random offset per row); configuration C is better by ``advantage`` MW.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    difficulty = rng.uniform(20.0, 60.0, size=n_instances)
    shift = {'A': 0.0, 'B': 0.0, 'C': -advantage}
    table = {
        name: np.median(rng.normal(difficulty[:, None] + shift[name], 3.0, size=(n_instances, n_seeds)), axis=1)
        for name in CONFIGS
    }
    return pd.DataFrame(table, index=[f'instance {i + 1}' for i in range(n_instances)])


def friedman(table: pd.DataFrame) -> tuple[float, float, pd.Series]:
    """Friedman statistic, p-value and the mean rank of each configuration (rank 1 = best = lowest value)."""
    res = stats.friedmanchisquare(*(table[c].to_numpy() for c in table.columns))
    ranks = table.rank(axis=1).mean()
    return float(res.statistic), float(res.pvalue), ranks


def holm(pvalues: dict[str, float], alpha: float = 0.05) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Holm step-down correction: sorted p-values compared with alpha / (m - i); adjusted p = max of running values."""
    items = sorted(pvalues.items(), key=lambda kv: kv[1])
    m = len(items)
    adjusted, running = [], 0.0
    for i, (_, p) in enumerate(items):
        running = max(running, min(1.0, (m - i) * p))
        adjusted.append(running)
    return pd.DataFrame(
        {
            'p': [p for _, p in items],
            'threshold': [alpha / (m - i) for i in range(m)],
            'p_holm': adjusted,
            'reject': [p_adj <= alpha for p_adj in adjusted],
        },
        index=[name for name, _ in items],
    )


def pairwise_signed_rank(table: pd.DataFrame) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value of every pair of columns (paired by instance)."""
    out = {}
    cols = list(table.columns)
    for i, a in enumerate(cols):
        for b in cols[i + 1 :]:
            out[f'{a} vs {b}'] = float(stats.wilcoxon(table[a], table[b]).pvalue)
    return out


def power_rank_sum(
    delta: float, n: int, sd: float = 3.0, n_rep: int = 2000, seed: int = 0, alpha: float = 0.05
) -> float:
    """Fraction of ``n_rep`` simulated experiments with p < alpha when the true medians differ by ``delta``."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    a = rng.normal(0.0, sd, size=(n_rep, n))
    b = rng.normal(delta, sd, size=(n_rep, n))
    p = stats.mannwhitneyu(a, b, alternative='two-sided', axis=1).pvalue
    return float((p < alpha).mean())


def power_table(deltas=(0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0), sizes=(5, 10, 30), **kwargs) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Power (rows: true difference in MW, columns: runs per configuration)."""
    return pd.DataFrame(
        {n: [power_rank_sum(d, n, seed=i * 100 + n, **kwargs) for i, d in enumerate(deltas)] for n in sizes},
        index=[f'{d:.0f} MW' for d in deltas],
    )


def main() -> None:
    """Exercise 2 on synthetic data with configuration C 1 MW better, and the power table of Exercise 5."""
    table = synthetic_instances()
    print(table.round(2).to_string())
    statistic, p, ranks = friedman(table)
    print(f'Friedman chi2 = {statistic:.2f}, p = {p:.4f}; mean ranks {ranks.round(2).to_dict()}')
    pairs = pairwise_signed_rank(table)
    print(holm(pairs).round(4).to_string())
    weak = synthetic_instances(advantage=0.3)
    statistic, p, _ = friedman(weak)
    rejections = int(holm(pairwise_signed_rank(weak))['reject'].sum())
    print(f'with a 0.3 MW advantage: Friedman p = {p:.4f}; Holm rejections: {rejections}')
    print(power_table().round(3).to_string())
    print(f'2 MW with 40 runs per configuration: power {power_rank_sum(2.0, 40, seed=7):.3f}')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

    Exercise 3¶

    performance_profile.synthetic_times gives a seeded table of times to reach the target for 20 instances: the GA is fast but fails on 7 instances (NaN), MAP-Elites is 1.5 to 2.5 times slower than the base time and never fails, annealing is the fastest on the easy half and four times slower on the hard half. For solver \(s\) and instance \(p\) the ratio is \(r_{ps} = t_{ps} / \min_s t_{ps}\), \(+\infty\) for a failure, and

    \[ \rho_s(\tau) = \frac{1}{n_p}\,\bigl|\{p : r_{ps} \le \tau\}\bigr|, \]

    a failure never counting, not even for \(\tau = \infty\) (inf <= inf is true in NumPy and in Octave, so the code masks the infinite ratios explicitly).

    \(\tau\) GA MAP-Elites annealing
    1 0.30 0.20 0.50
    2 0.65 0.35 0.60
    3 0.65 0.60 0.70
    5 0.65 1.00 0.95
    10 0.65 1.00 1.00
    \(\infty\) 0.65 1.00 1.00

    \(\rho_s(1)\) is the fraction of instances on which \(s\) was the fastest (ties count for every tied solver): annealing wins half of the instances, the GA 30 %, MAP-Elites 20 %. For large \(\tau\) every solver reaches the fraction of instances it solved at all: 1.0 for MAP-Elites and annealing, 0.65 for the GA, whose seven failures are visible as the gap to 1 that no \(\tau\) closes. In between the curves cross: the GA is within a factor 2 of the best on 65 % of the instances, where MAP-Elites is at 35 %, but MAP-Elites reaches 100 % at \(\tau = 5\) (its largest finite ratio is 3.81) while annealing needs \(\tau = 10\) (largest ratio 5.18). A profile shows speed and robustness on one picture, and it rewards a solver for being reliably close to the best rather than for winning races; the plot of plot_profile is the step version of this table on a logarithmic \(\tau\) axis. Dolan and Moré published the original as MATLAB code; perf_profile.m is the Octave version, with two %!test blocks on a table small enough to check by hand.

    performance_profile.py
    """Dolan-More performance profiles for three solvers over twenty instances (Chapter 26, Exercise 3).

For solver s and instance p, the performance ratio is r_ps = t_ps / min_s t_ps, with r_ps = +inf when the solver
failed to reach the target; the profile rho_s(tau) is the fraction of instances with r_ps <= tau. rho_s(1) is the
fraction of instances on which s was the fastest, and rho_s(tau) for large tau tends to the fraction of instances
the solver solved at all.
"""

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

SOLVERS = ('GA', 'MAP-Elites', 'annealing')


def synthetic_times(seed: int = 0, n_instances: int = 20) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Time to reach the target (s) per instance (rows) and solver (columns); NaN marks a failure.

    The GA is fast but fails on a quarter of the instances, MAP-Elites is slower and never fails, and annealing is
    the fastest on easy instances but slow on hard ones.
    """
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    base = rng.lognormal(mean=1.0, sigma=0.6, size=n_instances)
    ga = base * rng.uniform(0.8, 1.2, size=n_instances)
    ga[rng.random(n_instances) < 0.25] = np.nan
    me = base * rng.uniform(1.5, 2.5, size=n_instances)
    sa = np.where(base < np.median(base), base * 0.6, base * 4.0) * rng.uniform(0.9, 1.1, size=n_instances)
    return pd.DataFrame({'GA': ga, 'MAP-Elites': me, 'annealing': sa}, index=[f'p{i + 1}' for i in range(n_instances)])


def ratios(times: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Performance ratios r_ps = t_ps / best time on the instance; failures become +inf."""
    best = times.min(axis=1)
    return times.div(best, axis=0).fillna(np.inf)


def profile(times: pd.DataFrame, taus: np.ndarray) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """rho_s(tau) for every solver (columns) and every tau in ``taus`` (index)."""
    r = ratios(times)
    solved = np.isfinite(r)  # a failure has an infinite ratio and never counts, not even for tau = inf
    return pd.DataFrame({s: [(solved[s] & (r[s] <= tau)).mean() for tau in taus] for s in times.columns}, index=taus)


def plot_profile(times: pd.DataFrame, tau_max: float | None = None, ax=None) -> None:
    """Step plot of rho_s(tau) on a log tau axis."""
    ax = ax or plt.gca()
    r = ratios(times)
    finite = r.to_numpy()[np.isfinite(r.to_numpy())]
    tau_max = tau_max or float(finite.max()) * 1.2
    taus = np.geomspace(1.0, tau_max, 200)
    rho = profile(times, taus)
    for s in times.columns:
        ax.step(taus, rho[s], where='post', label=s)
    ax.set_xscale('log')
    ax.set_xlabel(r'$\tau$')
    ax.set_ylabel(r'$\rho_s(\tau)$')
    ax.set_ylim(0, 1.02)
    ax.legend()


def main() -> None:
    """The synthetic table, rho_s(1), rho_s at a few tau and the limit for large tau."""
    times = synthetic_times()
    print(times.round(2).to_string())
    r = ratios(times)
    taus = np.array([1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, np.inf])
    rho = profile(times, taus)
    print(rho.round(2).to_string())
    print('failures per solver:', times.isna().sum().to_dict())
    print('largest finite ratio per solver:', r.replace(np.inf, np.nan).max().round(2).to_dict())


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()
    perf_profile.m
    function rho = perf_profile(T, taus)
  % Dolan-More performance profile (Chapter 26, Exercise 3).
  % T(p, s) is the time solver s needed on instance p, NaN (or Inf) for a failure; taus is a vector of
  % thresholds. rho(i, s) is the fraction of instances whose performance ratio T(p, s) / min_s T(p, s) is at
  % most taus(i). Failures get an infinite ratio and never count.
  T(isnan(T)) = Inf;
  best = min(T, [], 2);
  r = T ./ best;                       % performance ratios, Inf where the solver failed
  rho = zeros(numel(taus), size(T, 2));
  for i = 1:numel(taus)
    rho(i, :) = mean(isfinite(r) & r <= taus(i), 1);   % failures never count, not even for tau = Inf
  end
end

    Exercise 5¶

    rank_tests.power_rank_sum draws \(n\) values from \(\mathcal N(0, 3^2)\) and \(n\) from \(\mathcal N(\delta, 3^2)\), runs the two-sided Mann–Whitney test and repeats 2 000 times (seeded); the power is the fraction of repetitions with \(p < 0.05\):

    true difference \(n = 5\) \(n = 10\) \(n = 30\)
    0 MW 0.032 0.048 0.048
    1 MW 0.056 0.105 0.236
    2 MW 0.099 0.267 0.715
    4 MW 0.356 0.758 0.998

    The first row is the size of the test: about 5 % false positives as designed, slightly less for \(n = 5\) because the exact rank-sum distribution is discrete and cannot hit 0.05 exactly. A difference of one third of a standard deviation (1 MW) is hopeless with fewer than a hundred runs; two thirds (2 MW) needs about 30 runs per configuration for a 70 % chance of being seen; a 4 MW difference, more than one standard deviation, is caught three times in four with 10 runs and almost surely with 30. The ten seeds of the lab therefore only detect effects of about one standard deviation of the run-to-run noise, which is why Lab step 4 says "no difference shown" rather than "no difference". How many to schedule: decide first the smallest difference worth detecting (say 2 MW, the size of a small redispatch) and the power wanted (80 %), then read the table or extend it: about 40 runs per configuration for 2 MW, 10 for 4 MW. Scheduling fewer and then reporting a non-significant result as evidence of equality is the mistake Exercise 1 describes from the other side.
