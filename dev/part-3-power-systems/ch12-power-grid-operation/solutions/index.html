    Lab¶

    1. The SMARD article for Q1 2026. The article (read on 16 October 2026) reports the total volume of congestion management measures (redispatch with operational and reserve plants, and countertrading), their estimated cost, and the share of the redispatch volume that curtailed renewable plants. In a DataFrame indexed by quarter:

      quarter volume (GWh) cost (M€) curtailment of renewables (GWh) €/MWh
      Q1 2025 9,035 882 2,153 97.62
      Q1 2026 8,248 784 2,215 95.05

      The average cost is \(1000 \times \text{cost}_{\text{M€}} / \text{volume}_{\text{GWh}}\), about 98 €/MWh in Q1 2025 and 95 €/MWh in Q1 2026, a change of −2.57 €/MWh (−2.6 %). The volume fell by 787 GWh (−8.7 %) and the cost by 98 M€ (−11.1 %), so the unit cost fell slightly: fewer MWh were moved, and each one cost a little less. The article's breakdown shows where the money went (redispatch of conventional plants 423 → 282 M€, compensation of curtailed renewables 71 → 55 M€, reserve plants 355 → 430 M€, countertrading 33 → 17 M€; both columns add up to the totals). For scale: 95 €/MWh is above the marginal cost of most plants in the merit order, which is why a switching action that avoids a MWh of redispatch is worth having even when it costs an hour of an operator's attention.

      congestion_cost.py
      """Average cost of German congestion management per MWh from SMARD's quarterly figures (Chapter 12, Lab step 1).

The figures are those of the SMARD article "Congestion management in Q1 2026: lower volume of measures and costs"
(read on 16 October 2026): total volume of measures (redispatch with operational and reserve plants, and
countertrading), estimated total cost, and the part of the redispatch volume that curtailed renewable plants. The
cost breakdown of the same article is kept in a second table; its rows add up to the totals.
"""

import pandas as pd

QUARTERS = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        'quarter': ['Q1 2025', 'Q1 2026'],
        'volume_gwh': [9035.0, 8248.0],
        'cost_meur': [882.0, 784.0],
        'curtailment_gwh': [2153.0, 2215.0],
    }
).set_index('quarter')

COST_BREAKDOWN_MEUR = pd.DataFrame(
    {
        'Q1 2025': [423.0, 71.0, 355.0, 33.0],
        'Q1 2026': [282.0, 55.0, 430.0, 17.0],
    },
    index=[
        'redispatch, conventional plants',
        'compensation, curtailed renewables',
        'reserve plants',
        'countertrading',
    ],
)


def cost_per_mwh(table: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    """Average cost of congestion management in euros per MWh of measures: million euros over GWh, times 1000."""
    return 1000.0 * table['cost_meur'] / table['volume_gwh']


def changes(table: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Absolute and relative change of each column, unit cost included, between the first and the last quarter."""
    with_unit = table.assign(eur_per_mwh=cost_per_mwh(table))
    first, last = with_unit.iloc[0], with_unit.iloc[-1]
    return pd.DataFrame({'first': first, 'last': last, 'change': last - first, 'change_pct': 100 * (last / first - 1)})


def main() -> None:
    """Print the unit cost of each quarter, the changes between them and the cost breakdown."""
    pd.set_option('display.width', 119)
    print(QUARTERS.assign(eur_per_mwh=cost_per_mwh(QUARTERS)).round(2))
    print()
    print(changes(QUARTERS).round(2))
    print()
    print(
        COST_BREAKDOWN_MEUR.assign(
            change_pct=(100 * (COST_BREAKDOWN_MEUR['Q1 2026'] / COST_BREAKDOWN_MEUR['Q1 2025'] - 1))
        ).round(1)
    )
    print('breakdown totals:', COST_BREAKDOWN_MEUR.sum().to_dict())


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

    2. One winter week as CSV. The week is Monday 12 to Sunday 18 January 2026. The SMARD download center writes one CSV per table (generation by source; consumption), and the first two lines of each file show everything the parser needs: a semicolon as delimiter, a UTF-8 byte-order mark before the first header cell, two timestamp columns (Start date, End date) in Berlin local time, - where a value is missing (the whole Nuclear column, since April 2023), and every value column named <source> [MWh] Calculated resolutions. Dates and numbers follow the language of the portal: the English export writes Jan 12, 2026 12:00 AM and 53,862.40, the German one (Datum von, Netzlast [MWh] Berechnete Auflösungen) writes 12.01.2026 00:00 and 53.862,40. The parser for the English file is pandas.read_csv(path, sep=';', thousands=',', decimal='.', na_values=['-'], encoding='utf-8-sig') followed by pandas.to_datetime(..., format='%b %d, %Y %I:%M %p') on the start column; the German file needs thousands='.', decimal=',' and format='%d.%m.%Y %H:%M'. smard_week.read_smard picks the layout from the first header cell and returns English column names either way (the two real exports of this week were parsed with it and agree value for value); the load column of the consumption table is called grid load.

      The download center is a form, but the numbers behind the portal's charts come from a JSON API that needs no login, and smard_api.fetch_week uses it: index_hour.json lists the available weeks (Mondays 00:00 Berlin time, in milliseconds since the epoch; 616 weeks from 29 December 2014 on the fetch date) and one <filter>_DE_hour_<timestamp>.json per source gives the 168 hourly values in MWh. The filter ids are those of the Bundesnetzagentur's description of the API (410 grid load, 4359 residual load, 4387 pumped-storage consumption; 1223 lignite, 1224 nuclear, 1225 wind offshore, 1226 hydropower, 1227 other conventional, 1228 other renewable, 4066 biomass, 4067 wind onshore, 4068 photovoltaics, 4069 hard coal, 4070 pumped storage, 4071 natural gas); nuclear answers 404, which the module turns into the - column of the export. smard_api.save_week writes the two tables in the export layout: the saved files are identical to the download center's English export of the same week except for the byte-order mark and the line endings. SMARD revises recent data, so a later fetch can differ from the saved files in the last decimals.

      Numbers of the week: consumption from 45.7 GW (Sunday 18 January 03:00) to 75.8 GW (Monday 12 January 10:00); wind, offshore plus onshore, from 9.1 GW (Saturday 17 January 13:00) to 38.2 GW (Friday 16 January 04:00); photovoltaics up to 20.6 GW (Friday 16 January 12:00); wind and solar together supplied 37.7 % of the week's consumption. The residual load recomputed as consumption − wind offshore − wind onshore − photovoltaics matches SMARD's own Residual load column to 0.01 MWh, the rounding of the file, which is the check to run on any download. Plots (described, not saved): wind and consumption never cross in this week, but they move against each other, wind rising through the nights of Tuesday to Wednesday and Thursday to Friday while consumption falls to about 51.5 GW; the residual load runs from 60.5 GW (Wednesday 14 January 18:00, the evening peak with little wind) down to 13.5 GW (Friday 16 January 02:00). Its duration curve (duration_curve, the 168 hourly values sorted from largest to smallest) falls from 60.5 GW at hour 1 through 38.2 GW at the median hour 84 to 13.5 GW at hour 168: in a real January week the thermal fleet is never idle, unlike in the synthetic sample, whose right tail drops towards zero.

      smard_api.py
      """Fetch one week of hourly SMARD generation and consumption through the public JSON API (Chapter 12).

The download center of SMARD (https://www.smard.de) is a form, but the data behind its charts is a JSON API that
needs no login: ``.../chart_data/{filter}/{region}/index_hour.json`` lists the weeks available for a filter (ms
since the epoch, each a Monday 00:00 Berlin time) and ``.../chart_data/{filter}/{region}/{filter}_{region}_hour_
{timestamp}.json`` returns ``{"meta_data": ..., "series": [[timestamp, value], ...]}``, one value per hour of that
week in MWh. The filter ids are the ones documented by the Bundesnetzagentur (github.com/bundesAPI/smard-api) and
were checked against the data: the residual-load filter equals grid load minus wind and solar to 0.01 MWh, and the
values are those of the CSV export of the same week. A filter without data in the week (nuclear, since April 2023)
answers 404 and becomes a column of NaN, where the export prints ``-``.

``fetch_week`` returns the two tables with the columns of the CSV export, so every function of ``smard_week``
processes them unchanged; ``save_week`` writes them in the export layout next to this file, and ``load_week``
reads them back, so the lab runs offline on the saved week of 12–18 January 2026.
"""

import json
import urllib.error
import urllib.request
from pathlib import Path

import pandas as pd
from smard_week import LOAD, read_smard, report, to_smard_csv

HERE = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
BASE_URL = 'https://www.smard.de/app/chart_data'
REGION = 'DE'
TIMEOUT_S = 30
TIMEZONE = 'Europe/Berlin'
GENERATION_FILTERS = {
    'Biomass': 4066,
    'Hydropower': 1226,
    'Wind offshore': 1225,
    'Wind onshore': 4067,
    'Photovoltaics': 4068,
    'Other renewable': 1228,
    'Nuclear': 1224,
    'Lignite': 1223,
    'Hard coal': 4069,
    'Fossil gas': 4071,
    'Hydro pumped storage': 4070,
    'Other conventional': 1227,
}
CONSUMPTION_FILTERS = {LOAD: 410, 'Hydro pumped storage': 4387, 'Residual load': 4359}
LIVE_WEEK = pd.Timestamp('2026-01-12')  # the Monday of the week saved next to this file


class SmardUnavailableError(ConnectionError):
    """The SMARD API could not be reached: no network, a timeout or a server error."""


def fetch_json(url: str, timeout: float = TIMEOUT_S) -> dict | None:
    """The JSON document at ``url``, or ``None`` when SMARD answers 404 (no data for that filter and week)."""
    try:
        with urllib.request.urlopen(url, timeout=timeout) as response:  # noqa: S310 -- https URL under BASE_URL
            return json.load(response)
    except urllib.error.HTTPError as error:
        error.close()  # the error carries the response body, a file that would otherwise stay open
        if error.code == 404:
            return None
        raise SmardUnavailableError(f'SMARD answered HTTP {error.code} for {url}') from error
    # RuntimeError: in the browser (JupyterLite, Pyodide) there are no TLS sockets, and urlopen says so this way.
    except (urllib.error.URLError, TimeoutError, OSError, RuntimeError) as error:
        raise SmardUnavailableError(
            f'cannot reach SMARD ({error}); check the network connection, then retry'
        ) from error


def to_berlin(timestamps_ms) -> pd.DatetimeIndex:
    """Epoch milliseconds as naive Berlin local timestamps, the clock the SMARD exports use."""
    return pd.to_datetime(list(timestamps_ms), unit='ms', utc=True).tz_convert(TIMEZONE).tz_localize(None)


def week_timestamp(monday: pd.Timestamp) -> int:
    """Epoch milliseconds of ``monday`` 00:00 Berlin time, the key under which SMARD files that week."""
    if monday.weekday() != 0 or monday != monday.normalize():
        raise ValueError(f'{monday} is not a Monday at 00:00')
    return int(monday.tz_localize(TIMEZONE).timestamp() * 1000)


def available_weeks(filter_id: int = 410, region: str = REGION) -> pd.DatetimeIndex:
    """The Mondays of every week SMARD has for a filter, oldest first."""
    index = fetch_json(f'{BASE_URL}/{filter_id}/{region}/index_hour.json')
    if index is None:
        raise SmardUnavailableError(f'SMARD has no hourly index for filter {filter_id} in {region}')
    return to_berlin(index['timestamps'])


def fetch_series(filter_id: int, monday: pd.Timestamp, region: str = REGION) -> pd.Series | None:
    """The hourly values (MWh) of one filter for the week of ``monday``, or ``None`` when SMARD has none."""
    stamp = week_timestamp(monday)
    data = fetch_json(f'{BASE_URL}/{filter_id}/{region}/{filter_id}_{region}_hour_{stamp}.json')
    if data is None:
        return None
    timestamps, values = zip(*data['series'], strict=True)
    return pd.Series(values, index=to_berlin(timestamps), dtype=float)


def fetch_table(filters: dict[str, int], monday: pd.Timestamp, region: str = REGION) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """One export table: a column per filter, in the given order, NaN for filters without data."""
    series = {name: fetch_series(filter_id, monday, region) for name, filter_id in filters.items()}
    table = pd.DataFrame({name: s for name, s in series.items() if s is not None}).reindex(columns=list(filters))
    table.index.name = 'start'
    return table


def fetch_week(monday: pd.Timestamp = LIVE_WEEK, region: str = REGION) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """Generation by source and consumption for the week of ``monday``, in the layout of the SMARD exports."""
    return fetch_table(GENERATION_FILTERS, monday, region), fetch_table(CONSUMPTION_FILTERS, monday, region)


def live_paths(monday: pd.Timestamp = LIVE_WEEK, folder: Path = HERE) -> tuple[Path, Path]:
    """Where the generation and consumption tables of the week of ``monday`` are saved."""
    day = monday.strftime('%Y-%m-%d')
    return folder / f'smard_live_week_{day}_generation.csv', folder / f'smard_live_week_{day}_consumption.csv'


def save_week(gen: pd.DataFrame, cons: pd.DataFrame, folder: Path = HERE) -> tuple[Path, Path]:
    """Write the two tables in the export layout (``smard_week.to_smard_csv``) and return their paths."""
    paths = live_paths(gen.index[0], folder)
    for path, table in zip(paths, (gen, cons), strict=True):
        path.write_text(to_smard_csv(table), encoding='utf-8', newline='\n')
    return paths


def load_week(monday: pd.Timestamp = LIVE_WEEK, folder: Path = HERE) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """The saved tables of the week of ``monday``, read with ``smard_week.read_smard``."""
    gen_path, cons_path = live_paths(monday, folder)
    return read_smard(gen_path), read_smard(cons_path)


def live_or_saved(monday: pd.Timestamp = LIVE_WEEK) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, str]:
    """The week from the API when SMARD answers, otherwise the saved copy; the third item says which."""
    try:
        gen, cons = fetch_week(monday)
    except SmardUnavailableError as error:
        print(f'{error}; using the saved week')
        gen, cons = load_week(monday)
        return gen, cons, 'saved'
    return gen, cons, 'api'


def main() -> None:
    """Fetch the live week, save it next to this file and print the numbers quoted in the solutions."""
    gen, cons = fetch_week(LIVE_WEEK)
    weeks = available_weeks()
    print(f'SMARD has {len(weeks)} weeks of hourly data, {weeks[0].date()} to {weeks[-1].date()}')
    print('no data (NaN):', [c for c in gen.columns if gen[c].isna().all()])
    gen_path, cons_path = save_week(gen, cons)
    print('saved', gen_path.name, 'and', cons_path.name)
    saved_gen, saved_cons = load_week(LIVE_WEEK)
    print('saved files reproduce the API week:', saved_gen.equals(gen.round(2)) and saved_cons.equals(cons.round(2)))
    report(gen, cons)


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

      smard_week.py
      """Parse a week of SMARD generation and consumption exports and derive the residual load (Chapter 12).

The SMARD download center writes one CSV per table (generation by source; consumption), with a semicolon as
delimiter, a UTF-8 byte-order mark, two timestamp columns for the start and the end of each interval, ``-`` where
a value is missing (nuclear, since April 2023) and every value column named ``<source> [MWh] Calculated
resolutions``. Dates and numbers follow the language of the portal: the English export writes
``Jan 12, 2026 12:00 AM`` and ``12,345.67``; the German export (``Datum von``, German column names, suffix
``Berechnete Auflösungen``) writes ``12.01.2026 00:00`` and ``12.345,67``. ``read_smard`` tells the two apart by the
first header cell and returns the English short names either way; both layouts were checked against the real
exports of the week of 12 January 2026. ``to_smard_csv`` writes the English layout.

The committed samples ``smard_generation_sample.csv`` and ``smard_consumption_sample.csv`` are synthetic:
``write_samples`` builds a plausible winter week (a weekday daily cycle, a lower weekend, a wind front passing
mid-week, short solar days) in exactly this layout, so that the parsing code can be exercised and tested without a
download. ``smard_api`` fetches a real week through the SMARD JSON API and saves it in the same layout.
"""

from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

HERE = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
GENERATION_CSV = HERE / 'smard_generation_sample.csv'
CONSUMPTION_CSV = HERE / 'smard_consumption_sample.csv'
WEEK_START = pd.Timestamp('2026-01-12 00:00')  # a Monday
HOURS = 7 * 24
SUFFIX = ' [MWh] Calculated resolutions'
GENERATION_COLUMNS = [
    'Biomass',
    'Hydropower',
    'Wind offshore',
    'Wind onshore',
    'Photovoltaics',
    'Other renewable',
    'Nuclear',
    'Lignite',
    'Hard coal',
    'Fossil gas',
    'Hydro pumped storage',
    'Other conventional',
]
LOAD = 'grid load'  # the consumption table's name for the total load, in the order of the export
CONSUMPTION_COLUMNS = [LOAD, 'Hydro pumped storage', 'Residual load']
RENEWABLE_VARIABLE = ['Wind offshore', 'Wind onshore', 'Photovoltaics']
GERMAN_NAMES = {
    'Biomasse': 'Biomass',
    'Wasserkraft': 'Hydropower',
    'Wind Offshore': 'Wind offshore',
    'Wind Onshore': 'Wind onshore',
    'Photovoltaik': 'Photovoltaics',
    'Sonstige Erneuerbare': 'Other renewable',
    'Kernenergie': 'Nuclear',
    'Braunkohle': 'Lignite',
    'Steinkohle': 'Hard coal',
    'Erdgas': 'Fossil gas',
    'Pumpspeicher': 'Hydro pumped storage',
    'Sonstige Konventionelle': 'Other conventional',
    'Netzlast': LOAD,
    'Residuallast': 'Residual load',
}
# The two languages of the portal, keyed by the first header cell: the end column, the date format, the thousands
# and decimal marks, the suffix of every value column and the column names to translate.
LAYOUTS = {
    'Start date': ('End date', '%b %d, %Y %I:%M %p', ',', '.', SUFFIX, {}),
    'Datum von': ('Datum bis', '%d.%m.%Y %H:%M', '.', ',', ' [MWh] Berechnete Auflösungen', GERMAN_NAMES),
}


def synthetic_week() -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """Generation by source and consumption, hourly MWh, for one synthetic winter week.

    The shapes are deterministic formulas of the hour of the week: consumption has a daytime hump and an evening
    peak, scaled down at the weekend; onshore wind is a bell-shaped front centred on Wednesday night on top of a
    slow swell; offshore wind follows onshore at a smaller scale; photovoltaics is a short daytime arc whose
    height changes from day to day; the thermal fleet covers what is left above a must-run floor.
    """
    t = np.arange(HOURS)
    hour, day = t % 24, t // 24
    weekend = np.where(day >= 5, 0.86, 1.0)
    load = weekend * (
        47000 + 13000 * np.exp(-(((hour - 12) / 5.0) ** 2)) + 4000 * np.exp(-(((hour - 18.5) / 2.0) ** 2))
    )
    wind_on = np.maximum(500.0, 8000 + 30000 * np.exp(-(((t - 80) / 30.0) ** 2)) + 6000 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * t / 40))
    wind_off = np.minimum(9000.0, 1500 + 0.22 * wind_on)
    solar_day = np.array([0.6, 1.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9, 0.7, 0.4])[day]
    solar = 11000 * solar_day * np.maximum(0.0, np.sin(np.pi * (hour - 8) / 9.0)) * ((hour >= 8) & (hour <= 17))
    biomass, hydro, other_ren = np.full(HOURS, 4800.0), np.full(HOURS, 1600.0), np.full(HOURS, 150.0)
    residual = load - wind_on - wind_off - solar
    pumped = np.maximum(0.0, residual - 45000) * 0.15
    thermal = np.maximum(residual - pumped - biomass - hydro - other_ren, 7000.0)
    gen = pd.DataFrame(
        {
            'Biomass': biomass,
            'Hydropower': hydro,
            'Wind offshore': wind_off,
            'Wind onshore': wind_on,
            'Photovoltaics': solar,
            'Other renewable': other_ren,
            'Nuclear': np.full(HOURS, np.nan),  # no nuclear generation in Germany since April 2023: SMARD prints '-'
            'Lignite': 0.45 * thermal,
            'Hard coal': 0.20 * thermal,
            'Fossil gas': 0.30 * thermal,
            'Hydro pumped storage': pumped,
            'Other conventional': 0.05 * thermal,
        },
        index=pd.date_range(WEEK_START, periods=HOURS, freq='h'),
    )
    cons = pd.DataFrame({LOAD: load, 'Hydro pumped storage': 1.1 * pumped, 'Residual load': residual}, index=gen.index)
    return gen, cons


def english_stamp(t: pd.Timestamp) -> str:
    """A timestamp as the English export writes it, ``Jan 5, 2026 1:00 AM``: day and hour without a leading zero."""
    return f'{t:%b} {t.day}, {t.year} {t.hour % 12 or 12}:{t.minute:02d} {"AM" if t.hour < 12 else "PM"}'


def to_smard_csv(table: pd.DataFrame) -> str:
    """Serialize a table in the layout of the English SMARD export: start and end columns, ``-`` for NaN."""
    lines = ['Start date;End date;' + ';'.join(f'{c}{SUFFIX}' for c in table.columns)]
    for start, row in zip(table.index, table.itertuples(index=False), strict=True):
        cells = ['-' if np.isnan(v) else f'{v:,.2f}' for v in row]
        lines.append(';'.join([english_stamp(start), english_stamp(start + pd.Timedelta(hours=1)), *cells]))
    return '\n'.join(lines) + '\n'


def write_samples(folder: Path = HERE) -> tuple[Path, Path]:
    """Write the two sample CSV files into ``folder`` and return their paths."""
    gen, cons = synthetic_week()
    paths = (folder / GENERATION_CSV.name, folder / CONSUMPTION_CSV.name)
    for path, table in zip(paths, (gen, cons), strict=True):
        path.write_text(to_smard_csv(table), encoding='utf-8', newline='\n')
    return paths


def read_smard(path: Path) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Read a SMARD CSV export (English or German) into a frame indexed by the start of each interval.

    The first header cell selects the layout (``Start date`` or ``Datum von``); the byte-order mark of a real
    download is skipped, ``-`` becomes NaN, the end column is dropped, the ``[MWh] ...`` suffix is removed and
    German column names are translated, so the result has the same short English names whatever the language.
    """
    with open(path, encoding='utf-8-sig') as file:
        first_cell = file.readline().split(';', 1)[0]
    if first_cell not in LAYOUTS:
        raise ValueError(f'{path}: not a SMARD export, the header starts with {first_cell!r}')
    end, date_format, thousands, decimal, suffix, names = LAYOUTS[first_cell]
    table = pd.read_csv(path, sep=';', thousands=thousands, decimal=decimal, na_values=['-'], encoding='utf-8-sig')
    table.index = pd.to_datetime(table.pop(first_cell), format=date_format)
    table.index.name = 'start'
    table = table.drop(columns=[end])
    table.columns = [names.get(c.removesuffix(suffix), c.removesuffix(suffix)) for c in table.columns]
    return table


def residual_load(gen: pd.DataFrame, cons: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    """Consumption minus wind (onshore and offshore) and photovoltaics, hour by hour."""
    return (cons[LOAD] - gen[RENEWABLE_VARIABLE].sum(axis=1)).rename('residual')


def duration_curve(series: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    """The values of a series sorted from largest to smallest, indexed by the number of hours at or above each."""
    values = np.sort(series.to_numpy())[::-1]
    return pd.Series(values, index=np.arange(1, len(values) + 1), name=series.name)


def critical_hours(gen: pd.DataFrame, cons: pd.DataFrame, q: float = 0.75) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Hours with wind above its ``q`` quantile and residual load below its ``1 - q`` quantile.

    These are the hours in which the north–south corridors of the German grid congest: much wind in the north,
    little demand left for the thermal plants of the south.
    """
    wind = gen[['Wind offshore', 'Wind onshore']].sum(axis=1).rename('wind')
    residual = residual_load(gen, cons)
    mask = (wind >= wind.quantile(q)) & (residual <= residual.quantile(1 - q))
    return pd.DataFrame({'wind': wind, 'consumption': cons[LOAD], 'residual': residual})[mask]


def daily_summary(gen: pd.DataFrame, cons: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Per day: share of wind and solar in consumption, maximum hourly ramp of the residual load, its minimum."""
    renewable = gen[RENEWABLE_VARIABLE].sum(axis=1)
    load = cons[LOAD]
    residual = residual_load(gen, cons)
    by_day = pd.Grouper(freq='D')
    return pd.DataFrame(
        {
            'weekday': load.groupby(by_day).first().index.day_name(),
            'share_pct': 100 * renewable.groupby(by_day).sum() / load.groupby(by_day).sum(),
            'max_ramp_mw_h': residual.diff().abs().groupby(by_day).max(),
            'min_residual_mw': residual.groupby(by_day).min(),
        }
    )

High wind, low residual load. critical_hours marks the hours whose wind is in the top quarter of the week (at or above 25.5 GW) and whose residual load is in the bottom quarter (at or below 31.0 GW): 19 hours in the week of 12 January 2026, all at night, in three runs: Monday 12 January 05:00; Tuesday 13 January 22:00 to Wednesday 14 January 05:00 (8 hours); and Thursday 15 January 21:00 to Friday 16 January 06:00 (10 hours). The extreme is Friday 02:00, with 38.1 GW of wind against 51.5 GW of consumption and 13.5 GW of residual load. The pattern is that of a windy January week: the wind blows day and night, but demand drops at night, so the hours of least residual load are the small hours.

      Why these hours congest the north–south corridors: German wind capacity, offshore and onshore, sits in the north, while the large loads and much of the remaining thermal and hydro capacity sit in the south and west. When wind is high the northern injections exceed the northern load, and the surplus has to cross the few 380 kV corridors to the south. When at the same time the residual load is low, the southern thermal plants that would otherwise feed the south are out of the market (prices are low), so the south imports even more. The corridor flows then reach their limits; the market result is physically infeasible. Operators try the cheapest remedies first: switching actions (busbar splitting, line switching) and phase-shifter taps, which redirect flow at no fuel cost; then redispatch (curtail northern wind or conventional plants, start southern plants, paid at cost) and countertrading with neighbours; renewable curtailment is in principle the last step but, as the Q1 figures show (2,215 GWh in one quarter), it is routine in these hours.

    Exercise 1¶

    (a) Nodal pricing. The cheap generator at A can send at most 500 MW over the line, so it is dispatched at 500 MW; the remaining 300 MW of the 800 MW load are generated at B. The price at each node is the cost of the generator that would serve one more MW there: 20 €/MWh at A (its generator has 500 MW spare but cannot reach B) and 60 €/MWh at B. The congestion rent is the price difference times the flow:

    \[ R = (\lambda_B - \lambda_A)\, F = (60 - 20) \times 500 = 20{,}000 \text{ €/h}. \]

    It is what the load pays (800 × 60 = 48,000 €/h) minus what the generators receive (500 × 20 + 300 × 60 = 28,000 €/h), and accrues to the owner of the line (or to the holders of its financial transmission rights).

    (b) Zonal market. Without the limit the merit order dispatches the cheap generator for the whole load, 800 MW from A, 0 from B, at a single price of 20 €/MWh. The line would carry 800 MW, 300 MW too many. The operator buys 300 MW down at A and 300 MW up at B; in cost-based redispatch it pays B's cost and recovers A's saved fuel, a net cost of

    \[ 300 \times (60 - 20) = 12{,}000 \text{ €/h}, \]

    which falls on the grid tariff instead of on the market price. The final physical dispatch (500, 300) is the same as in (a); what differs is who pays: in the nodal market the load at B pays 60 €/MWh and the rent goes to the grid, in the zonal market the load pays 20 €/MWh and the grid pays 12,000 €/h. two_node_market.nodal() and two_node_market.zonal() reproduce these numbers.

    two_node_market.py
    """Nodal and zonal clearing of a two-node market with one line limit (Chapter 12, Exercises 1 and 2).

Node A has a cheap generator, node B an expensive one and the load. Under nodal pricing the line limit is part of
the market clearing; under zonal pricing it is ignored and the operator restores it afterwards by redispatch.
Costs are in euros per MWh, powers in MW, so every money figure is in euros per hour.
"""

from dataclasses import dataclass

import numpy as np

COST_A, COST_B = 20.0, 60.0  # marginal costs in euros per MWh
CAP_A, CAP_B = 1000.0, 1000.0  # generator capacities in MW
LOAD_B, LIMIT = 800.0, 500.0  # load at node B and line limit of the exercise, MW


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Nodal:
    """Result of a nodal market clearing: dispatch, nodal prices, flow and congestion rent."""

    gen_a: float
    gen_b: float
    price_a: float
    price_b: float
    flow: float
    rent: float


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Zonal:
    """Result of a zonal clearing followed by redispatch: dispatch and price, then the redispatch and its cost."""

    gen_a: float
    gen_b: float
    price: float
    redispatch_mw: float
    redispatch_cost: float


def nodal(limit: float = LIMIT, load: float = LOAD_B) -> Nodal:
    """Clear the two-node market with the line limit enforced.

    The cheap generator at A serves as much of the load as the line (and its own capacity) allows; the rest comes
    from B. The price at B is the cost of the last generator serving B, the price at A the cost of generator A
    while it has spare capacity. The congestion rent is the price difference times the flow.

    Args:
        limit: Transfer limit of the line A–B in MW.
        load: Load at node B in MW; it must not exceed the generation reachable at B.
    """
    flow = min(load, CAP_A, limit)
    gen_b = load - flow
    if gen_b > CAP_B:
        raise ValueError('the load at B cannot be served')
    price_b = COST_B if gen_b > 0 else COST_A
    price_a = COST_A if flow < CAP_A else price_b
    return Nodal(flow, gen_b, price_a, price_b, flow, (price_b - price_a) * flow)


def zonal(limit: float = LIMIT, load: float = LOAD_B) -> Zonal:
    """Clear the market as one zone, then redispatch to bring the line flow back within its limit.

    The zonal clearing stacks the generators by cost and ignores the line. The flow it implies is all of A's
    output; whatever exceeds the limit is moved from A to B, and the operator pays the cost difference for each
    MW moved (cost-based redispatch).
    """
    gen_a = min(load, CAP_A)
    gen_b = load - gen_a
    price = COST_B if gen_b > 0 else COST_A
    moved = max(0.0, gen_a - limit)
    return Zonal(gen_a, gen_b, price, moved, moved * (COST_B - COST_A))


def sweep(limits: np.ndarray, load: float = LOAD_B) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Redispatch cost and congestion rent for each line limit, the two curves of Exercise 2."""
    cost = np.array([zonal(lim, load).redispatch_cost for lim in limits])
    rent = np.array([nodal(lim, load).rent for lim in limits])
    return cost, rent


def main() -> None:
    """Print the solution of Exercise 1 and the kink of Exercise 2."""
    n = nodal()
    print(f'nodal   : A {n.gen_a:.0f} MW at {n.price_a:.0f} EUR/MWh, B {n.gen_b:.0f} MW at {n.price_b:.0f} EUR/MWh')
    print(f'          flow {n.flow:.0f} MW, congestion rent {n.rent:,.0f} EUR/h')
    z = zonal()
    print(f'zonal   : A {z.gen_a:.0f} MW, B {z.gen_b:.0f} MW, one price {z.price:.0f} EUR/MWh')
    print(f'          redispatch {z.redispatch_mw:.0f} MW, cost {z.redispatch_cost:,.0f} EUR/h')
    limits = np.array([0.0, 200.0, 400.0, 500.0, 600.0, 799.0, 800.0, 1000.0])
    cost, rent = sweep(limits)
    print('limit MW        ' + ' '.join(f'{v:8.0f}' for v in limits))
    print('redispatch EUR/h' + ' '.join(f'{v:8.0f}' for v in cost))
    print('rent EUR/h      ' + ' '.join(f'{v:8.0f}' for v in rent))

    Exercise 2¶

    GeoGebra construction not run here; the same functions are evaluated in Python by two_node_market.sweep, and the GeoGebra sliders only change the two parameters \(L\) (line limit) and \(D\) (load at B). With \(c_A = 20\), \(c_B = 60\) and the generator at A large enough,

    \[ \text{redispatch cost}(L) = (c_B - c_A)\,\max(0,\, D - L), \qquad \text{rent}(L) = \begin{cases} (c_B - c_A)\, L & L < D \\ 0 & L \ge D \end{cases} \]
    limit (MW) 0 200 400 500 600 799 800 1,000
    redispatch cost (€/h) 32,000 24,000 16,000 12,000 8,000 40 0 0
    congestion rent (€/h) 0 8,000 16,000 20,000 24,000 31,960 0 0

    The kink is at \(L = D = 800\) MW. Below it the line is congested: every extra MW of capacity saves 40 €/h of redispatch, so the cost falls with slope \(-(c_B - c_A)\), and the rent grows with slope \(+(c_B - c_A)\) because one more MW flows at the full 40 €/MWh price difference. At \(L = D\) the congestion disappears: the redispatch cost reaches zero and stays there (a kink, continuous but with a slope change from −40 to 0), while the rent does not decline gradually but jumps from 31,960 €/h to 0, because the price difference itself collapses from 40 €/MWh to zero the moment the constraint stops binding. Moving the load slider moves the kink: it is always at the load (or at the capacity of A, if that is smaller). In GeoGebra, define L and D as sliders, cost(x) = 40 max(0, D − x), rent(x) = If(x < D, 40 x, 0), and watch the kink follow D.

    The planning timeline, from two days ahead (D-2) to real time, with the time a topology optimization has at each stage:

    flowchart LR
    A["D-2<br/>two-day-ahead congestion forecast<br/>(D2CF): expected grid, outages, forecasts"]
    B["D-1 ~12:00<br/>day-ahead market coupling clears<br/>(zonal prices, scheduled exchanges)"]
    C["D-1 afternoon/evening<br/>DACF: common grid model for every hour of D<br/>N-1 analysis, remedial actions chosen"]
    D["D, every hour<br/>intraday congestion forecast (IDCF):<br/>updated forecasts and schedules, actions revised"]
    E["real time<br/>state estimation every few seconds<br/>only pre-validated actions, operator decides"]
    A --> B --> C --> D --> E
    Stage When What is fixed What can still change Time for a ToOp run
    D2CF D-2 planned outages, load and renewable forecasts everything about the dispatch hours; results are indicative
    day-ahead market D-1, around noon zonal dispatch and exchanges the grid topology and remedial actions –
    DACF D-1 afternoon to evening one grid model per hour of D, N-1 results topology (bus splits, line switching), PST taps, then redispatch an hour or more per batch of 24 hourly models: this is where ToOp runs
    IDCF D, hourly forecasts updated with the latest measurements the same actions, re-checked tens of minutes per update
    real time D, continuously the actual state (state estimator) switching actions already validated, redispatch activation none for optimization; seconds for the operator to act

    A ToOp run belongs to the DACF and to the intraday updates: it needs a converged grid model (the common grid model built after the market result) and an N-1 analysis to improve, and its output, a list of proposed splittings with their security metrics and switching distances, must be validated (AC check, operator judgement) before it can be executed. At D-1 evening the engine has hours for 24 hourly cases; intraday it must answer in tens of minutes; in real time nothing is optimized any more, the operator only applies actions that were already studied. This is why the chapters on the solver stress speed per topology (Chapter 31): the budget is set by the timeline, not by the algorithm.

    Exercise 5¶

    Ranking from cheapest to most expensive, and from fastest to slowest to activate; the two orders are nearly reversed, which is the whole point of putting topology first in the remedial-action list:

    Remedial action Cost per MW relieved Speed of activation Market participant involved
    Busbar splitting, line switching lowest: no fuel, only switching wear and operator time; the risk is a worse N-1 state afterwards minutes (remote switching), but only after an N-1 check of the new topology, so hours of preparation in the DACF no: entirely within the TSO
    Phase-shifter tap change very low: no fuel, mechanical wear of the tap changer one tap every few seconds to minutes; also coordinated with neighbours when the PST sits on a border no (TSO, or two TSOs for a border PST)
    Redispatch (conventional plants) high: the cost difference between the plants moved, about 95 €/MWh on average in Q1 2026 (Lab step 1) from a quarter of an hour (gas, hydro) to several hours (coal start-up); it is planned day-ahead and intraday yes: the plant operators, compensated at cost
    Countertrading high: the price difference between the two zones, paid on the intraday market roughly the intraday gate closure, an hour or less yes: traders in both zones
    Renewable curtailment high per MWh and the most visible: lost zero-cost energy, compensation of the plant owner (55 M€ for 2,215 GWh in Q1 2026) fast once instructed (minutes), but legally the last resort after conventional measures yes: the renewable plant operators, compensated

    The German figures of Lab step 1 show the asymmetry: topology changes and PST taps cost almost nothing per MWh and do not appear in the cost table at all, while the three market-based measures cost 784 M€ in a quarter. That is why an engine that finds a switching action for a congestion, instead of paying for redispatch, pays for itself after a few hours of operation.
