    Chapter 19 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 19. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: metrics.py (the DC metrics in NumPy), security57_toop.py (Lab Part A, ToOp), ac_n1_toop.py (Lab Part B, ToOp), backend_compare.py (Lab Part B, step 5), case7.py (SciPy and pandapower) and case7_toop.py (ToOp), metric_design.py (Exercises 1–4 and 6) and matpower_n1_metrics.m (Exercise 5, Octave + MATPOWER). Each module's main() prints the numbers quoted below; the ToOp numbers were produced with ToOp ba1874e on CPU, the Octave ones with Octave 11.3 and MATPOWER 8.1. python case7.py prints every number of the Part C answers and of Exercise 10 (a) and (c); python case7_toop.py <folder> prints those of Exercises 7–10 (b). The notebooks examples.ipynb, lab.ipynb (Part C), lab_toop.ipynb (Parts A and B) and exercises.ipynb import these modules.

    Lab, Part A: DC metrics from the full N-1 matrix¶

    1. Load IEEE 57. case57_data_powsybl(folder) writes ToOp's IEEE 57 example in PowSyBl format and load_grid(DirFileSystem(folder), pandapower=False, parameters=PreprocessParameters(action_set_filter_bsdf_lodf=False)) preprocesses it. stats reports 72 nodes (57 buses plus the auxiliary nodes of the relevant stations), 82 branches, 81 branch outages + 3 injection outages = 84 N-1 cases, 12 relevant substations, 127 actions and has_double_limits=True. run_initial_loadflow returns the unsplit (overload_energy_n_0, overload_energy_n_1) = (454.2859, 3249.7961) MW: this example is heavily overloaded on purpose.
    2. Full matrices. default_topology(solver_config, batch_size=4) gives four unsplit topologies (every action slot holds the padding index). With passthrough(lf) = (lf.n_0_matrix, lf.n_1_matrix) defined once at module level, run_solver_symmetric(topologies, None, None, dynamic_information, solver_config, passthrough) returns n_0 of shape (4, 1, 82) and n_1 of shape (4, 1, 84, 82): topology, timestep, N-1 case, monitored branch; all 4 × 84 success flags are True. Defining the function inside a loop would make JAX retrace and recompile the solver on every call.
    3. Energies in NumPy. branch_limits.overload_weight is 2.0 on the 64 lines and 1.0 on the 18 transformers, and max_mw_flow_n_1 is exactly twice max_mw_flow for every branch. With metrics.overload_energy_n1(n_1[0], max_mw_flow_n_1, weight) and the N-0 vector as a one-case matrix, NumPy gives 454.285915469 and 3249.796127648 MW; the differences to ToOp are \(1 \cdot 10^{-13}\) and \(4 \cdot 10^{-13}\) MW (test_security57_toop.py asserts \(10^{-6}\)).
    4. Double limits. metrics.double_limits (90 % of the rating below 90 % loading, the flow itself between 90 % and 100 %, the rating above) reproduces max_mw_flow_limited and max_mw_flow_n_1_limited exactly (largest difference 0.0). For the N-1 limits, 26 branches are in the lower regime (ratio limited/rating 0.9), 8 in the dead zone (ratio equal to their loading) and 48 above 100 % (ratio 1). Plotted with the relative loading on the x axis and limited/rating on the y axis, the points lie on a horizontal line at 0.9 up to \(x = 0.9\), on the diagonal \(y = x\) between 0.9 and 1.0, and on a horizontal line at 1.0 beyond: the three regimes of compute_double_limits.

    5. Real actions. topologies.action.at[1, 0].set(1).at[2, 0].set(5), rebuilt as ActionIndexComputations(action=..., pad_mask=topologies.pad_mask), applies action 1 to the second topology and action 5 to the third. For the four topologies ToOp gives, through functools.partial(aggregate_to_metric, branch_limits=..., reassignment_distance=..., n_relevant_subs=..., metric=...) (one partial per metric, each built once):

      Metric unsplit action 1 action 5 unsplit
      overload_energy_n_0 454.29 459.77 468.13 454.29
      overload_energy_n_1 3249.80 3465.16 3386.51 3249.80
      exponential_overload_energy_n_1 6841.80 6994.97 7171.36 6841.80
      critical_branch_count_n_1 48 53 49 48
      n0_n1_delta 0 0 0 0
      overload_energy_limited_n_0 454.29 461.24 469.00 454.29
      overload_energy_limited_n_1 3249.80 3496.37 3403.98 3249.80

      numpy_metrics reproduces all seven columns to \(10^{-12}\). Both actions make this grid worse. n0_n1_delta is zero because the case57 generator writes no *_n0_n1_max_diff_factor.npy masks, so every factor is \(-1\) and the penalty is disabled for every branch (compute_n0_n1_max_diff stores \(-1\), get_n0_n1_delta_penalty zeroes those branches).

      security57_toop.py
      """Lab Part A of Chapter 19 with ToOp: the DC security metrics of IEEE 57 recomputed in NumPy from the full matrices.

Run it with ToOp's interpreter (its container or virtual environment) from this folder; ``metrics.py`` must be
next to it::

    python security57_toop.py /tmp/case57

It writes ToOp's IEEE 57 example (``case57_data_powsybl``), preprocesses it, obtains the full N-0 and N-1 flow
matrices with a pass-through aggregation function and checks every metric of the chapter against ToOp's own
``aggregate_to_metric``. ToOp's flows and limits are in MW; ``overload_weight`` is 2.0 on the lines of this case.
"""

import functools
import sys
from pathlib import Path

import jax
import metrics
import numpy as np
from fsspec.implementations.dirfs import DirFileSystem
from toop_engine_dc_solver.example_grids import case57_data_powsybl
from toop_engine_dc_solver.jax import default_topology, run_solver_symmetric
from toop_engine_dc_solver.jax.aggregate_results import aggregate_to_metric
from toop_engine_dc_solver.jax.types import ActionIndexComputations
from toop_engine_dc_solver.preprocess.convert_to_jax import load_grid, run_initial_loadflow
from toop_engine_interfaces.messages.preprocess.preprocess_commands import PreprocessParameters

jax.config.update('jax_enable_x64', True)

METRICS = (
    'overload_energy_n_0',
    'overload_energy_n_1',
    'exponential_overload_energy_n_1',
    'critical_branch_count_n_1',
    'n0_n1_delta',
    'overload_energy_limited_n_0',
    'overload_energy_limited_n_1',
)


def load(folder: Path):
    """Step 1: write the case, preprocess it and run the initial load flow; returns stats, static info, metrics."""
    case57_data_powsybl(folder)
    stats, si, _ = load_grid(
        DirFileSystem(str(folder)),
        pandapower=False,
        parameters=PreprocessParameters(action_set_filter_bsdf_lodf=False),
    )
    si, unsplit = run_initial_loadflow(si)  # (overload_energy_n_0, overload_energy_n_1) of the unsplit grid
    return stats, si, unsplit


def passthrough(lf):
    """Aggregation function that returns the raw flow matrices. Defined once, never inside a loop."""
    return lf.n_0_matrix, lf.n_1_matrix


def matrices(si, topologies) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Step 2: N-0 (topology, timestep, branch), N-1 (topology, timestep, case, branch) and the success flags."""
    (n0, n1), ok = run_solver_symmetric(topologies, None, None, si.dynamic_information, si.solver_config, passthrough)
    return np.asarray(n0), np.asarray(n1), np.asarray(ok)


def limits(si) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
    """The arrays of ``branch_limits`` as NumPy, with the N-1 limit falling back to the N-0 one."""
    bl = si.dynamic_information.branch_limits
    n_1 = bl.max_mw_flow_n_1 if bl.max_mw_flow_n_1 is not None else bl.max_mw_flow
    return {
        'max_mw_flow': np.asarray(bl.max_mw_flow),
        'max_mw_flow_n_1': np.asarray(n_1),
        'overload_weight': np.asarray(bl.overload_weight) if bl.overload_weight is not None else None,
        'max_mw_flow_limited': np.asarray(bl.max_mw_flow_limited),
        'max_mw_flow_n_1_limited': np.asarray(bl.max_mw_flow_n_1_limited),
        'n0_n1_max_diff': np.asarray(bl.n0_n1_max_diff),
    }


def numpy_metrics(n0: np.ndarray, n1: np.ndarray, lim: dict[str, np.ndarray]) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Step 3 and 5: every metric of ``METRICS`` for one topology, from ``metrics.py`` and the chapter's formulas."""
    w = lim['overload_weight']
    delta = metrics.n0_n1_delta(n0, n1)  # (timesteps, branches)
    d = lim['n0_n1_max_diff']
    penalty = np.where(d < 0, 0.0, np.clip(delta - d, 0, None)).sum()
    return {
        'overload_energy_n_0': metrics.overload_energy_n1(n0[:, None, :], lim['max_mw_flow'], w),
        'overload_energy_n_1': metrics.overload_energy_n1(n1, lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'], w),
        'exponential_overload_energy_n_1': metrics.power_law_n1(n1, lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'], weight=w),
        'critical_branch_count_n_1': float(metrics.critical_branch_count_n1(n1, lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'])),
        'n0_n1_delta': float(penalty),
        'overload_energy_limited_n_0': metrics.overload_energy_n1(n0[:, None, :], lim['max_mw_flow_limited'], w),
        'overload_energy_limited_n_1': metrics.overload_energy_n1(n1, lim['max_mw_flow_n_1_limited'], w),
    }


def toop_metric(si, topologies, metric: str) -> np.ndarray:
    """Step 5: the same metric from ToOp, through ``aggregate_to_metric`` passed as the aggregation function."""
    di = si.dynamic_information
    fn = functools.partial(
        aggregate_to_metric,
        branch_limits=di.branch_limits,
        reassignment_distance=di.action_set.reassignment_distance,
        n_relevant_subs=si.n_sub_relevant,
        metric=metric,
    )
    values, _ = run_solver_symmetric(topologies, None, None, di, si.solver_config, fn)
    return np.asarray(values)


def with_actions(si, actions: dict[int, int], batch_size: int = 4) -> ActionIndexComputations:
    """Step 5: a batch of unsplit topologies with real action indices in the first slot of some of them."""
    base = default_topology(si.solver_config, batch_size=batch_size)
    act = base.action
    for topology, action in actions.items():
        act = act.at[topology, 0].set(action)
    return ActionIndexComputations(action=act, pad_mask=base.pad_mask)


def regimes(n1_unsplit: np.ndarray, rating: np.ndarray, limited: np.ndarray) -> dict[str, int]:
    """Step 4: how many branches fall in each regime of the double limit, from the unsplit worst flows."""
    loading = np.abs(n1_unsplit).max(axis=(0, 1)) / rating
    ratio = limited / rating
    return {
        'below 90 %, limit = 0.9 rating': int(((loading < 0.9) & np.isclose(ratio, 0.9)).sum()),
        'dead zone, limit = flow': int(((loading >= 0.9) & (loading <= 1.0) & np.isclose(ratio, loading)).sum()),
        'above 100 %, limit = rating': int(((loading > 1.0) & np.isclose(ratio, 1.0)).sum()),
    }


def main() -> None:
    """Steps 1 to 5 on IEEE 57, printing every number the solution quotes."""
    folder = Path(sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else 'case57')
    stats, si, unsplit = load(folder)
    print(
        f'stats: {stats.n_nodes} nodes, {stats.n_branches} branches, {stats.n_branch_outages} branch outages + '
        f'{stats.n_injection_outages} injection outages = {stats.n_nminus1_cases} N-1 cases, '
        f'{stats.n_relevant_subs} relevant substations, {stats.n_actions} actions, '
        f'double limits: {stats.has_double_limits}'
    )
    print('run_initial_loadflow (overload_energy_n_0, overload_energy_n_1) =', unsplit)
    lim = limits(si)
    w = lim['overload_weight']
    values, counts = np.unique(w, return_counts=True)
    print('overload weights:', {float(v): int(c) for v, c in zip(values, counts, strict=True)})
    print('N-1 limits / N-0 limits:', np.unique(np.round(lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'] / lim['max_mw_flow'], 6)))

    topologies = default_topology(si.solver_config, batch_size=4)
    n0, n1, ok = matrices(si, topologies)
    print(f'n_0 {n0.shape}, n_1 {n1.shape}, all cases successful: {ok.all()}')
    mine = numpy_metrics(n0[0], n1[0], lim)
    print(f'NumPy unsplit energies: N-0 {mine["overload_energy_n_0"]:.9f}, N-1 {mine["overload_energy_n_1"]:.9f}')
    print(
        f'  differences to ToOp: {abs(mine["overload_energy_n_0"] - unsplit[0]):.1e}, '
        f'{abs(mine["overload_energy_n_1"] - unsplit[1]):.1e}'
    )

    lim0 = metrics.double_limits(n0[0][:, None, :], lim['max_mw_flow'])
    lim1 = metrics.double_limits(n1[0], lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'])
    print(
        'double limits, max |mine - ToOp|: N-0',
        np.abs(lim0 - lim['max_mw_flow_limited']).max(),
        'N-1',
        np.abs(lim1 - lim['max_mw_flow_n_1_limited']).max(),
    )
    print('N-1 regimes:', regimes(n1[0], lim['max_mw_flow_n_1'], lim['max_mw_flow_n_1_limited']))
    print('n0_n1_max_diff factors: all negative (penalty disabled)?', bool((lim['n0_n1_max_diff'] < 0).all()))

    actions = {1: 1, 2: 5}
    topologies = with_actions(si, actions)
    n0, n1, ok = matrices(si, topologies)
    print(f'batch with actions {actions}: successful {ok.all(axis=1)}')
    for metric in METRICS:
        toop = toop_metric(si, topologies, metric)
        mine = np.array([numpy_metrics(n0[t], n1[t], lim)[metric] for t in range(len(toop))])
        print(f'  {metric:32s} ToOp {np.round(toop, 4)}  max |diff| {np.abs(toop - mine).max():.1e}')


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

    Lab, Part B: AC N-1 and metrics from result tables¶

    1. The N-1 definition. data/grid_node_breaker/grid.xiidm has 9 lines, no transformers, 5 buses in the bus-breaker view and 106 switches. One MonitoredElement(id, type="LINE", kind="branch") per line and one MonitoredElement(id, type="BUS", kind="bus") per bus (14 monitored elements), one Contingency(id, elements=[GridElement(id, type="LINE", kind="branch")]) per line plus Contingency(id="BASECASE", elements=[]): 10 contingencies.
    2. Run. get_ac_loadflow_results(net, definition, job_id="lab", timestep=0, n_processes=1) returns polars LazyFrames; .collect().to_pandas() gives 162 branch rows, 49 node rows, 9 va_diff rows and 10 convergence rows, all CONVERGED.

    3. Metrics with pandas. The MW limit behind a row is \(|p| / \text{loading}\), so its overload is \(|p| - |p|/\text{loading}\) (the same with the current i); per (timestep, element) the maximum over contingencies and sides, then the sum of the positive ones. Critical branches are the distinct (timestep, element) with loading > 1 among the N-1 rows; max_va_diff_n_1 is the largest \(|\text{va\_diff}|\).

      Metric pandas compute_metrics
      overload_energy_n_1 62.113903 MW 62.113903 MW
      overload_current_n_1 160.378002 A 160.378002 A
      critical_branch_count_n_1 3 3
      max_va_diff_n_1 0.485122° 0.485122°

      The worst rows are all in the L9 contingency: L3 at 191 % (66.7 MW), L1 and L2 at 143 %. 4. The row count. The formula gives \((2 \cdot 9 + 0 + 0) \cdot 10 = 180\) rows; the table has 162. The 18 missing rows are the two sides of the outaged line in its own contingency (9 contingencies × 2 sides): an element that is out of service has no flow to report, and compute_overload_column would otherwise divide by a zero loading. A contingency that did not converge would remove all its rows as well (none here).

      ac_n1_toop.py
      """Lab Part B of Chapter 19 with ToOp: an AC N-1 on the node-breaker example grid, metrics recomputed with pandas.

Run it with ToOp's interpreter (its container or virtual environment) from this folder::

    python ac_n1_toop.py ../../../../../ToOp/data/grid_node_breaker/grid.xiidm

Without an argument the grid is looked up in ``$TOOP_REPO`` or in the ``../ToOp`` sibling of this repository. The
N-1 definition is built by hand (one monitored element per line, transformer and bus; one contingency per branch
plus the base case), the AC analysis runs through ``get_ac_loadflow_results`` and the metrics are recomputed from
the result tables and compared with ``compute_metrics``.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import os
import sys
from pathlib import Path
from typing import TYPE_CHECKING

import pypowsybl
from toop_engine_contingency_analysis.ac_loadflow_service import get_ac_loadflow_results
from toop_engine_contingency_analysis.ac_loadflow_service.compute_metrics import compute_metrics
from toop_engine_interfaces.nminus1_definition import Contingency, GridElement, MonitoredElement, Nminus1Definition

if TYPE_CHECKING:
    import pandas as pd

BASECASE = 'BASECASE'


def default_grid_path() -> Path:
    """ToOp's ``data/grid_node_breaker/grid.xiidm``."""
    repo = Path(os.environ.get('TOOP_REPO', Path(__file__).resolve().parents[4].parent / 'ToOp'))
    return repo / 'data' / 'grid_node_breaker' / 'grid.xiidm'


def n1_definition(net: pypowsybl.network.Network) -> Nminus1Definition:
    """Step 1: monitor every line, transformer and bus; outage every branch one at a time, plus the base case."""
    branches = [(i, 'LINE') for i in net.get_lines().index] + [
        (i, 'TWO_WINDINGS_TRANSFORMER') for i in net.get_2_windings_transformers().index
    ]
    monitored = [MonitoredElement(id=i, type=t, kind='branch') for i, t in branches]
    monitored += [MonitoredElement(id=i, type='BUS', kind='bus') for i in net.get_buses().index]
    contingencies = [Contingency(id=BASECASE, elements=[])]
    contingencies += [Contingency(id=i, elements=[GridElement(id=i, type=t, kind='branch')]) for i, t in branches]
    return Nminus1Definition(monitored_elements=monitored, contingencies=contingencies)


def run(net: pypowsybl.network.Network, definition: Nminus1Definition):
    """Step 2: the AC N-1 through ToOp's load-flow service; returns the polars result bundle."""
    return get_ac_loadflow_results(net, definition, job_id='lab', timestep=0, n_processes=1)


def frames(results) -> dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
    """The result tables as pandas DataFrames."""
    return {
        'branch': results.branch_results.collect().to_pandas(),
        'node': results.node_results.collect().to_pandas(),
        'va_diff': results.va_diff_results.collect().to_pandas(),
        'converged': results.converged.collect().to_pandas(),
    }


def pandas_metrics(branch: pd.DataFrame, va_diff: pd.DataFrame, base_case: str = BASECASE) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Step 3: ``overload_energy_n_1``, ``overload_current_n_1``, ``critical_branch_count_n_1``, ``max_va_diff_n_1``.

    The MW limit of a row is |p| / loading, so its overload is |p| - |p| / loading (the same with the current
    ``i``); per branch the worst contingency and side counts, then the overloads are summed.
    """
    n1 = branch[branch.contingency != base_case]
    out = {}
    for field, name in (('p', 'overload_energy_n_1'), ('i', 'overload_current_n_1')):
        over = n1[field].abs() - (n1[field] / n1.loading).abs()
        worst = over.groupby([n1.timestep, n1.element]).max()
        out[name] = float(worst[worst > 0].sum())
    critical = n1[n1.loading.fillna(-1) > 1.0][['timestep', 'element']].drop_duplicates()
    out['critical_branch_count_n_1'] = float(len(critical))
    out['max_va_diff_n_1'] = float(va_diff[va_diff.contingency != base_case].va_diff.abs().max())
    return out


def expected_rows(net: pypowsybl.network.Network, n_contingencies: int) -> int:
    """Step 4: the row count the formula promises, two sides per line and transformer, three per 3-winding one."""
    return (
        2 * len(net.get_lines())
        + 2 * len(net.get_2_windings_transformers())
        + 3 * len(net.get_3_windings_transformers())
    ) * n_contingencies


def main() -> None:
    """Steps 1 to 4 on the node-breaker example, printing every number the solution quotes."""
    path = Path(sys.argv[1]) if len(sys.argv) > 1 else default_grid_path()
    net = pypowsybl.network.load(str(path))
    definition = n1_definition(net)
    print(
        f'{len(net.get_lines())} lines, {len(net.get_2_windings_transformers())} transformers, '
        f'{len(net.get_buses())} buses, {len(net.get_switches())} switches; '
        f'{len(definition.monitored_elements)} monitored elements, {len(definition.contingencies)} contingencies'
    )
    results = run(net, definition)
    f = frames(results)
    print({k: len(v) for k, v in f.items()}, 'convergence:', f['converged'].status.value_counts().to_dict())
    print('branch result rows', len(f['branch']), 'of', expected_rows(net, len(definition.contingencies)), 'expected')
    own = f['branch'][f['branch'].contingency == f['branch'].element]
    print('rows where the outaged branch reports in its own contingency:', len(own))
    mine = pandas_metrics(f['branch'], f['va_diff'])
    toop = compute_metrics(results, base_case_id=BASECASE)
    for name, value in mine.items():
        print(f'  {name:26s} pandas {value:12.6f}   compute_metrics {toop[name]:12.6f}')
    worst = f['branch'][f['branch'].contingency != BASECASE].sort_values('loading', ascending=False).head(3)
    print('three most loaded N-1 rows:')
    print(worst[['contingency', 'element', 'side', 'p', 'i', 'loading']].to_string(index=False))
    va = f['va_diff']
    print(
        'va_diff rows above 20 deg:',
        int((va.va_diff.abs() > 20).sum()),
        ' above 10 deg:',
        int((va.va_diff.abs() > 10).sum()),
    )
    print(
        va.loc[
            va.va_diff.abs().sort_values(ascending=False).index[:3], ['contingency', 'element', 'va_diff']
        ].to_string(index=False)
    )


if __name__ == '__main__':

      """Lab Part B, step 5 of Chapter 19: the same single-branch AC N-1 of IEEE 14 in pandapower and in pypowsybl.

pandapower's ``case14`` is exported with ``to_mpc`` and loaded by pypowsybl from the MATPOWER file, as
``test_backend_comparison.py`` in ToOp does. Both engines then outage every branch one at a time; the branch flows
``p`` at the from side are compared per (contingency, branch). Needs only pandapower and pypowsybl.
"""

import tempfile
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
import pandapower as pp
import pandapower.networks as pn
import pandas as pd
import pypowsybl
import pypowsybl.security as security
from pandapower.converter.matpower import to_mpc

# One slack at bus 1 and no reactive limits, as pandapower's runpp does by default (enforce_q_lims=False).
LF_PARAMS = pypowsybl.loadflow.Parameters(distributed_slack=False, use_reactive_limits=False)


def branch_ids(net: pp.pandapowerNet) -> list[str]:
    """Pypowsybl's id of every pandapower branch, lines first then transformers: ``LINE-<from>-<to>`` style."""
    ids = [f'{f + 1}-{t + 1}' for f, t in zip(net.line.from_bus, net.line.to_bus, strict=True)]
    ids += [f'{h + 1}-{lv + 1}' for h, lv in zip(net.trafo.hv_bus, net.trafo.lv_bus, strict=True)]
    return ids


def powsybl_network(net: pp.pandapowerNet) -> pypowsybl.network.Network:
    """Round trip through a MATPOWER file (``to_mpc`` wants a solved network to initialise voltages from)."""
    pp.rundcpp(net)
    with tempfile.TemporaryDirectory() as folder:
        path = Path(folder) / 'ieee14.mat'
        to_mpc(net, str(path))
        return pypowsybl.network.load(str(path))


def powsybl_ids(pnet: pypowsybl.network.Network) -> dict[str, str]:
    """Map ``<from>-<to>`` to pypowsybl's element id (``LINE-4-7`` or ``TWT-4-7``, depending on the tap ratio)."""
    out = {}
    for prefix, table in (('LINE-', pnet.get_lines()), ('TWT-', pnet.get_2_windings_transformers())):
        for element in table.index:
            out[element.removeprefix(prefix)] = element
    return out


def pandapower_n1(net: pp.pandapowerNet) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Flows ``p`` at the from/hv side in MW: one row per outaged branch, one column per branch, NaN when diverged."""
    ids = branch_ids(net)
    rows = {}
    for k, key in enumerate(ids):
        table, idx = ('line', k) if k < len(net.line) else ('trafo', k - len(net.line))
        net[table].at[idx, 'in_service'] = False
        try:
            pp.runpp(net)
            flows = np.concatenate([net.res_line.p_from_mw.to_numpy(), net.res_trafo.p_hv_mw.to_numpy()])
        except pp.LoadflowNotConverged:
            flows = np.full(len(ids), np.nan)
        net[table].at[idx, 'in_service'] = True
        flows[k] = np.nan
        rows[key] = flows
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, index=ids).T


def powsybl_n1(pnet: pypowsybl.network.Network, ids: list[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """The same table from pypowsybl's security analysis, columns in the pandapower order."""
    names = powsybl_ids(pnet)
    analysis = security.create_analysis()
    analysis.add_single_element_contingencies([names[i] for i in ids])
    analysis.add_monitored_elements(branch_ids=[names[i] for i in ids])
    result = analysis.run_ac(pnet, LF_PARAMS)
    table = result.branch_results.reset_index()
    out = pd.DataFrame(np.nan, index=ids, columns=ids)
    for key in ids:
        rows = table[table.contingency_id == names[key]].set_index('branch_id')
        for col in ids:
            if names[col] in rows.index:
                out.at[key, col] = rows.at[names[col], 'p1']
    return out


def compare() -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, pd.Series]:
    """Both N-1 tables and the absolute differences per (contingency, branch), as a flat series without NaNs."""
    net = pn.case14()
    pnet = powsybl_network(net)
    pp_table = pandapower_n1(net)
    py_table = powsybl_n1(pnet, branch_ids(net))
    diff = (pp_table - py_table).abs().stack()
    return pp_table, py_table, diff


def main() -> None:
    """Print the distribution of the branch-flow differences between the two engines."""
    pp_table, py_table, diff = compare()
    print(f'{pp_table.shape[0]} contingencies x {pp_table.shape[1]} branches; compared entries: {len(diff)}')
    print(
        '|p_pandapower - p_pypowsybl| in MW: quantiles 50 / 90 / 99 / max =',
        np.round(diff.quantile([0.5, 0.9, 0.99]).to_list() + [diff.max()], 4),
    )
    worst = diff.idxmax()
    print(
        f'largest difference: outage {worst[0]}, branch {worst[1]}: '
        f'{pp_table.loc[worst]:.3f} vs {py_table.loc[worst]:.3f} MW'
    )
    print('contingencies not converged in pandapower:', pp_table.index[pp_table.isna().all(axis=1)].tolist())
    print('entries missing in pypowsybl (outaged branch itself or disconnected):', int(py_table.isna().sum().sum()))


if __name__ == '__main__':
    main()

    Lab, Part C: the price of security on the 7-bus case¶

    1. Binding constraints. binding(dispatch(range(11)).x) returns two pairs: line 1–3 after the outage of line 1–2, and line 1–2 after the outage of line 1–3. Both lines carry exactly 240 MW. The cheap generator at bus 1 (capped at 250 MW) has only these two lines to leave the bus, so when one trips the other carries all of it, and the SCOPF holds it to the limit by moving 10 MW to the generator at bus 2.
    2. AC security. The AC N-1 of the SCOPF dispatch peaks at 109.7 % (114.7 % for the plain OPF). Deriating the limits of the overloaded lines to (worst DC flow) × 100 / (worst AC loading) and solving again gives a SCOPF cost of $4,400/h, then $4,612/h, then $4,672/h, at which point the AC worst loading is at most 100 % (ac_secure_dispatch()). AC security costs $272/h more than DC security and $372/h more than the plain OPF.
    3. Line 1–3 limited to 150 MW. The OPF stays at $4,300/h: its base-case flow on line 1–3 is 133 MW, below the new limit, and an OPF never looks at outages. The SCOPF costs $5,360/h with dispatch (146.3, 192.2, 1.5) MW. The outage of line 1–2 sends all of the generator at bus 1 through line 1–3, so that generator must stay below about 150 MW. Instead of 10 MW, about 104 MW move to the $20 generator at bus 2, which then reaches its own network limits (binding: line 1–3 after the outage of line 2–3, line 2–3 after the outage of line 2–5), and 1.5 MW come from the $35 generator at bus 7.     Exercise 1¶

    The worked example has \(f_\max = (100, 200, 50)\) MW and N-1 flows \((95, -210, 40)\) and \((120, -160, 55)\): overload energy \(20 + 10 + 5 = 35\) MW, critical branch count 3. All three branches are already critical, so the count can only rise from a state where one of them is not; and the count can never move while the energy stays exactly put: a branch becomes (or stops being) critical exactly when its worst-case overload \(\max_c (|F_{cb}| - \bar F_b)^+\) leaves (or reaches) zero, and that same quantity is the branch's term in the energy. What the exercise shows is how little energy a count change needs. Start from a state where branch 3 is below its limit (scale its two flows by 0.9: 36 and 49.5 MW; energy 30 MW, count 2) and raise the 49.5 MW to 50.001 MW: the energy moves by \(\varepsilon = 0.001\) MW, the count by one (metric_design.energy_and_count). The opposite direction is easy: raise branch 1 in the second contingency from 120 to 140 MW and the energy goes from 35 to 55 MW while the count stays 3. The change that moves neither metric: raise branch 1 in the first contingency from 95 to 115 MW and nothing moves at all, because 115 MW is still below that branch's worst case of 120 MW (the per-branch maximum hides every contingency but the worst).

    What each objective prefers: under overload energy the optimizer trades a 20 MW relief on one branch against a 19.9 MW new overload elsewhere, and it is indifferent to how many branches carry the overload; under the critical branch count it would rather concentrate 50 MW on one branch than spread 2 MW over three, and it gives nothing for relieving an overloaded branch that stays overloaded. ToOp's weighted sum (\(-(35 + 10 \cdot 3) = -65\) in the chapter) is the compromise: the count term rewards healing a branch completely, the energy term rewards every MW on the way there.

def energy_and_count(n_1: np.ndarray, f_max: np.ndarray = metrics.f_max) -> tuple[float, int]:
    """Exercise 1: overload energy and critical branch count of an N-1 matrix."""
    return metrics.overload_energy_n1(n_1, f_max), metrics.critical_branch_count_n1(n_1, f_max)


def changed(n_1: np.ndarray, contingency: int, branch: int, value: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Exercise 1: a copy of ``n_1`` with one entry (timestep 0) replaced."""
    out = n_1.copy()
    out[0, contingency, branch] = value
    return out


def penalty_curves(loading_pct: np.ndarray, f_max: float, alpha: float = 1.5) -> dict[str, np.ndarray]:
    """Exercise 2: per-branch penalty of three metrics as a function of the loading in percent.

    Overload energy is (|F| - f_max)+ in MW; the power law is (max(|F| / f_max, 1)^alpha - 1) f_max in MW;
    cumulative overload is the relative overload (|F| - f_max)+ / f_max, blind to the rating.
    """
    rel = loading_pct / 100.0
    over = np.clip(rel - 1.0, 0, None)
    return {
        'overload_energy': over * f_max,
        'power_law': (np.clip(rel, 1, None) ** alpha - 1) * f_max,
        'cumulative_overload': over,
    }


def dead_zone_example(f_max=(100.0, 100.0), unsplit=(95.0, 60.0), split=(97.0, 58.0)) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Exercise 3: two branches, the unsplit flows set the limited limits, a redistribution gets penalised."""
    f_max, unsplit, split = (np.asarray(x, float) for x in (f_max, unsplit, split))
    limited = metrics.double_limits(unsplit[None, None, :], f_max)
    return {
        'limited_limits': limited,
        'unsplit_overload_energy_limited_n_0': metrics.overload_energy_n1(unsplit[None, None, :], limited),
        'split_overload_energy_limited_n_0': metrics.overload_energy_n1(split[None, None, :], limited),
        'split_overload_energy_n_0': metrics.overload_energy_n1(split[None, None, :], f_max),
    }


def acceptance(
    split: dict[str, float],
    unsplit: dict[str, float],
    convergence: float = 1.0,
    overload: float = 0.95,
    critical: float = 1.1,
) -> str | None:
    """Exercise 4: ToOp's ``evaluate_acceptance`` with its default thresholds and voltage checks off.

    ``split`` and ``unsplit`` hold ``non_converging_loadflows``, ``overload_energy_n_1``, ``critical_branch_count_n_1``
    and, for the split, ``disconnected_branches``. Returns the rejection criterion, or ``None`` when accepted.
    """
    non_converged = split.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0) - split.get('disconnected_branches', 0)
    if non_converged > unsplit.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0) * convergence:
        return 'convergence'
    if split.get('overload_energy_n_1', 99999) > unsplit.get('overload_energy_n_1', 0) * overload:
        return 'overload-energy'
    if split.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 0) > unsplit.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 999) * critical:
        return 'critical-branch-count'
    return None


def acceptance_with_slack(split: dict[str, float], unsplit: dict[str, float], slack: int = 1) -> str | None:
    """Exercise 4: the same rule with an absolute slack of ``slack`` critical branches instead of 10 %."""
    non_converged = split.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0) - split.get('disconnected_branches', 0)
    if non_converged > unsplit.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0):
        return 'convergence'
    if split.get('overload_energy_n_1', 99999) > unsplit.get('overload_energy_n_1', 0) * 0.95:
        return 'overload-energy'
    if split.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 0) > unsplit.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 999) + slack:
        return 'critical-branch-count'
    return None


def fitness_table(weights: dict[str, float], values: dict[str, float]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Exercise 6: one row per metric with value, weight and contribution; the DC fitness is minus the total."""
    table = pd.DataFrame(
        {'metric': list(weights), 'value': [values[m] for m in weights], 'weight': list(weights.values())}
    )
    table['contribution'] = table.value * table.weight
    return table

    Exercise 2¶

    Per branch and as functions of the loading \(\ell = |F| / \bar F\) (metric_design.penalty_curves):

    \[ \text{overload energy} = (\ell - 1)^+ \bar F, \qquad \text{power law} = \left(\max(\ell, 1)^{1.5} - 1\right)\bar F, \qquad \text{cumulative overload} = (\ell - 1)^+ . \]

    Plotted from 0 to 200 % loading for \(\bar F = 100\) MW and \(\bar F = 1\,000\) MW (two panels, one per metric, or one panel per limit), the first two are flat at zero up to 100 % and then rise, linearly for the energy and convexly for the power law, with the 1 000 MW curves ten times higher than the 100 MW ones; the cumulative overload is the same curve for both limits. At 110 % and 150 % the values are 10 and 50 MW (energy), 15.4 and 83.7 MW (power law) for 100 MW, 100 and 500 MW, 153.7 and 837.1 MW for 1 000 MW, and 0.1 and 0.5 (cumulative overload) for both. The cumulative overload is the capacity-blind metric that makes a 10 % overload on a 110 kV line as costly as on a 380 kV line; it is the only one of the three that does so, since the power law scales with \(\bar F\) like the energy (ToOp's get_cumulative_overload_n_1_matrix divides the overload matrix by max_mw_flow and sums the per-branch maxima). The power law's extra is a bias against deep overloads: at 150 % it charges 1.67 times the energy, at 110 % 1.54 times.

    Exercise 3¶

    Two branches rated 100 MW, unsplit flows (95, 60) MW (metric_design.dead_zone_example). double_limits gives limited limits (95, 90): branch 1 is in the dead zone, so its limit becomes its current flow; branch 2 is below 90 %, so its limit becomes 90. The unsplit overload_energy_limited_n_0 is \(0\) (\(95 \le 95\), \(60 \le 90\)). A topology that only shifts 2 MW from branch 2 to branch 1, flows (97, 58), is overloaded nowhere against the ratings (overload_energy_n_0 = 0) but gets overload_energy_limited_n_0 = 2 MW. That is the purpose of the rule: a branch at 95 % is one forecast error, one AC correction or one reactive flow away from its rating, and the DC solver that ranks topologies does not see those. The dead zone tells the optimizer "do not load this branch any further" without declaring it overloaded, which would otherwise push the search towards relieving a branch that is in fact fine, and the 90 % rule keeps lightly loaded branches from being filled up to the brim.

    Exercise 4¶

    evaluate_acceptance checks, in order, with the default thresholds 1.0, 0.95 and 1.1 (metric_design.acceptance reproduces the rule):

    1. Convergence: non-converging cases of the candidate minus its disconnected branches, \(2 - 1 = 1\), must be \(\le 1.0 \times 2\): passes.
    2. Overload energy: \(0 \le 0.95 \times 0 = 0\): passes (zero is not an improvement, but it is not above zero).
    3. Critical branches: \(1 \le 1.1 \times 0 = 0\): fails. The candidate is rejected with the criterion critical-branch-count.

    The rejection is right in substance (the candidate created an overloaded branch where there was none) but the mechanism is fragile: a relative threshold around a zero baseline is an absolute threshold of zero, so a grid that is N-1 secure to begin with can accept no candidate with the slightest new violation, and the 10 % margin that the designers intended never exists. An absolute slack (allow at most \(k\) new critical branches, or at most \(\delta\) MW of new overload energy) behaves the same whether the baseline is 0 or 100; a hybrid \(\max(1.1 \cdot x, x + 1)\) keeps the relative behaviour where the baseline is large. ToOp's own design notes agree: with a zero baseline, "\(1.1 \times 0 = 0\) rejects any increase" (chapter text). acceptance_with_slack with one branch of slack accepts this candidate. The same rule accepts (94 MW, 11 branches) against (100 MW, 10 branches) and rejects (96 MW, 10) against (100 MW, 10) for the energy, which shows the intended behaviour away from zero.

    matpower_n1_metrics.m
    function [oe, nc, worst, F1, lim] = matpower_n1_metrics(casename, load_bus, factor)
  % DC N-1 flow matrix of a MATPOWER case from makePTDF and makeLODF, with ToOp's overload energy and
  % critical branch count (Chapter 19, Exercise 5). Every limit is 1.2 times the base-case flow of the
  % unmodified case; with load_bus > 0 the load of that bus is multiplied by factor before the flows are
  % computed, the limits staying as they were. Bridges (NaN columns of the LODF) are skipped.
  % F1(:, k) holds the flows after the outage of branch k; worst(b) is the outage with the largest
  % overload of branch b (1 when it is never overloaded).
  if nargin < 2, load_bus = 0; end
  if nargin < 3, factor = 1; end
  define_constants;
  mpc = ext2int(loadcase(casename));
  nb = size(mpc.bus, 1);
  nl = size(mpc.branch, 1);
  H = makePTDF(mpc);
  L = makeLODF(mpc, H, true);                       % mask_bridge = true: NaN columns for bridges
  Cg = sparse(mpc.gen(:, GEN_BUS), 1:size(mpc.gen, 1), 1, nb, size(mpc.gen, 1));
  P = Cg * mpc.gen(:, PG) - mpc.bus(:, PD);         % MW injections; the slack absorbs the mismatch
  lim = 1.2 * abs(H * P);                           % limits from the original base case
  if load_bus > 0
    P(load_bus) = P(load_bus) - (factor - 1) * mpc.bus(load_bus, PD);
  end
  F = H * P;
  F1 = repmat(F, 1, nl) + L .* repmat(F', nl, 1);   % F + LODF(:, k) F(k), column by column
  over = max(abs(F1) - repmat(lim, 1, nl), 0);      % NaN columns (bridges) are ignored by max
  over(over < 1e-9) = 0;                            % round-off on zero-flow branches is not an overload
  [oe_b, worst] = max(over, [], 2);
  oe = sum(oe_b);
  nc = sum(oe_b > 0);
end

    Exercise 6¶

    The DC fitness is \(-\sum_m w_m\,\text{metric}_m\). One objective that gives \(-713\) is the chapter's own weight pair, overload_energy_n_1 with weight 1 and critical_branch_count_n_1 with weight 10, at 213 MW and 50 branches: \(213 + 10 \cdot 50 = 713\) (metric_design.fitness_table, a pandas table with one row per metric and a contribution column). Many other combinations produce the same number (113 MW and 60 branches, or an extra n0_n1_delta term), which is the first reason the number cannot be read back: a weighted sum has no inverse. The AC fitness 12.4 is a single metric, overload_energy_n_1 in MW from the AC N-1, with the opposite sign convention (lower is better). The two are not comparable because: they have opposite signs; one is a weighted composite and the other a single quantity; the DC value is computed on DC flows against (possibly double) MW limits over the DC contingency list, the AC value on AC loadings (current, including reactive flow and losses) with the limit of each row derived from its loading; and the AC stage reports it only for the topologies that the DC stage proposed, while the DC optimizer compares its number across its whole population. What can be compared is a difference within one stage: the DC fitness of two topologies, or the AC overload energy of a candidate against the unsplit grid, which is exactly what evaluate_acceptance does.

    Exercise 7¶

    write_case in the script writes the folder; the pass-through function returns (lf.n_0_matrix, lf.n_1_matrix) and is defined once, at module level. run_initial_loadflow returns (overload_energy_n_0, overload_energy_n_1) = (0.0, 20.0), and the unsplit N-1 matrix gives, per outage, the worst loaded line:

    Outage Worst line Loading
    L1-2 L1-3 104.2 %
    L1-3 L1-2 104.2 %
    L2-3 L1-3 71.8 %
    L2-5 L2-3 85.7 %
    every other line L1-3 or L2-5 below 67 %

    The SciPy code gives the same 104.2 % (n1_loading(dispatch().x).max()). The two agree to the decimal because both solve the same lossless DC equations, and the LODF update is exact, not an approximation. The 20 MW follow from the definition: for each branch the worst contingency, then the sum over branches. L1-3 carries 250 MW after the L1-2 outage (10 MW above its 240 MW), L1-2 carries 250 MW after the L1-3 outage (another 10 MW), and no other branch exceeds its limit in any contingency: 10 + 10 = 20 MW. Under the "worst contingency per timestep" wording of metrics.md (see "Documentation vs code" above) the value would be 10 MW; this case shows that the code sums per branch.

    Exercise 8¶

    Choosing the set \(B\) of branches that go to the second busbar with \(2 \le |B| \le d - 2\) gives \(2^d - 2 - 2d\) configurations; each split appears twice (\(B\) and its complement), so a substation with \(d\) branches has \(2^{d-1} - d - 1\) splits: 3 for \(d = 4\), 10 for \(d = 5\), 25 for \(d = 6\) and none for \(d = 3\). Buses 3, 5 and 6 have four lines each, bus 4 has three (L3-4, L4-5, L4-6), so the prediction is \(3 \times (3 + 1) = 12\) actions. ToOp reports n_actions_per_sub = [4, 4, 4] for buses 3, 5 and 6, and bus 4 is not in relevant_nodes at all. In the code, enumerate_branch_actions_for_sub (dc_solver/preprocess/action_set.py) asserts \(d \ge 4\) (smaller substations are dropped earlier), enumerates \(2^{d-1}\) configurations (the mirror image is not generated), removes those that isolate a single branch (exclude_isolations) and then those that leave fewer than two non-bridge branches on a busbar (filter_splits_by_bridge_lookup; no line of this grid is a bridge, so nothing more goes). Decoded, the twelve actions are:

    Action Substation Branches on a busbar of their own
    0, 4, 8 bus 3, 5, 6 none (unsplit)
    1, 2, 3 bus 3 L3-6 + L1-3; L3-4 + L1-3; L3-4 + L3-6
    5, 6, 7 bus 5 L5-7 + L2-5; L5-6 + L2-5; L5-6 + L5-7
    9, 10, 11 bus 6 L3-6 + L4-6; L6-7 + L4-6; L6-7 + L3-6

    The load of a substation stays with the branches that are not moved (inj_actions is all False here).

    Exercise 9¶

    Kirchhoff's current law at bus 1: with L1-2 out, L1-3 is the only branch left at bus 1, so it carries the bus's net injection, 250 MW of generation and no load, whatever the impedances or the topology downstream: 250 / 240 = 104.2 %. The same holds for L1-2 after the L1-3 outage. A split at buses 3, 5 or 6 changes how the 250 MW spread beyond bus 3, never how they leave bus 1. ToOp confirms it:

    Action Split Largest N-0 change Worst N-1 loading
    0, 4, 8 unsplit 0 MW 104.2 % (L1-3 after L1-2 out)
    1 bus 3: L3-6 + L1-3 44 MW 125.0 % (L3-6 after L1-2 out)
    2 bus 3: L3-4 + L1-3 31 MW 125.0 % (L3-4 after L1-2 out)
    3 bus 3: L3-4 + L3-6 137 MW islanded (solver flag False, nan)
    5 bus 5: L5-7 + L2-5 66 MW 126.5 % (L2-3 after L1-3 out)
    6 bus 5: L5-6 + L2-5 73 MW 109.3 % (L2-3 after L1-3 out)
    7 bus 5: L5-6 + L5-7 13 MW 104.2 %
    9 bus 6: L3-6 + L4-6 38 MW 104.2 %
    10 bus 6: L6-7 + L4-6 1 MW 104.2 %
    11 bus 6: L6-7 + L3-6 35 MW 104.2 %

    No action gets below 104.2 %, four add a second overload, and action 3 turns line 2–5 into a bridge: the busbar with L3-4 and L3-6 has no injection and no other link to buses 1 and 2, so already in the base case L2-5 carries the whole 260 MW of load of buses 4–6 (108 % of its limit), and its outage islands buses 4–7 without generation. The action_set_filter_bsdf_lodf option, switched off in write_case, removes such actions from the set (Chapter 22). The only remedy is redispatch: the SCOPF moves 10 MW from the $10 generator at bus 1 to the $20 one at bus 2, the $100/h of the worked example. In general, a bus split redistributes flow among parallel paths; it cannot change the flow through a cut whose remaining branches all lie on one side of a fixed injection.

    Exercise 10¶

    (a) The old SCOPF dispatch loads L3-6 to 111.5 % after the L2-5 outage. The SCOPF with the tighter limit costs $4,739/h with dispatch (240, 77.4, 22.6): the $35 generator at bus 7 must run, and the binding constraints are now (L1-2 out, L1-3), (L1-3 out, L1-2) and (L2-5 out, L3-6). That is $339/h more than the original SCOPF, the redispatch an operator would like to avoid.

    (b) ToOp: the unsplit grid has overload_energy_n_1 = 11.5 MW (111.5 MW on a 100 MW line). Two of the twelve actions clear every N-1 overload, both at bus 6: action 11, L3-6 + L6-7 on their own busbar, brings L3-6 down to 60.2 MW, and action 9, L3-6 + L4-6, to 76.8 MW; the worst loading is then exactly 100.0 %, on the lines 1–2 and 1–3 that the SCOPF had left at their limits. The third bus-6 split (L6-7 + L4-6) leaves 109.0 %, and every bus-3 and bus-5 split is worse than doing nothing (104.7 % to 240 %, or islanding).

    (c) split_bus(pandapower_net(pg, lim), 6, ['L3-6', 'L6-7']) followed by pandapower_n1 gives the same 60.2 MW on L3-6 and a worst DC loading of 100.0 %.

    (d) After the L2-5 outage the 260 MW of load at buses 4, 5 and 6 must all come from bus 3 through the cut {L3-4, L3-6}. The total through the cut is fixed by KCL, as in Exercise 9, but its split between the two lines is not: it follows the impedances of the parallel paths behind the cut. Unsplit, L3-6 feeds bus 6 directly and bus 5 through L5-6 (\(x = 0.14 + 0.10\)). Paired with L6-7, whatever enters through L3-6 must continue to bus 7 and reach bus 5 through L5-7 (\(x = 0.14 + 0.12 + 0.15\)), a much longer path than L3-4 → L4-5 (\(0.20\)) or L3-4 → L4-6 (\(0.22\)), so most of the flow moves to L3-4 (199.8 MW of 200 MW after the same outage). A split helps when the overloaded line has a parallel path downstream that the split can lengthen or shorten; it is powerless when the line is the only path, as at bus 1 (Chapter 28). The AC check shows the price of the thin DC margin: the split topology's worst AC N-1 loading is 116 % on L3-4, so this remedial action passes ToOp's DC stage and fails its AC validation, which is the reason the two stages exist.
