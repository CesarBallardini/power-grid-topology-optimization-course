    Chapter 28 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 28. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: ieee30_ots.py (DC-OPF, optimal transmission switching and lazy N-1 constraints with PuLP and HiGHS; it imports the IEEE 30 network data from Chapter 27's ch27_ieee30.py), braess3.py (the 3-bus case of Exercise 2 and the big-M check of Exercise 1) and acceptance.py (a port of ToOp's AC acceptance rule and the table of a validation run). Each module prints the numbers quoted below when run as a script. The port was checked against ToOp ba1874e with test_acceptance_toop.py (ToOp's interpreter, make test-toop). Lab B uses two fresh runs that lab_b_toop.py made in the course's ToOp container (image toop-course:cpu, ToOp ba1874e, docker run --cpus 4): the run_pipeline call of notebooks/example2_small_grid_toop.ipynb with its settings (30 s of DC search with ToOp's default seed 42, one descriptor split_subs with 4 cells, AC validation of the 5 best topologies), once on the notebook's grid data/grid_node_breaker/grid.xiidm (44 s for the whole pipeline) and once on the complex example grid data/complex_grid/grid.xiidm (65 s). Two settings differ from the notebook: the preprocessing keywords that Pydantic drops are left out, and switching_distance is added to the observed metrics so that dc_info carries it for step 4. The small result files of both runs are in src/lab_b_run/ (res.json, unsplit_ac_metrics.json, and per topology ac_metrics.json, orao_summary.json and the switches.json that lab_b_toop.py adds), and handoff.py reads them anywhere.

    The IEEE 30 instance: pandapower's case30 (MATPOWER data: 30 buses, 41 lines with ratings of 16 to 130 MW, 189.2 MW of load). MATPOWER's costs are quadratic, so the LP uses ten times the linear coefficient of each generator: 20 $/MWh at bus 1 (80 MW), 17.5 at bus 2 (80 MW), 10 at bus 22 (50 MW), 32.5 at bus 27 (55 MW), 30 at bus 23 (30 MW) and 30 at bus 13 (40 MW).

    Lab A¶

    1. DC-OPF and LMPs. The LP of the worked example costs 3101.0 $/h with the dispatch (57.5, 80, 50, 0, 1.7, 0) MW: the 10 $/MWh unit at bus 22 and the 17.5 unit at bus 2 run at their limits, bus 1 (20 $/MWh) is marginal, and 1.7 MW come from the 30 $/MWh unit at bus 23. The LP flows agree with PTDF @ (generation - load) to \(10^{-13}\) MW. One line is at its rating, 22–24 (16 MW): the cheap unit at bus 22 can only leave through 21–22 and 22–24, and 22–24 is full. The duals of the balance constraints range from 11.95 to 38.88 $/MWh. Without congestion every LMP equals the marginal unit's cost (with the ratings relaxed to 10 GW all thirty prices are 20.00); with 22–24 binding, the buses behind it are cheap (bus 22: 11.95, bus 21: 12.60) and those in front of it expensive (bus 24: 38.88, buses 25–26: 32.54, bus 23: 30.00, the 30 $/MWh unit there being marginal for its corner of the grid). An LMP above the dearest running unit is possible: a MW delivered at bus 24 has to come from bus 23 or 27 and back off the cheap unit to keep 22–24 at 16 MW. The difference of the duals across a congested line is its shadow price.

      ieee30_ots.py (data and DC-OPF)
      def data(scale: float = 1.0) -> dict:
    """IEEE 30 for the OPF: the Chapter 27 network dict plus ``load`` (MW per bus) and ``gens`` (bus, MW, $/MWh)."""
    case = ch27_ieee30.load_case(scale)
    net = pn.case30()
    load = np.zeros(case['n_bus'])
    np.add.at(load, net.load.bus.to_numpy(), scale * net.load.p_mw.to_numpy())
    buses = [int(net.ext_grid.bus.iloc[0]), *net.gen.bus.astype(int).tolist()]
    p_max = [float(net.ext_grid.max_p_mw.iloc[0]), *net.gen.max_p_mw.tolist()]
    cost = (10 * net.poly_cost.cp1_eur_per_mw).tolist()  # ext_grid first, then the gens, as in poly_cost
    case.update(load=load, gens=list(zip(buses, p_max, cost, strict=True)), b=1 / case['x'])
    return case


def _base_model(case: dict, name: str) -> tuple[pulp.LpProblem, dict]:
    """Variables and constraints shared by the DC-OPF and the switching MILP; returns the problem and its parts."""
    n_bus, lines = case['n_bus'], case['lines']
    m = pulp.LpProblem(name, pulp.LpMinimize)
    p = [m.add_variable(f'p_{g}', 0, p_max) for g, (_, p_max, _) in enumerate(case['gens'])]
    theta = [m.add_variable(f'theta_{n}', None, None) for n in range(n_bus)]
    f = [m.add_variable(f'f_{k}', -r, r) for k, r in enumerate(case['rating'])]
    m += theta[0] == 0
    balance = []
    for n in range(n_bus):
        generation = pulp.lpSum(p[g] for g, (bus, _, _) in enumerate(case['gens']) if bus == n)
        out_flow = pulp.lpSum(f[k] for k in range(len(lines)) if lines[k][0] == n)
        in_flow = pulp.lpSum(f[k] for k in range(len(lines)) if lines[k][1] == n)
        c = generation - case['load'][n] == out_flow - in_flow
        m += c, f'balance_{n}'
        balance.append(c)
    m += pulp.lpSum(cost * p[g] for g, (_, _, cost) in enumerate(case['gens']))
    return m, {'p': p, 'theta': theta, 'f': f, 'balance': balance}


def dc_opf(case: dict, contingencies: list[int] | None = None) -> dict:
    """DC-OPF as an LP; ``contingencies`` adds the post-outage limits of those lines through the LODF.

    Returns cost ($/h), dispatch ``p`` (MW), flows ``f`` (MW), angles ``theta`` (rad) and the nodal prices ``lmp``
    ($/MWh, the duals of the balance constraints).
    """
    m, v = _base_model(case, 'dc_opf')
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(case['lines']):
        m += v['f'][k] == case['b'][k] * (v['theta'][i] - v['theta'][j])
    if contingencies:
        lodf = _lodf(case)
        for c in contingencies:
            if np.isnan(lodf[:, c]).any():
                continue  # a bridge: its outage islands the grid and has no LODF, ToOp drops it too
            for k in range(len(case['lines'])):
                if k != c:
                    post = v['f'][k] + lodf[k, c] * v['f'][c]
                    m += post <= case['rating'][k]
                    m += -post <= case['rating'][k]
    m.solve(pulp.HiGHS(msg=False))
    return {
        'status': pulp.LpStatus[m.status],
        'cost': pulp.value(m.objective),
        'p': np.array([x.value() for x in v['p']]),
        'f': np.array([x.value() for x in v['f']]),
        'theta': np.array([x.value() for x in v['theta']]),
        'lmp': np.array([c.pi for c in v['balance']]),
    }

    2. Switching with big-M. The lines are ranked by their loading in the DC-OPF (22–24 at 100 %, 6–8 at 89 %, 21–22 at 82 %, 2–6, 2–4, ...), and the first 5, 10, 20 or all 41 are switchable. With mode='global' the big-M of line \(\ell\) is \(b_\ell \sum_{k \ne \ell} \bar f_k / b_k\) (520 to 16,037 MW, valid for every connected topology); the cycle bound of Exercise 1 (mode='cycle', 16 to 2,231 MW) is much tighter but valid only while the other lines stay closed. The sweep (HiGHS, 20 s limit):

      5 1, 2, 3 3095.5 2–6 0.05–0.10 0
      10 1, 2, 3 3095.5 2–6 (plus a no-effect line for \(k \ge 2\)) 0.06–0.20 0
      20 1 3084.0 23–24 0.07 0

      Switching saves at most 17 $/h (0.5 %): it reroutes flow around 22–24 so that bus 1 can replace the 1.7 MW of the 30 $/MWh unit, and the dispatch becomes (59.2, 80, 50, 0, 0, 0). The optimum is degenerate (several lines give the same cost, and for \(k \ge 2\) the solver adds lines whose opening changes nothing), so the "switched lines" column should be read as one optimal set among many. Every MILP, 41 binaries included, solves to a zero gap in a fraction of a second: on a 30-bus grid HiGHS's branch and bound sees nothing of the exponential growth, which only shows in the brute-force counts of step 4: with 41 binaries and no N-1 rows the model has a few hundred rows, and the solver's LP bounds prune almost all of the tree. The scaling appears on grids with hundreds of lines and N-1 constraints, where the literature of the chapter reports hours or time-outs; with 20 switchable lines and \(k = 3\) the enumeration already has 1,351 topologies, with 41 lines 11,522.

      ieee30_ots.py (switching MILP and brute force)
      def big_m(case: dict, mode: str = 'global') -> np.ndarray:
    """A bound on |b_l (theta_i - theta_j) - f_l| when line ``l`` is open: ``b_l`` times a bound on the angle gap.

    ``'global'``: the angle gap is at most the sum over every other line of rating / b (a simple path uses each
    line at most once), valid for every connected topology. ``'cycle'``: the shortest path around the line in the
    intact network with weights rating / b, the bound of Exercise 1, valid while every other line stays closed.
    """
    rating_over_b = case['rating'] / case['b']
    if mode == 'global':
        return case['b'] * (rating_over_b.sum() - rating_over_b)
    import networkx as nx  # noqa: PLC0415

    g = nx.Graph()
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(case['lines']):
        g.add_edge(int(i), int(j), weight=rating_over_b[k], key=k)
    bound = big_m(case, 'global')  # a bridge has no cycle: its opening islands the grid, keep the global bound
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(case['lines']):
        h = g.copy()
        h.remove_edge(int(i), int(j))
        if nx.has_path(h, int(i), int(j)):
            bound[k] = case['b'][k] * nx.shortest_path_length(h, int(i), int(j), weight='weight')
    return bound


def ots(case: dict, k_max: int, switchable: list[int], time_limit: float = TIME_LIMIT, mode: str = 'global') -> dict:
    """Optimal transmission switching: at most ``k_max`` of the ``switchable`` lines open, big-M formulation."""
    m, v = _base_model(case, 'ots')
    big = big_m(case, mode)
    z = {k: m.add_variable(f'z_{k}', cat='Binary') for k in switchable}
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(case['lines']):
        gap = case['b'][k] * (v['theta'][i] - v['theta'][j]) - v['f'][k]
        if k in z:
            m += v['f'][k] <= case['rating'][k] * z[k]
            m += -v['f'][k] <= case['rating'][k] * z[k]
            m += gap <= big[k] * (1 - z[k])
            m += -gap <= big[k] * (1 - z[k])
        else:
            m += gap == 0
    m += pulp.lpSum(1 - z[k] for k in switchable) <= k_max
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    m.solve(pulp.HiGHS(msg=False, timeLimit=time_limit))
    info = m.solverModel.getInfo()
    return {
        'status': pulp.LpStatus[m.status],
        'cost': pulp.value(m.objective),
        'switched': sorted(k for k in switchable if z[k].value() is not None and z[k].value() < 0.5),
        'time_s': time.perf_counter() - t0,
        'gap': info.mip_gap,
        'p': np.array([x.value() for x in v['p']]),
    }


def sweep(case: dict, ranked: list[int], time_limit: float = TIME_LIMIT) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Lab A step 2: k = 1, 2, 3 for the 5, 10, 20 and all 41 switchable lines (first entries of ``ranked``)."""
    rows = []
    for n_switchable in (5, 10, 20, 41):
        for k in (1, 2, 3):
            r = ots(case, k, ranked[:n_switchable], time_limit)
            rows.append(
                {'switchable': n_switchable, 'k': k, **{key: r[key] for key in ('cost', 'switched', 'time_s', 'gap')}}
            )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def brute_force(case: dict, k_max: int, switchable: list[int]) -> tuple[float, tuple[int, ...], int, float]:
    """One DC-OPF per set of at most ``k_max`` open lines: best cost, its set, the number of LPs and the time."""
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    best, arg, count = np.inf, (), 0
    for k in range(k_max + 1):
        for subset in itertools.combinations(switchable, k):
            count += 1
            reduced = _without(case, subset)
            if reduced is None:
                continue
            r = dc_opf(reduced)
            if r['status'] == 'Optimal' and r['cost'] < best - 1e-6:
                best, arg = r['cost'], subset
    return best, arg, count, time.perf_counter() - t0


def _without(case: dict, switched: tuple[int, ...]) -> dict | None:
    keep = np.ones(len(case['lines']), dtype=bool)
    keep[list(switched)] = False
    if ch27_ieee30.ptdf(case, keep) is None:
        return None
    return {
        **case,
        'lines': case['lines'][keep],
        'x': case['x'][keep],
        'b': case['b'][keep],
        'rating': case['rating'][keep],
    }

    3. Lazy N-1 constraints. For a fixed topology the post-outage flow is linear in the base flows, \(f_k^{(c)} = f_k + \mathrm{LODF}_{kc} f_c\), so a contingency adds \(2(n_\ell - 1)\) linear rows to the LP. The DC-OPF dispatch has a worst post-contingency loading of 155 % (22–24 after the outage of 27–28); the five worst contingencies by that measure are 27–28, 21–22, 6–8, 6–28 and 10–20. With those five the SCOPF costs 3357.6 $/h and leaves one violation, 101.8 % on a line after the outage of 22–24 itself (index 30); adding it gives 3369.2 $/h with the dispatch (48.9, 80, 43.0, 17.2, 0, 0) and no violation: two iterations, six contingencies, instead of forty-one. The same LP with every non-bridge contingency at once gives the same 3369.2 $/h, so the lazy loop lost nothing. Security costs 268 $/h here (8.6 %), far more than switching saves, which is why the chapter treats the SCOPF as the yardstick. With switching the LODFs would depend on the topology, so N-1 constraints inside the MILP need the angle formulation per contingency (one copy of the network per outage) or a shift-factor formulation that recomputes the factors per candidate topology (Exercise 3); the lazy loop still applies, with a MILP solve per iteration.

      ieee30_ots.py (LODF constraints and the lazy loop)
      def _lodf(case: dict) -> np.ndarray:
    """LODF matrix (line, outage) of the intact network; a bridge outage gets a column of NaN (its own entry -1)."""
    h = ch27_ieee30.ptdf(case, np.ones(len(case['lines']), dtype=bool))
    hk = h[:, case['lines'][:, 0]] - h[:, case['lines'][:, 1]]
    denominator = 1 - np.diag(hk)
    with np.errstate(divide='ignore', invalid='ignore'):
        lodf = np.where(np.abs(denominator) > 1e-9, hk / denominator, np.nan)
    np.fill_diagonal(lodf, -1.0)
    return lodf


def post_contingency_loading(case: dict, f: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Loading in percent after every single outage, shape (outage, line); a bridge gives a row of NaN."""
    lodf = _lodf(case)
    post = f[None, :] + lodf.T * f[:, None]  # (outage, line)
    return 100 * np.abs(post) / case['rating']


def lazy_scopf(case: dict, n_initial: int = 5) -> tuple[dict, list[dict]]:
    """Lab A step 3: start with the ``n_initial`` worst contingencies, add violated ones until none is left."""
    base = dc_opf(case)
    loading = post_contingency_loading(case, base['f'])
    worst = np.nan_to_num(loading, nan=0).max(axis=1)
    active = list(np.argsort(-worst)[:n_initial])
    history = []
    while True:
        r = dc_opf(case, active)
        loading = post_contingency_loading(case, r['f'])
        worst = np.nan_to_num(loading, nan=0).max(axis=1)
        violated = [int(c) for c in np.flatnonzero(worst > 100 + 1e-6) if c not in active]
        history.append(
            {'constraints': len(active), 'cost': r['cost'], 'worst_loading': worst.max(), 'violated': violated}
        )
        if not violated:
            return r, history
        active.append(violated[int(np.argmax(worst[violated]))])

    4. Comparison. Brute force over every set of at most two open lines solves 862 LPs in 3.1 s and finds the same 3084.0 $/h (one line, 4–6, is enough; another of the degenerate optima); the MILP with \(k = 2\) and all 41 lines takes 0.1 s. For \(|D| \le 3\) the enumeration needs 11,522 LPs (about 40 s at this rate) and the MILP still a fraction of a second: the exact method wins by three orders of magnitude at this size, and a GA with the same budget as in Chapter 24 cannot beat an exact solver that finishes in 0.1 s. The Chapter 24 brute-force and GA figures of your own run and ToOp's total_branch_combis divided by its run time in res.json were not available here; ToOp's DC solver evaluates batches of thousands of topologies per second on a GPU, but with a different objective (N-1 overload energy of a fixed dispatch, no cost) and without optimality proof, so the comparison is between an exact answer to a small LP-based question and a fast approximate answer to a larger N-1 question.

      acceptance.py (the run table)
      METRICS = ['non_converging_loadflows', 'disconnected_branches', 'overload_energy_n_1', 'critical_branch_count_n_1']


def run_table(run: Path) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Lab B1: one row per ``topology_N/ac_metrics.json`` with the metrics, every check by hand and the verdict."""
    unsplit = json.loads((run / 'unsplit_ac_metrics.json').read_text(encoding='utf-8'))['extra_scores']
    rows = []
    for folder in sorted(run.glob('topology_*'), key=lambda p: int(p.name.split('_')[1])):
        res = json.loads((folder / 'ac_metrics.json').read_text(encoding='utf-8'))
        s = res['extra_scores']
        row = {'topology': folder.name, 'ac_fitness': res['fitness'], **{m: s.get(m, 0) for m in METRICS}}
        row['dc_fitness'] = res.get('dc_info', {}).get('metrics', {}).get('fitness')
        row['convergence_ok'] = s.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0) - s.get(
            'disconnected_branches', 0
        ) <= unsplit.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0)
        row['overload_ok'] = s.get('overload_energy_n_1', 99999) <= 0.95 * unsplit.get('overload_energy_n_1', 0)
        row['critical_ok'] = s.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 0) <= 1.1 * unsplit.get(
            'critical_branch_count_n_1', 999
        )
        verdict = evaluate_acceptance(s, unsplit)
        row['rejected_by'] = None if verdict is None else verdict['criterion']
        rows.append(row)
    table = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    table['dc_rank'] = (
        table['dc_fitness'].rank(ascending=False, method='first') if table['dc_fitness'].notna().any() else None
    )
    return table

    2. Probe the traps. acceptance.py runs them (ToOp's own function returns the same objects in the test):

      • Zero baseline: unsplit overload_energy_n_1 = 0.0, candidate 0.01 → rejected, overload-energy, value_after = 0.01, value_before = 0.0, threshold 0.95. With an unsplit value of 1.0 MW the same candidate is accepted. A multiplicative threshold on a zero baseline rejects every positive value, so on a worst-k subset where the unsplit grid happens to be clean no candidate can pass.
      • Convergence before overload: unsplit non_converging_loadflows = 1, overload_energy_n_1 = 0.0; candidate 0 non-converging, 5.0 MW → rejected, overload-energy, 5.0 against 0.0. The candidate made the diverging contingency converge and thereby revealed 5 MW of overload the unsplit run never computed; the rule compares that against the unsplit "zero" and rejects an improvement (docs/wall_of_shame.md).
      • The other order: unsplit 1 non-converging and 100 MW; candidate 2 non-converging and 1.0 MW → rejected, convergence, 2 against 1. The convergence check runs first, so a 99 % overload reduction is never looked at when one more case diverges.
      acceptance.py (the rule)
      def evaluate_acceptance(
    split: dict, unsplit: dict, critical_voltage: bool = False, thresholds: dict = THRESHOLDS
) -> dict | None:
    """``None`` when the candidate is accepted, else the first failed check with its values and threshold.

    ``split`` and ``unsplit`` are ``extra_scores`` dicts. The checks run in ToOp's order: non-converging loadflows
    (minus the candidate's disconnected branches), N-1 overload energy, critical branch count and, only with
    ``critical_voltage``, voltage jumps and angle differences. Every threshold multiplies the unsplit value, and
    a metric missing from ``split`` or ``unsplit`` takes ToOp's defaults (99999 for the split overload, 999 for
    the unsplit counts, 0 otherwise).
    """
    checks = [
        (
            'convergence',
            split.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0) - split.get('disconnected_branches', 0),
            unsplit.get('non_converging_loadflows', 0),
        ),
        ('overload-energy', split.get('overload_energy_n_1', 99999), unsplit.get('overload_energy_n_1', 0)),
        (
            'critical-branch-count',
            split.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 0),
            unsplit.get('critical_branch_count_n_1', 999),
        ),
    ]
    if critical_voltage:
        checks += [
            ('voltage-magnitude', split.get('voltage_jump_count_n_1', 0), unsplit.get('voltage_jump_count_n_1', 999)),
            (
                'voltage-angle',
                split.get('critical_va_diff_count_n_1', 0),
                unsplit.get('critical_va_diff_count_n_1', 999),
            ),
        ]
    for criterion, after, before in checks:
        if not after <= before * thresholds[criterion]:
            return {
                'criterion': criterion,
                'value_after': float(after),
                'value_before': float(before),
                'threshold': thresholds[criterion],
            }
    return None

    Exercise 1¶

    Let line \(\ell = (i, j)\) with susceptance \(b_\ell\) lie on a cycle whose other \(m\) lines \(k = 1, \dots, m\) have ratings \(\bar f_k\) and susceptances \(b_k\). When \(\ell\) is open the two voltage angles are still linked by the rest of the cycle: going from \(i\) to \(j\) along it,

    \[ \theta_i - \theta_j = \sum_{k=1}^{m} \pm(\theta_{k}^{+} - \theta_{k}^{-}) = \sum_{k=1}^{m} \pm \frac{f_k}{b_k}, \qquad |\theta_i - \theta_j| \le \sum_{k=1}^{m} \frac{\bar f_k}{b_k}, \]

    because every line of the path respects its rating in any feasible point. The constraint \(-M_\ell(1 - z_\ell) \le b_\ell(\theta_i - \theta_j) - f_\ell \le M_\ell(1 - z_\ell)\) must not cut off any feasible angle pair when \(z_\ell = 0\) (then \(f_\ell = 0\)), so

    \[ M_\ell = b_\ell \sum_{k=1}^{m} \frac{\bar f_k}{b_k} \]

    is valid, and it is the smallest valid value when the rest of the cycle is the only path between \(i\) and \(j\). In the 3-bus ring of Exercise 2 (equal reactances 0.1 p.u., ratings 200, 200 and 100 MW) the bound for line 1–3 is \(M = b (200/b + 200/b) = 400\) MW; at the DC-OPF optimum with that line open the angle gap \(b(\theta_1 - \theta_3)\) is 360 MW, inside the bound (braess3.big_m_cycle, gap_when_open). A bound over all topologies is harder because the other lines may be open too: the cycle may not exist, and the angle gap is then set by whichever path survives, so the valid \(M_\ell\) is the maximum over all connected topologies of the shortest weighted path between \(i\) and \(j\) with weights \(\bar f_k / b_k\), a combinatorial quantity. The safe choice is the sum over every other line, \(b_\ell \sum_{k \ne \ell} \bar f_k / b_k\) (a simple path uses each line at most once), which ieee30_ots.big_m(case, 'global') uses and which is 10 to 100 times looser than the cycle bound (520 to 16,037 MW against 16 to 2,231 MW on IEEE 30): a loose \(M\) weakens the LP relaxation and slows the MILP, as in Chapter 23.

    Exercise 2¶

    Three buses, three lines of equal reactance (1–2, 2–3 and 1–3, ratings 200, 200 and 100 MW), a 10 $/MWh generator at bus 1, a 50 $/MWh generator at bus 3 and 180 MW of load at bus 3. With every line closed, two thirds of a transfer from bus 1 to bus 3 take the direct line 1–3 and one third the path 1–2–3, so line 1–3 reaches its 100 MW rating when bus 1 produces 150 MW; the DC-OPF dispatches (150, 30) MW at 3000 $/h, flows (50, 50, 100) MW. With line 1–3 open, all of bus 1's output takes 1–2–3, each line can carry 200 MW, and the DC-OPF dispatches (180, 0) MW at 1800 $/h: opening a line lowers the cost by 40 %. The dual values explain it. Closed: LMPs (10, 30, 50) $/MWh; the price at bus 3 is the cost of the expensive unit, which is marginal there, and bus 2 sits at 30 because a MW injected at bus 2 reaches bus 3 one third through 2–3 directly and two thirds via 2–1–3, loading the congested line, so its value is a mix of the two generator prices. Open: LMPs (10, 10, 10), one price, the cheap unit marginal everywhere, no congestion rent. The 1200 $/h saved are the 30 MW that the congestion forced onto the expensive unit times the 40 $/MWh price difference. As in Braess's road-network paradox, removing a link helps because flows follow impedances (Kirchhoff), not the cheapest path: the direct line attracts two thirds of the transfer whether or not that is useful, and the DC-OPF has exactly that structure.

    Exercise 3¶

    • SOCP / QC / SDP relaxation of the AC equations. Used to validate, a relaxation gives a lower bound on the AC cost or a proof of infeasibility of a candidate topology in seconds, without a Newton solve that may diverge; it would complement the AC stage by rejecting infeasible candidates early rather than compete with the DC stage. Used to seed, the relaxation's angles and voltages are a better starting point than the DC flows, but a relaxation can be inexact (its solution need not correspond to a real operating point), so it cannot replace the AC validation that ToOp runs on the realized topology.
    • Shift-factor (PTDF/LODF) MILP. It would replace ToOp's DC search: the same linear model ToOp evaluates by the million, written as a compact MILP without angle variables, solved to proven optimality for a small number of switching actions. It competes with the DC repertoire directly, with a guarantee instead of a population, and loses when the action set is busbar splits (the factors change per candidate topology and must be regenerated, which is what ToOp's BSDF/LODF updates do inside the solver).
    • Lazy N-1 constraint generation. It would keep the exact method tractable: only the contingencies that actually bind are in the model (six of forty-one in Lab A3). In ToOp the analogue is the worst-k early stopping of the AC stage (n_worst_contingencies), which also looks at a subset first; a lazy exact method would compete with the DC stage for the proposal of candidates and with the early-stopping heuristic for the way contingencies are prioritized.
    • Benders decomposition. The master picks the topology (binaries), the subproblem is the DC-OPF or the N-1 check for that topology, and its duals return a cut to the master; the subproblem is exactly what ToOp's solver evaluates, so Benders would turn ToOp's evaluator into the oracle of an exact search and compete with the evolutionary DC stage. Its cost is one LP per candidate and a master MILP that grows with the cuts, so it scales to hundreds, not millions, of candidates.

    Exercise 4¶

    The question. The day-ahead grid has an N-1 overload (line \(\ell\) after outage \(c\)); the operator wants the switching actions that remove it without creating others, by tomorrow morning.

    MILP with lazy N-1 constraints. Formulation: DC-OPF or fixed-dispatch feasibility with binaries per switchable line or busbar coupler, the big-M constraints of the worked example, and the post-contingency flow limits added only for the contingencies found violated (Lab A3). Scalability: exact for tens of switchable elements and a handful of binding contingencies (IEEE 30 solves in a fraction of a second); grows exponentially in the worst case, and node-breaker busbar models multiply the binaries, so real grids need decomposition or a restricted candidate list and still risk time-outs. Guarantees: optimality and infeasibility proofs for the DC model and the contingencies in the set; nothing about contingencies not yet added, nothing about AC. AC feasibility: none; the DC model ignores reactive power, voltages and losses, so every solution needs an AC N-1 check afterwards, like ToOp's. Interpretability: high for the result (a list of switches with a proven cost) and low for the reasoning (duals and cuts are not an operator's language), and a MILP's answer changes abruptly with the data.

    Rule-based expert system. Rules of the form "if line \(\ell\) overloads after \(c\), try the splits of the substations adjacent to \(\ell\), then the reconnection of parallel lines", encoded from operating experience and checked by simulation before being proposed. Scalability: linear in the number of rules and simulations; real-time capable; coverage limited to situations someone has written a rule for, and maintenance grows with the grid. Guarantees: none of optimality; what it proposes has been simulated, so it is at least feasible in the model used. AC feasibility: good when the rules are checked with an AC tool, which operators insist on. Interpretability: the best of the three: every proposal comes with its rule, in the operator's vocabulary.

    ToOp. A GPU DC solver evaluates millions of topologies, a MAP-Elites repertoire keeps the best per (number of splits, switching distance), and an AC stage re-validates the best cells with an acceptance rule (Lab B). Scalability: the DC stage scales with GPU throughput, not with the number of binaries, and handles busbar splits natively through BSDF/LODF updates; the AC stage is the bottleneck and is throttled by worst-k early stopping. Guarantees: none; an evolutionary search can miss the optimum and its result depends on seed and budget (Chapter 26), and the acceptance rule has the traps of Lab B2. AC feasibility: built in, every proposal that reaches the operator passed a full AC N-1 with PowSyBl; the price is the mismatch between DC and AC metrics (the DC winner can be rejected). Interpretability: medium: the output is a concrete list of switch operations (orao_summary.json) with before/after metrics and diagrams, but why this split works is not explained, and the weighted-sum fitness hides trade-offs (Chapter 27).

    Verdict. For a next-day question with a few known overloads and a small candidate list, the MILP gives the defensible answer and the expert system the fastest; ToOp is the tool when the candidate space is large (many substations, splits rather than line switching) and AC feasibility must be part of the search rather than a check afterwards. A reasonable process uses ToOp or the MILP to propose, the AC N-1 to validate, and keeps the rule base as the operator's sanity check and the fallback when the optimizers time out.

    Exercise 5¶

    The checks run in ToOp's order (acceptance.py reproduces it; ToOp's own function returns the same in the test):

    1. Convergence: candidate \(1 - 0 = 1\) non-converging loadflow \(\le 1.0 \times 2\): passes.
    2. Overload energy: \(0.4 \le 0.95 \times 0.0 = 0\): fails. The function returns criterion='overload-energy', value_after=0.4, value_before=0.0, threshold=0.95.
    3. Critical branch count (\(1 \le 1.1 \times 0 = 0\)) would fail too, but is never reached.

    This is the zero-baseline trap: the unsplit grid has no N-1 overload (perhaps only on the worst-k subset being evaluated), so any candidate with a positive overload is rejected, however small, even though it also made one diverging contingency converge, which is the improvement the operator would most want. Two changes make the rule robust. First, compare with an absolute tolerance as well: accept when split <= max(threshold * unsplit, absolute) with an absolute allowance in MW and in branches (for instance 1 MW and 1 branch), so that a zero baseline does not become an impossible target. Second, give the convergence improvement credit: when the candidate converges on cases the unsplit grid did not, compare the overload only on the cases both runs computed, or recompute the unsplit baseline on the candidate's converging set; ToOp's docs/wall_of_shame.md records this exact failure. The unused early_stopping_non_convergence_percentage_threshold parameter suggests such a rule was planned.
