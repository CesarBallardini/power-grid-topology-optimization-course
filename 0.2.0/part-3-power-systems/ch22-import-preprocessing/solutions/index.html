    Chapter 22 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 22. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: grid_pipeline_toop.py (the Lab, runs with ToOp's interpreter), action_count.py and bridge_lookup.py (Exercise 1), node_reduction.py (Exercise 2), loadflow_limits.py (Exercise 3), se_case14.py (Exercise 4) and dc_state_estimation.py (Exercise 5). Each module's main() prints every number quoted below; the ToOp numbers were produced with ToOp ba1874e on CPU (Windows, outside the container).

    Lab¶

    python grid_pipeline_toop.py <folder> runs the six steps; the functions named below are in that module and lab_toop.ipynb calls them. Running ToOp in place would rewrite data/grid_node_breaker/grid/, so everything happens in a copy.

    1. Import and preprocess. import_grid() copies grid.xiidm (five 225 kV node-breaker voltage levels, nine lines of equal reactance, three generators, two loads, 106 switches), builds prepare_importer_parameters(grid_file, data_folder) with area_settings.cutoff_voltage = 10 and runs convert_file. The importer writes grid.xiidm, importer_auxiliary_data.json, initial_topology/asset_topology_master_data.json and initial_topology/original_gridfile/grid.xiidm, loadflow_parameters.json, the masks/ folder and nminus1_definition.json, in about two seconds. run_preprocessing(importer_parameters, data_folder, PreprocessParameters(), is_pandapower_net=False) (or funnel(), which calls load_grid directly) adds action_set.json, action_set_diffs.hdf5, static_information.hdf5 and static_information_stats.json. During import the log says why six lines leave the reward mask (excluded_branch_ids=['L4', ..., 'L9'] reason=missing_operational_limits: only L1–L3 carry a permanent limit, 90 A) and warns that VLevel5 has no couplers.
    2. Inspect the folder. mask_counts() tabulates the True entries: relevant_subs 2 of 5 buses (VL2_0 and VL3_0, the two voltage levels with at least two busbars and four branches; importer_auxiliary_data.json names them under id_lists.relevant_subs), line_for_nminus1 9, line_for_reward 3, line_disconnectable 9, generator_for_nminus1 3, load_for_nminus1 2, busbar_for_nminus1 8 of 11 busbar sections, switch_for_nminus1 106 (every switch), switch_for_reward 0; the 17 other masks are all False or empty (no transformers, tie lines or boundary lines). nminus1_definition.json has 5 monitored elements (the three lines with limits and the two relevant buses) and 129 contingencies: BASECASE, 9 lines, 3 generators, 2 loads, 8 busbars and 106 switches. static_information_stats.json after preprocessing: n_relevant_subs 1, n_actions 10, n_monitored_branches 3, n_nminus1_cases 11 (8 branch outages, 3 injection outages; the switch and busbar contingencies are not part of the DC solver's N-1), overload_energy_n0 0.0 and overload_energy_n1 61.44 MW. Counting actions per bus_group_id needs both files: action_set.json stores the stations without their actions, which live as diffs in action_set_diffs.hdf5; load_action_set(json, hdf5) expands them (actions_per_station()). The only relevant station, VL2_a, has \(d = 5\) branches (L1, L2, L6, L7, L8) and 10 actions against the bound \(2^{d-1} - d = 11\); the eleventh assignment is removed by the BSDF/LODF filter (step 4). VL3_a has 4 branches (bound 4), VL4_a 3 and VL5_a 1, and none of them is relevant after preprocessing.
    3. Watch the funnel. funnel() passes record(stage, message, *, stats=None) to load_grid and turns the records into a table (28 stages carry stats, 9 more do not). Nothing changes in n_branches (9). The stages that remove the most: assert_network_data for the relevant substations (2 → 1: the log says Removed 1 from relevant nodes. Reason: Less than 4 non-bridge branches connected ... VL3_0; the filter runs in filter_relevant_nodes and the drop shows up in the stats of the next stage), reduce_branch_dimension for the disconnectable branches (9 → 8: L9, the bridge to VL5, left the outage masks one stage earlier and now leaves the disconnectable set), and enumerate_station_realizations for the actions (0 → 10, the only stage that creates them). n_nodes grows, 5 → 6 → 7: combine_phaseshift_and_injection adds the reduced-node column and add_bus_b_columns_to_ptdf the bus-B column of the relevant station. The whole load_grid takes 4 to 5 s here, most of it JAX compilation.

The times are dominated by JAX compilation, not by the filters, on a grid this small.     Exercise 1¶

    Enumeration: \(2^{d-1} = 32\) bit patterns for \(d = 6\) (each A/B assignment once, up to swapping the busbars). Isolation removal drops the patterns with one branch alone on a busbar, \(\binom{6}{1} = 6\) of them, leaving \(2^{5} - 6 = 26\): the unsplit configuration, 15 splits of type 2|4 and 10 of type 3|3. The bridge lookup requires at least two non-bridge branches on every busbar in use. With one bridge \(b_0\), the splits that fail are those whose busbar holding \(b_0\) contains exactly one other branch: \(b_0\) paired with each of the five others, 5 assignments (bridge_lookup.funnel() lists them as b0+b1, b0+b2, b0+b3, b0+b4, b0+b5). The 3|3 splits with the bridge keep two non-bridge branches beside it and pass. So 32 → 26 → 21 actions, the unsplit one included (20 splits). The reason: after the outage of a non-bridge branch on that busbar, the bridge would be the busbar's only link, so the LODF of that outage is undefined (the busbar islands); the lookup removes these cases without running a single load flow, and is_valid_bsdf_lodf would otherwise catch them at a much higher price.

    Exercise 2¶

    node_reduction.py uses a ten-bus ring with two chords (unit reactances), reference injections summing to zero and four significant buses {0, 2, 5, 7}. reduce_ptdf() keeps the four PTDF columns and appends one column, \(\text{PTDF}_{\cdot,\text{insig}}\,p_\text{insig}\), the flow every branch receives from the six collapsed buses at their reference injections; the reduced injection vector is \((p_0, p_2, p_5, p_7, 1)\). At the reference the reduced model reproduces all twelve flows to \(10^{-14}\) MW: the full product \(Hp\) was simply split into two sums and the second one precomputed. After moving 20 MW of load to collapsed bus 3 (balanced by the slack) the reduced model is wrong by up to 11.5 MW on a branch, because the precomputed column still carries bus 3's old injection; a change at significant bus 2 is reproduced exactly. This is acceptable for ToOp because the optimizer never changes injections: per timestep they are inputs, fixed when load_grid builds the static information, and only the topology at relevant substations varies, which touches the kept columns (and their bus-B copies). The reduction trades a column per insignificant bus for one column, which is what makes the PTDF of a continental grid fit on a GPU; the price is that a new timestep needs a new precomputed column, which is why the column is stored per timestep.

    Exercise 3¶

    For \(I_\text{old} = 1\,000\) A, \(f = 1.2\), \(m = 0.05\) the rule \(I_\text{new} = \operatorname{clip}(f I_\text{lf},\ \min(I_\text{old}, I_\text{lf} + m I_\text{old}),\ I_\text{old})\) has three regimes (loadflow_limits.breakpoints()): below \(I_\text{lf} = m I_\text{old}/(f-1) = 250\) A the lower clip is active and \(I_\text{new} = I_\text{lf} + 50\) A; between 250 A and \(I_\text{old}/f = 833.3\) A the factor rules, \(I_\text{new} = 1.2\,I_\text{lf}\); above 833.3 A the upper clip returns the old limit, 1 000 A. With the N-1 factor \(f = 1.4\) the breakpoints move to 125 A and 714.3 A. The plot is a broken line through (0, 50), (250, 300), (833.3, 1000) and flat afterwards, always below the old limit. A border line that is unloaded in the base case gets \(I_\text{new} = m I_\text{old} = 50\) A, 5 % of its rating: ToOp will treat any flow above 50 A on it as an overload, which is the intended behaviour (the neighbouring TSO's lines are not to be loaded by this TSO's remedial actions) but also a trap when a line happens to be out of service or lightly loaded in the forecast. new_limit(100) = 150, new_limit(500) = 600, new_limit(900) = 1000.

    Exercise 4¶

    se_case14.py builds 82 measurements on case14 after runpp: 14 voltage magnitudes (\(\sigma = 1\,\%\)), p and q at every bus and at the from (hv) side of the 15 lines and 5 transformers (\(\sigma = 2\,\%\) of the value, at least 0.1). Two pandapower conventions matter: bus powers are consumption (res_bus, load positive) and must exclude the shunt at bus 8, which the estimator models as a network element (with the shunt included, even noise-free data give 0.005 pu of voltage error and a failed chi-square test). With exact data the estimate reproduces the power flow to \(10^{-11}\) pu and chi2 is False; with noisy data (seed 14) the largest errors are 0.0015 pu and 0.09°, and chi2_analysis passes. Scaling the active-power measurement of line 0 (measurement 42, from side) by 1.3 makes chi2_analysis return True; the largest normalized residual is that measurement's, \(r_N = 15.2\) (the next one is −3.1), and remove_bad_data(rn_max_threshold=3) drops exactly measurement 42, after which the test passes again. The topology error opens line 8 (buses 5–11) in the model while the measurements still describe it in service: chi2 is True and the six largest normalized residuals all touch the opened line: its own p and q measurements (48.9 and 25.5: the estimator predicts zero flow on a line it believes open, the meters say 7.6 MW), the injections at bus 11 (37.2 and 13.0) and the flows of line 13 (12–13; 30.1 and 11.9), the path that now has to feed bus 11 in the model. A single bad meter produces one dominant residual; a wrong breaker status produces a cluster of residuals around one station, which is how topology errors are told from bad data. The pandapower estimation code emits pandas' SettingWithCopyWarning and, under Python 3.14, a DeprecationWarning for ~bool; quiet_estimation() silences both around the library calls, since the test suite treats warnings as errors.

    Exercise 5¶

    For the three-bus ring of Chapter 16 (\(x = 0.1, 0.1, 0.2\), \(p = (100, 0, -100)\) MW, slack bus 0) the measurement model is \(z = H\theta + e\) with \(\theta = (\theta_1, \theta_2)\), five rows (the flows \(b_k(\theta_f - \theta_t)\) of the three branches and the injections at buses 1 and 2, i.e. rows of \(B_\text{bus}\)) and \(\sigma = 2\) MW for the flow meters, 3 MW for the injection meters. wls() solves the normal equations \(\hat\theta = (H^\top W H)^{-1} H^\top W z\), \(W = \operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma_i^2)\), and returns the residuals \(r = z - H\hat\theta\) and \(J = \sum_i r_i^2/\sigma_i^2\). Noise-free, the true flows (50, 50, 50) and injections (0, −100) are recovered exactly with \(J = 0\). With one noisy sample (seed 16): \(z = (48.8, 51.3, 52.1, 3.1, -94.5)\), \(\hat\theta = (-4.85, -9.86)\) against \((-5, -10)\), residuals \((0.33, 1.17, 2.79, 1.48, 4.83)\) and \(J = 5.16\), below the 95 % chi-square threshold with \(m - n = 3\) degrees of freedom, \(\chi^2_{0.95,3} = 7.815\): the model and the data agree. Adding a 30 MW error to the first flow meter gives \(J = 110.0\), far above the threshold, and the largest normalized residual points at measurement 0. Over 2 000 noisy samples the mean of \(J\) is 3.0, the expected value of \(\chi^2_3\) (test_dc_state_estimation.py), which is the justification of the test: \(J\) is a chi-square sample only when \(H\) is the right model and the \(\sigma_i\) are right.
