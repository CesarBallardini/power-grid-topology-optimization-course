    Chapter 21 – Solutions¶

    Solutions of the Lab and the Exercises of Chapter 21. Try each task before reading its solution. The code is in the chapter's src folder: ieee14_flow_based.py (the Lab, NumPy and pypowsybl), curative_timing.py, domain_2d.py, glsk_sensitivity.py and time_coupling.py (Exercises 1, 2, 3 and 6) and orao_switches_toop.py (Exercise 5, ToOp). Each module's main() prints every number quoted below; the ToOp numbers were produced with ToOp ba1874e on CPU.

    Lab¶

    python ieee14_flow_based.py runs the six steps on the base topology and on two topology actions. The functions named below are in that module.

    1. IEEE 14 and its nodal PTDF. load_ieee14() reads buses, lines, transformers, generators and loads from pypowsybl.network.create_ieee14() and ptdf_matrix() builds \(H\) as in Chapter 17 with bus 1 as slack. Two details decide whether H @ p matches pypowsybl's DC flows: the per-unit base of the reactances, which pypowsybl gives in ohms (a line between two nominal voltages \(V_1\), \(V_2\) uses \(V_1 V_2 / S_\text{base}\); a transformer uses the square of its side-2 voltage, where its ohms are expressed), and the transformer ratio. pypowsybl's default DC load flow keeps the off-nominal ratios \(\rho = (U_{1,\text{rated}}/V_1)/(U_{2,\text{rated}}/V_2)\) (0.978, 0.969 and 0.932 for the three transformers), which multiplies their reactance by \(\rho\) in the DC equations; with Parameters(distributed_slack=False, dc_use_transformer_ratio=False) the textbook model applies and H @ p reproduces every pypowsybl flow to \(2.6 \times 10^{-13}\) MW. The injection vector must be balanced: the data have 272.4 MW of generation set points against 259 MW of load, and in a lossless DC model the slack generator at bus 1 produces 219.0 MW, not its 232.4 MW set point. The net positions at the reference point are then \(NP_A = 87.7\), \(NP_B = -34.3\), \(NP_C = -53.4\) MW (zones A = {1–5}, B = {6, 11, 12, 13}, C = {7, 8, 9, 10, 14}), summing to zero.
    2. Zones and GLSKs. glsk(grid, 'generation') spreads a zone's net position over its generators in proportion to target_p: zone A gets (0.853, 0.147, 0, 0, 0) over buses 1–5 (232.4 and 40 MW at buses 1 and 2; the 0 MW generators at buses 3, 6 and 8 get no weight). Zones B and C have no generation in the base case, so the generation GLSK falls back to flat there: 0.25 on each of buses 6, 11, 12, 13 and 0.2 on each of buses 7, 8, 9, 10, 14. glsk(grid, 'flat') is flat in all three zones. Every column sums to one.

For zone A with the generation GLSK, pypowsybl_zone_factors() builds the same zone with create_zone_from_injections_and_shift_keys('A', ['B1-G', 'B2-G'], [0.853, 0.147]), runs the DC sensitivity analysis with the parameters of step 1 and reads get_sensitivity_matrix(): the 20 factors agree with the NumPy column to \(5.6 \times 10^{-17}\) (the zone factor is the shift-key-weighted sum of the injection factors, and the injection factors are the PTDF columns).       The negative RAM of L3-4-1 in the negative direction means that the point of zero net positions is outside the domain: with no exchange at all the line would carry 36 MW from bus 4 to bus 3, above its 31.4 MW stand-in limit. The market must trade (zone A exporting, as it does at the reference point) to keep this line within its limit. Every RAM is positive at the reference point by construction, since \(|F_\text{ref}| \le F_\max/1.3\) and \(F_\max - \text{FRM} = 0.95\,F_\max\). 5. The domain. With \(NP_C = -NP_A - NP_B\) each CNEC is a half-plane \((\text{PTDF}_A - \text{PTDF}_C)\,NP_A + (\text{PTDF}_B - \text{PTDF}_C)\,NP_B \le \text{RAM}\) (half_planes()). domain_vertices() intersects the boundary lines pairwise and keeps the points that satisfy all 40 constraints: a pentagon with vertices (52.3, −76.3), (70.7, −83.1), (107.8, −47.4), (79.9, 9.5), (41.4, 23.7), whose edges belong to L10-11-1 (both directions), T4-7-1+, T5-6-1+ and L3-4-1− (binding_cnecs()). The reference point (87.7, −34.3) is inside.       Only A→B gains: the line 13–14 was the parallel path between zones B and C, so every exchange that used it loses capacity, B→C most of all. The N-1 check of Chapter 19 (security_metrics(), LODF over every non-bridge outage, loading against the same \(F_\max\)) says: base topology worst N-0 loading 76.9 % (that is \(1/1.3\), by construction), worst N-1 loading 300 % on L3-4-1 after the outage of L2-3-1, N-1 overload energy 382.8 MW; with L13-14 open the N-0 worst rises to 81.1 %, the worst N-1 case is the same 300 % and the overload energy grows to 414.7 MW. The stand-in limits of step 4 make the base topology itself N-1 insecure, so the check is a comparison: the action makes N-1 security worse and would not pass. The module also tries line 3–4, the action that enlarges four of the six margins: it leaves no domain at all. Bus 3 becomes radial, so L2-3-1 carries the whole 94.2 MW load of bus 3 whatever the net positions (zonal PTDFs exactly zero), above its 91.1 MW limit: \(\text{RAM} = -7.7\) MW with zero sensitivity is a constraint no exchange can satisfy. In N-0 the line is at 103.4 %, so this action fails before any N-1 case is run (its N-1 energy, 285.4 MW, would be better). A remedial action is only a candidate when the base case stays within limits; the flow-based domain then tells the market how much room the action leaves.

    Exercise 1¶

    The TATL is a temporary admissible loading: the line may carry up to 1 250 A for 15 minutes after the contingency, provided the flow is brought back under the PATL (1 000 A) within that time. verdict() in curative_timing.py encodes the three conditions.

    • 1 180 A after the contingency, split active after 10 minutes, 940 A afterwards: the flow is under the TATL, the action arrives before the 15 minutes run out and leaves 940 A < 1 000 A. The curative action is sufficient; no preventive action is needed, and the market keeps the capacity.
    • Activation takes 20 minutes: the line would stay at 1 180 A for 20 minutes, 5 minutes beyond the TATL duration. A preventive action (or a faster curative one) is needed to bring the post-contingency flow under the PATL, or at least to a level the TATL duration covers.
    • Post-contingency flow 1 300 A: above the TATL, so the line is not allowed to carry it even for a second; the protection would trip it. Only a preventive action helps, since there is no time window in which to act.

    A boundary case: 1 250 A and exactly 15 minutes pass (the TATL is inclusive in CRAC conventions); a curative action that leaves 1 020 A does not pass, because the end state must respect the PATL.

    Exercise 2¶

    The GeoGebra construction (not run here): five lines \(a_k\,NP_A + b_k\,NP_B = \text{RAM}_k\), the inequality side of each shaded, the intersection filled, a slider on one RAM. domain_2d.py holds one such set, with the zonal PTDFs given relative to the balancing zone C (so \(a_k = \text{PTDF}_{A,k}\), \(b_k = \text{PTDF}_{B,k}\)):

    CNEC \(\text{PTDF}_A\) \(\text{PTDF}_B\) RAM (MW) \(s = \text{PTDF}_A - \text{PTDF}_B\) RAM / \(s\)
    1 0.5 0.1 200 0.4 500
    2 −0.3 0.4 180 −0.7 relieved
    3 −0.4 −0.5 250 0.1 2 500
    4 0.2 −0.6 220 0.8 275
    5 0.6 −0.4 150 1.0 150

    An A→B exchange of \(e\) MW is the point \(NP = (e, -e, 0)\); CNEC \(k\) sees \(s_k e\) with \(s_k = a_k - b_k\) and allows \(e \le \text{RAM}_k / s_k\) when \(s_k > 0\) (a CNEC with \(s_k < 0\) is relieved). The largest exchange is 150 MW, bound by CNEC 5. The domain itself is the pentagon with vertices (−612.9, −9.7), (−117.6, −405.9), (7.1, −364.3), (365.4, 173.1) and (269.6, 652.2); it contains the origin. Moving the slider: with RAM\(_5\) = 200 MW the exchange is 200 MW, still bound by CNEC 5; from RAM\(_5\) = 275 MW on, CNEC 4 takes over at 275 MW and raising RAM\(_5\) further (300, 400 MW) changes nothing. That is the general picture: enlarging the margin of the binding CNEC moves the boundary until the next CNEC binds, and a remedial action that only relieves one element has a bounded payoff.

    The reference flows are the same column three times: they come from the nodal injections, \(F_\text{ref} = H\,p\), and the GLSK plays no part in them. The zonal PTDFs, \(F_0 = F_\text{ref} - \text{PTDF}_z\,NP_\text{ref}\) and the margins all change, and so does the capacity the market sees: 317 MW of extra A→B exchange (branch 0–2 binding) with (0.6, 0.4), 299 MW with (1.0, 0.0), 396 MW with (0.0, 1.0). Note the change of sign of the zone-to-zone PTDF of branch 0–1 when zone A's export is assumed to come from bus 1: the same exchange then relieves the branch that the other GLSKs load. The GLSK is a forecast of where in the zone the generators will react to the market; if the real dispatch differs, the flow on each CNEC differs from the forecast by \((\text{PTDF}_{\text{true}} - \text{PTDF}_{\text{GLSK}})\,\Delta NP\). This forecast error, together with load, renewable and topology forecast errors between D-2 and real time, is what the flow reliability margin covers (CACM Art. 22); the FRM is estimated statistically from the observed differences between forecast and realized flows on each CNEC.

    Exercise 4¶

    ToOp artifact CRAC element What ToOp lacks
    nminus1_definition.json, contingencies (Contingency(id, elements=[GridElement]), plus BASECASE) Contingencies: a named list of network elements that trip together Nothing structural; BASECASE is the preventive state. The line_for_nminus1 mask (and trafo_*, generator_for_nminus1 etc.) is the list of elements turned into single contingencies
    monitored_elements and the line_for_reward mask The network elements of the CNECs A CNEC in a CRAC is one element in one state (after one contingency, at one instant); ToOp monitors every reward element after every contingency, the full product
    branch_limits.max_mw_flow (N-0) and max_mw_flow_n_1 Thresholds of the CNECs: PATL for the preventive instant, TATL for the outage instant Only MW thresholds (a CRAC also takes A and % of \(I_\max\)), only two instants: preventive (N-0 flows against max_mw_flow) and outage (N-1 flows against max_mw_flow_n_1). No auto and curative instants, no TATL durations
    action_set.json local_actions (busbar assignments) and disconnectable_branches Network actions: switch pairs (open/close) and terminal connections All are preventive; the ORAO summary writes them under preventive-actions-list
    the fact that every action may be applied Usage rules Only "available everywhere, preventive". No onContingencyState, onFlowConstraint, onInstant rules, so no action can be reserved for one contingency or triggered by one CNEC

    Three CRAC features with no representation in ToOp: (1) curative and automatic remedial actions, with their instants, TATL durations and the flow-based idea that a curative action restores the PATL after the TATL window (Exercise 1); (2) range actions, PST tap and HVDC set-point optimization as part of the remedial action search (ToOp's pst_ranges are only read by the DC solver as fixed data, and it has no redispatching or countertrading, hence no costs); (3) CNEC-specific usage rules and thresholds (per-instant, per-contingency limits in A or %, voltage and angle CNECs, loop-flow and RAM-based objectives). Smaller gaps: a CRAC names contingencies with several elements and busbar outages explicitly, while ToOp's multi-outages and busbar outages are preprocessing options.

    Exercise 5¶

    orao_switches_toop.py imports and preprocesses data/grid_node_breaker/grid.xiidm exactly as the Chapter 22 Lab does (cutoff voltage 10 kV; one relevant substation, VL2, survives preprocessing with ten actions, the unsplit one included). Two findings change the exercise as stated:

    • run_pipeline (the cell of example2_small_grid_toop.ipynb) fails on Windows outside the container: the DC optimizer names its TensorBoard folder after datetime.now(), and the colons of the time are not valid in a Windows path (WinError 123). So the DC optimization stage was not run here. Instead the module writes a res.json that lists every single action as a candidate and calls perform_ac_analysis, the same function the notebook runs after the DC stage, so that the orao_summary.json files come from ToOp's own writer.
    • No topology of this grid is accepted. ToOp's evaluate_acceptance requires at most as many non-converging load flows as the unsplit grid and at least 5 % less AC overload_energy_n_1. The unsplit grid has an AC overload energy of 16.34 MW with 3 non-converging cases; the best split (action 5, 25.38 MW) is worse, the unsplit action 0 is rejected for lack of improvement, and a scan of the 80 split-plus-one-disconnection and 8 disconnection-only candidates (run once, not kept as a test) finds every one rejected on convergence or overload energy. The exercise's "accepted topology" does not exist for this grid; perform_ac_analysis writes the ORAO summary of every assessed topology anyway, so the table below is that of the best split.

    The ORAO summary is a dictionary forced-actions → preventive-actions-list → actions, each action {"type": "SWITCH", "id": "Open VL2_BREAKER", "switchId": "VL2_BREAKER", "open": true}. Joined with net.get_switches() (switch_table()) and with the bays of the action set (bay_breakers(), flag_disconnectors()):

    Switch Operation Kind Voltage level Bay breaker Needs breaker
    VL2_BREAKER open BREAKER (coupler) VL2 – no
    L12_DISCONNECTOR_4_0 open DISCONNECTOR VL2 L12_BREAKER yes
    L12_DISCONNECTOR_4_2 close DISCONNECTOR VL2 L12_BREAKER yes

    Action 5 moves line L1's bay from busbar BBS2_1 to BBS2_3 and opens the coupler between them. The list contains the two disconnector operations of the bay but not its breaker: a disconnector cannot make or break load current (Chapter 20), so the switching sequence must open L12_BREAKER first, then open the busbar disconnector 4_0, close 4_2 and close the breaker again; the coupler VL2_BREAKER, a breaker, can be opened under load as the last step. The ORAO list is a target state, not a sequence: the ordering and the bay breakers are what the operator or OpenRAO's switch sequence has to add. (ToOp's logs call the result "Accepted Topology" while printing the rejection reason; the verdict is the return value of evaluate_acceptance, None when accepted.)

    Exercise 6¶

    time_coupling.py builds a 24 × 4 table of hourly overload energies (a daily load shape with topology 0 the unsplit grid, topology 1 best in the morning, 2 best in the evening, 3 mediocre everywhere; overload_table() prints it). (a) The single topology with the smallest total, the way ToOp scores one topology on all timesteps of a run, is topology 1 with 262.5 MW. (b) The dynamic program best_sequence(table, c) minimizes \(\sum_h E[h, t_h] + c \cdot \#\{h : t_h \ne t_{h-1}\}\) over sequences: for each hour and topology, the best cost of ending there is its own overload plus the cheaper of staying in the same topology or coming from the best previous one plus \(c\); a back-pointer array recovers the sequence (test_time_coupling.py checks it against brute force on a six-hour day).

    Switching cost \(c\) Sequence Overload Switches Total cost
    0 2 at hour 0, then 1 for hours 1–12, then 2 196.5 2 196.5
    5 1 for hours 0–12, 2 for hours 13–23 199.2 1 204.2
    10 same 199.2 1 209.2
    20 same 199.2 1 219.2
    63.5 topology 1 all day 262.5 0 262.5

    The one-switch plan costs \(199.2 + c\) and the fixed plan 262.5, so they coincide at \(c^\star = 63.3\) MW per change (63.5 on the 0.5 MW grid of break_even()): a switching cost of about a quarter of the day's overload energy makes the single topology optimal. Below it, a planner who may switch once a day saves 63 MW of overload energy. ToOp's choice of one topology per run is the \(c \to \infty\) end of this trade-off; it is the right choice when switching between hours is operationally expensive or unverified, and it is why a time-coupled RAO (OpenRAO's MARMOT) needs a switching cost or a limit on the number of changes to be meaningful.
